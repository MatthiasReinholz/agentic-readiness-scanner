## In short

Shoppers ask what delivery costs and whether they can send an item back before they buy. Structured data can answer both in a form an agent reads without parsing your policy pages. This check passes when every sampled product has shipping details and a return policy in its markup, either on the Offer (the price part of the product markup) or once for the whole store on your Organization node (the markup that describes your business), with the fields Google requires.

## What this check looks at

schema.org offers two places for these policies.

**On the product's Offer or Product**, for terms that differ per product:

- `shippingDetails` holding an `OfferShippingDetails` node.
- `hasMerchantReturnPolicy` holding a `MerchantReturnPolicy` node.

**On the Organization node**, for store-wide standard terms. The node may also use a more specific type, such as `OnlineStore`, `Store` or `LocalBusiness`:

- `hasShippingService` holding a `ShippingService` node.
- `hasMerchantReturnPolicy` holding a `MerchantReturnPolicy` node.

Google now documents the Organization level as the place for your standard shipping and return policy, with Offer-level details as an override for specific products. The scanner accepts either. It reads the Organization node from the product page and from the home page, since many stores output it only once, on the home page.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Total cost decides many purchases. An item at 20 euros with 9 euros shipping loses to one at 25 euros with free shipping. An agent comparing offers needs the shipping rate and delivery time to make that comparison honestly.

Return terms are a trust signal. "Free returns within 30 days" is a reason to choose one store over another, and an agent can only repeat it if it can read it. Google uses this markup to show shipping and return information with merchant listings.

This check has weight 2, expected, and gates no level. Your human-readable policy pages are checked separately by the [return policy page check](/knowledge-base/return-policy-page) and the [shipping policy page check](/knowledge-base/shipping-policy-page). This check is about the machine-readable version.

## How the scanner decides

For each sampled product with an Offer, the scanner looks for shipping details and a return policy on the Offer, the Product, the variants, or an Organization node. It then checks each policy for the fields Google requires.

| Node                   | Required by the scanner                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `MerchantReturnPolicy` | `merchantReturnLink`, or `applicableCountry` plus `returnPolicyCategory`, plus `merchantReturnDays` when the category is a finite return window |
| `OfferShippingDetails` | `shippingDestination` with `addressCountry`, `shippingRate` and `deliveryTime`                                                                  |
| `ShippingService`      | `shippingConditions`                                                                                                                            |

- **Pass:** every product has both shipping details and a return policy, and no policy lacks a required field.
- **Warn:** every product has both, but a policy lacks a required field. Also a warning when some products have both, or when products have only one of the two.
- **Fail:** no sampled product has shipping details or a return policy.
- **Skip:** no sampled page has Product JSON-LD, or no product has an Offer.

The report names each missing field, for example "MerchantReturnPolicy lacks merchantReturnDays", and says when the policies were found at Organization level.

## How to fix it

Decide first whether your terms are the same for every product. If they are, add them once to the Organization node. If some products differ, add Offer-level details to those.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WooCommerce stores shipping zones and rates under WooCommerce, Settings, Shipping. That is the source for `shippingRate` and `shippingDestination`.
- Some SEO plugins can output shipping and return markup from settings fields. Check whether yours does before writing code.
- Otherwise, a developer can extend the Product markup through the `woocommerce_structured_data_product` filter, or add an Organization block to the home page with `hasShippingService` and `hasMerchantReturnPolicy`.
- Keep the values in sync with your return policy page. If the page says 14 days, the markup must say 14.

### Shopify

- Shopify stores shipping rates under Settings, Shipping and delivery, and the return policy under Settings, Policies.
- Most themes do not output shipping or return markup. Add an Organization JSON-LD block in `theme.liquid` with your standard policies, or extend the product template's markup.
- If you sell to several countries, add one policy per country or region, or use `applicableCountry` with a list.

### Any other stack

- Generate the markup from the same configuration that calculates shipping at checkout.
- Use ISO 3166-1 two-letter country codes for `addressCountry` and `applicableCountry`.
- Express delivery time with `handlingTime` and `transitTime` in days.

A store-wide policy on the Organization node, the form Google now documents for standard terms, looks like this:

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "OnlineStore",
  "name": "Example Ceramics",
  "url": "https://example.com/",
  "hasMerchantReturnPolicy": {
    "@type": "MerchantReturnPolicy",
    "applicableCountry": ["DE", "AT"],
    "returnPolicyCategory": "https://schema.org/MerchantReturnFiniteReturnWindow",
    "merchantReturnDays": 30,
    "returnMethod": "https://schema.org/ReturnByMail",
    "returnFees": "https://schema.org/FreeReturn"
  },
  "hasShippingService": {
    "@type": "ShippingService",
    "name": "Standard shipping",
    "shippingConditions": {
      "@type": "ShippingConditions",
      "shippingDestination": { "@type": "DefinedRegion", "addressCountry": "DE" },
      "shippingRate": { "@type": "MonetaryAmount", "value": "4.90", "currency": "EUR" },
      "transitTime": {
        "@type": "ServicePeriod",
        "duration": {
          "@type": "QuantitativeValue",
          "minValue": 1,
          "maxValue": 3,
          "unitCode": "DAY"
        }
      }
    }
  }
}
```

## Verify it yourself

Run a product URL through Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results). Shipping and return issues appear under "Merchant listings". Run your home page through it as well if your policies live on the Organization node. The [Schema Markup Validator](https://validator.schema.org/) shows the nested nodes so you can check each required field.

A quick command-line check for the property names:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ https://example.com/product/stoneware-mug/ | grep -o -E 'hasMerchantReturnPolicy|shippingDetails|hasShippingService' | sort | uniq -c
```

## Common mistakes

- **A finite return window without the number of days.** `MerchantReturnFiniteReturnWindow` needs `merchantReturnDays`.
- **Shipping details without a destination country.** A rate with no `shippingDestination` does not say where it applies.
- **Markup that contradicts the policy page.** The markup promises free returns while the page charges a fee.
- **Organization markup on the home page with a different domain or name.** Keep one Organization description, consistent across pages.
- **Shipping markup for one country on a store that ships to many.** Agents in other countries get the wrong cost.

## FAQ

### Should return policy markup go on the Product or on the Organization?

Put your standard policy on the Organization node, which Google now documents for store-wide terms. Add Offer-level policies only for products whose terms differ, such as final-sale items.

### What does merchantReturnLink do?

It points to your return policy page. Google accepts a `MerchantReturnPolicy` with only that link as an alternative to spelling out country and category. Spelling out the terms gives agents more to work with.

### How do I mark up free shipping?

Set `shippingRate` to a `MonetaryAmount` with a `value` of `0` and your currency. If free shipping starts at an order value, add a second `ShippingConditions` entry with an `orderValue` range and a rate of `0`, as Google's shipping policy documentation shows.

### Can I use Merchant Center settings instead of markup?

Google lets merchants configure shipping and returns in Merchant Center or Search Console, and says those settings take precedence over markup for its own surfaces. They help Google only. This check measures what any agent can read on your own pages.
