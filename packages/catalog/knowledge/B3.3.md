## In short

Shoppers ask for products by brand, and agents match products by brand plus identifier. This check reads `brand` in the Product JSON-LD of sampled pages. It passes when every product names its brand and the spelling is consistent, and it prefers a Brand object with a `name` over a bare string.

## What this check looks at

The Product JSON-LD is the block of structured data that describes a product to machines. Its `brand` property says who makes or markets the product. schema.org lets you write it in two ways:

```json
"brand": { "@type": "Brand", "name": "Example Ceramics" }
```

or as a plain string, `"brand": "Example Ceramics"`. Google reads both, but its documentation recommends the object form with `brand.name`. An `Organization` object is also a valid value, because a brand can be the company itself.

The check also looks across the sample. "Example Ceramics" on one product and "EXAMPLE CERAMICS" on another read like two different brands to a machine.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Brand is one of the first filters in a shopping question: "Le Creuset casserole under 200 euros", "running shoes like my Asics". An agent that cannot tell which brand a product belongs to has to guess from the title, and titles are written for people, not parsers.

Brand is also half of the identifier for products without a GTIN (Global Trade Item Number). Google documents brand plus MPN (manufacturer part number) as the alternative to a GTIN. A missing or inconsistent brand weakens that fallback too.

A consistent name lets systems group all your products under one entity. That is what the "entity" in the title means: one brand, one name, one object, everywhere.

This check carries weight 2, which means it is expected of a well-run store. It is part of the Level 3 gate, "Identifiable products". Only a pass satisfies the gate. A warning does not.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reads each sampled product page that answers and has Product JSON-LD. It takes the first `brand` value it finds on the Product nodes.

- **Pass:** every considered product names a brand, as a Brand or Organization object with a `name`, or as a bare string, and no brand name appears with different casing. When some brands are bare strings, the message notes that Google recommends `brand.name` on a Brand object, but the check still passes.
- **Warn:** a brand object has no `name`, or its `@type` is missing or is something other than Brand or Organization. It also warns when the same brand appears with different capitalisation across the sample.
- **Fail:** at least one considered product has no brand at all.
- **Skip:** none of the sampled pages carries Product JSON-LD. That is the [Product JSON-LD check](/knowledge-base/product-json-ld)'s finding, so this check depends on it.

The comparison is case-insensitive, so only spelling differences in capitalisation are detected. Two completely different spellings, such as "Example Ceramics" and "ExampleCeramics", are counted as two brands and not flagged.

## How to fix it

Keep one canonical list of brand names and assign each product exactly one entry from it. Free-text brand fields are where inconsistencies start.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Recent WooCommerce versions include a built-in Brands taxonomy under Products, Brands. Create each brand once and assign one per product.
- Check that your structured data uses it. View a product's source and look for `"brand"`. If an SEO plugin generates the Product markup, set its brand option to read from the Brands taxonomy rather than a custom attribute.
- If brands currently live in a product attribute, migrate them to the taxonomy so the name is stored once.

### Shopify

- Shopify's Vendor field on each product is the usual brand source. Most themes map it into the product structured data as the brand.
- Pick one spelling per vendor and fix the others. The product list in the admin can be filtered by vendor, which makes duplicates easy to spot.
- If your theme writes the brand as a plain string or leaves it out, edit the product JSON-LD in the theme's product section to output a Brand object with `product.vendor` as its name.

### Any other stack

- Store brands in their own table or taxonomy and reference them from products by ID.
- Emit `brand` as `{"@type": "Brand", "name": "..."}` on every Product, and on each variant if your variants are separate Product nodes.
- Normalise casing on input so a supplier feed in capitals does not create a second brand.

## Verify it yourself

Extract the brand from a product page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/product/blue-mug/ | grep -oE '"brand"\s*:\s*(\{[^}]*\}|"[^"]*")'
```

Repeat it on a few products and compare the names. Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator at validator.schema.org both show the parsed brand. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **A Brand object without a name.** `{"@type": "Brand"}` alone tells a machine nothing. Some templates output it when the brand field is empty.
- **The store name as every product's brand.** If you resell other makers' products, the brand is the maker, not your shop.
- **Mixed capitalisation from imports.** One supplier sends "ASICS", another "Asics". Normalise to one.
- **Brand in the title only.** A brand in the product name helps people but is not structured data.
- **Wrong type.** `"@type": "Thing"` or a misspelled `"Brands"` makes the object incomplete.

## FAQ

### Should brand be a string or a Brand object?

Both are valid schema.org. Google recommends the object form with `brand.name`, so that is the safer choice. The scanner passes both and notes when strings are used.

### What brand do I use for my own private-label products?

Your own brand name, as you print it on the product. A brand that is also your company can be an Organization object.

### Can a product have more than one brand?

schema.org allows several values, but a product usually has one brand. The scanner reads the first one it finds, so put the real brand first.

### Does brand affect products that already have a GTIN?

Yes. Google's Merchant Center documentation names brand next to the GTIN among the identifiers that help it understand what you sell, and shoppers filter by brand. For products without a GTIN it becomes essential, because brand plus MPN is the fallback identifier.
