## In short

Square Online lets you hide the whole site, or single pages, from search engine results. A hidden site stays online for anyone with the link, but drops out of search indexes and the AI answers built on them. This check reports whether your Square Online home page carries `noindex`, a directive that asks search engines to keep the page out of their index, or whether robots.txt (the file that tells crawlers what they may fetch) shuts out both Googlebot and Bingbot. It runs only on Square Online sites.

## What this check looks at

Square documents two places where you can hide a site:

- For the whole site, the "Hidden from search engine results" option under Online, Website, SEO, in the Search Engine Optimization (SEO) section.
- For a single page, the Search visibility option in that page's SEO settings in the site editor.

Either change takes effect when you publish. Hiding does not unpublish anything. Anyone with the link can still open the site.

The check reads the public signals a search engine reads:

- the robots meta tag and the `X-Robots-Tag` header of the home page
- the robots.txt rules for Googlebot and Bingbot

## Why it matters for AI agents

Search engines leave out pages that ask not to be indexed, and do not crawl paths robots.txt disallows. Google and Bing indexes are what many AI answers draw on, such as Google's AI Overviews and Microsoft Copilot. A store that is missing from them is rarely named or linked when someone asks an assistant where to buy.

A hidden site can look fine to you, because it still opens normally in the browser. That makes the setting easy to leave on after a soft launch.

## How the scanner decides

This check runs only on Square Online sites, when the scan recognises the platform (see [platform detection](/knowledge-base/ecommerce-platform-detection)). On any other platform it is skipped and left out of the report. It makes no extra request.

- **Fail:** the home page's robots meta tag or `X-Robots-Tag` header contains `noindex` or `none`, or robots.txt disallows both Googlebot and Bingbot at the root path `/`. The message names which signal it found.
- **Pass:** neither signal is present.

An `X-Robots-Tag` header aimed at one named crawler, such as `bingbot: noindex`, is ignored. A robots.txt that blocks only one of the two search crawlers does not fail this check.

The check reads these two signals. It does not see the setting in your dashboard, and it looks at the home page only. A single hidden product page is not reported here.

The weight is 1, which means nice to have. It is not a gate for any level.

## How to fix it

Make the site visible again, then check the pages that matter most.

### Square Online

1. In the Square Dashboard, go to Online, Website, SEO.
2. Under Search Engine Optimization (SEO), clear "Hidden from search engine results" for the site.
3. Check single pages too. Open Online, Website, Edit site, select the page, open the gear icon, then View page settings, SEO. Make sure Search visibility is not set to hidden for the home page, the shop page and your main product pages.
4. Publish the site. The change takes effect only after publishing.

Square serves the sitemap at `/sitemap.xml`. Once the site is visible, you can submit it in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools to speed up recrawling.

## Verify it yourself

Check the home page for a noindex and robots.txt for blanket rules:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -i 'name=.robots'
curl -sI https://example.com/ | grep -i x-robots-tag
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt
```

A robots value with `noindex` or `none` means the page is still hidden. In robots.txt, look for `Disallow: /` in a group that applies to Googlebot and Bingbot, including the wildcard group `User-agent: *`. Rescan after publishing to confirm.

## Common mistakes

- **Changing the setting but not publishing.** Nothing changes for search engines until the site is published.
- **Unhiding the site but not the pages.** A page set to hidden in its own settings stays hidden.
- **Treating hidden as private.** Hiding only asks search engines to stay away. Anyone with the link can still open the site.
- **Expecting instant results.** Search engines need to recrawl the site before it reappears. Submitting the sitemap helps.

## FAQ

### Why is my Square Online site not showing up on Google?

One common cause is the "Hidden from search engine results" option under Online, Website, SEO, or a page set to hidden in its own SEO settings. Clear it, publish, and give search engines time to recrawl.

### Does hiding a Square Online site make it private?

No. Hiding does not unpublish the site. Anyone with the link can still view it. It only asks search engines to keep it out of their results.

### Does this check read my Square dashboard settings?

No. It reads what any crawler sees: the robots meta tag and header of the home page, and the robots.txt rules for Googlebot and Bingbot.

### Does a hidden site also disappear from AI answers?

Mostly, yes. Many AI answers draw on the Google and Bing indexes, so a site missing from them is rarely cited. An assistant may still open a page when a person pastes the link.
