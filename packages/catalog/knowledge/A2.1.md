## In short

This check asks whether your robots.txt says anything about AI training at all. Allowing training and blocking it pass equally. Only silence produces a warning, because an explicit decision is clearer for crawlers and for you than an accidental default.

## What this check looks at

Model makers collect training data with crawlers that are separate from the crawlers behind search and AI answers. Each has its own product token, the name you write in a robots.txt `User-agent` line. robots.txt is the plain text file at the root of a host, read under RFC 9309, the Robots Exclusion Protocol.

The scanner accepts a training decision in any of three forms:

- **The shared training token.** A group for `__usage_ai-training`. This token comes from an IETF (Internet Engineering Task Force) draft that lets one group speak to every training crawler that honours it. Microsoft honours it from 1 February 2027. Google intends to honour it from early 2027, and an explicit `Google-Extended` group takes precedence over it for Google.
- **Named training crawlers.** A group that names at least one of these tokens: GPTBot (OpenAI), ClaudeBot (Anthropic), Google-Extended (Google), Applebot-Extended (Apple), meta-externalagent (Meta), Amazonbot (Amazon), CCBot (Common Crawl), Bytespider (ByteDance) and MistralAI-Training (Mistral).
- **A usage line.** A `Content-Signal` line that sets `ai-train`, or a `Content-Usage` line that sets `train-ai`, anywhere in robots.txt.

The direction does not matter. `Allow: /` and `Disallow: /` under any of these count as a stated decision.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A store that never wrote a training rule has not made a choice. It has inherited whatever its wildcard group says, often without knowing it. That leaves two kinds of surprise. Some owners find out later that their catalog trained models they meant to keep it from. Others block "all AI bots" in a panic and lose the search crawlers along with the training crawlers.

Writing the decision down separates the two questions. Training is a licensing decision. Being found in AI answers depends on the search and assistant crawlers, which the [search and assistant crawler check](/knowledge/search-and-assistant-crawlers-allowed) covers. A store can block every training crawler and still be fully visible in ChatGPT search, Google AI Overviews or Perplexity.

The scanner takes no side on the decision itself. Blocking training never costs a point anywhere in this tool. The warning for silence is this project's position that the decision should be explicit. It is not a rule any vendor imposes.

## How the scanner decides

The check depends on robots.txt being found and parsed (see [robots.txt exists and parses](/knowledge/robots-txt-exists-and-parses)).

- **Pass:** robots.txt has a group for `__usage_ai-training`, or a group that names at least one of the nine training crawlers, or a `Content-Signal` or `Content-Usage` line that sets a training value.
- **Warn:** none of those is present, or the site has no robots.txt.

The evidence lists every training token with two facts: whether robots.txt names it in a group of its own, and whether it may read `/` under the rules that apply to it. It also records whether the shared token is present and whether it allows or disallows. Use that table to confirm the effective policy matches what you intended.

This check has weight 1, which means nice to have. It is not a gate for any level. Fix the blocking checks first.

## How to fix it

Decide first. Then write the decision in one place and keep it separate from the rules for search crawlers.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WordPress serves a virtual robots.txt unless a physical `robots.txt` file sits in the web root. Look for that file first, because it overrides everything else.
- SEO plugins such as Yoast SEO and Rank Math include a robots.txt editor. Add the training group there so it survives updates.
- If a security plugin offers to "block AI bots", check which tokens it adds. Keep the training tokens if that is your choice and remove any search or assistant crawlers it lumped in.

### Shopify

- Shopify builds robots.txt from the theme template `robots.txt.liquid`. Without that template you get Shopify's defaults.
- To add a group, create or open the template under Online Store, Themes, Edit code, and append a group for `__usage_ai-training` or for the named crawlers after the default rules.
- Apps that toggle AI crawler access often edit this template. Review the result after installing one.

### Any other stack

- Edit robots.txt where your framework, CMS or web server generates it.
- If a CDN manages robots.txt for you, check its settings too. Cloudflare's managed robots.txt, for example, can prepend AI crawler rules and Content-Signal lines to your own file.
- Write the shared token group and, if you want belt and braces, groups for the individual crawlers. Remember that a crawler with its own group ignores the wildcard group entirely, so give that group the paths it needs.

## Verify it yourself

List every line that mentions a training token or a training usage value:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt | grep -inE "__usage_ai-training|gptbot|claudebot|google-extended|applebot-extended|meta-externalagent|amazonbot|ccbot|bytespider|mistralai-training|ai-train|train-ai"
```

No output means no decision is stated. Then rescan and read the per-token table in the evidence.

## Common mistakes

- **Blocking training by blocking search.** A list of "AI bots" that includes OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot or PerplexityBot removes the store from AI answers. Training and search tokens are different.
- **Naming a crawler with no rules after it.** A group that names GPTBot must contain an `Allow` or `Disallow` line to say anything useful. An empty group leaves the crawler with no restriction.
- **Forgetting that named groups replace the wildcard.** If you add `User-agent: GPTBot` with `Allow: /`, GPTBot no longer sees the `Disallow: /checkout/` rule in your wildcard group.
- **Inventing tokens.** Tokens that only appear in third-party block lists are not documented by any operator, so this scanner does not count them.
- **Blocking Amzn-SearchBot along with Amazonbot.** Amazonbot is on the training list. Amzn-SearchBot feeds Alexa and Amazon search, and belongs with the search crawlers.

## FAQ

### Does blocking GPTBot or ClaudeBot hurt my score?

No. Blocking a training crawler is a licensing decision and never loses points in this scanner. This check only notes when robots.txt says nothing about training.

### What is __usage_ai-training?

It is a shared robots.txt token from an IETF draft. One group for it states your training preference to every crawler that honours the token. Microsoft honours it from 1 February 2027, and Google intends to from early 2027.

### If I block Google-Extended, do I disappear from Google AI Overviews?

Google-Extended is a training control, not a search crawler. Google's AI features answer from the index Googlebot builds, which this check does not touch. Keep Googlebot allowed if you want to appear in those answers.

### Is a Content-Signal line enough?

For this check, yes. A `Content-Signal` line that sets `ai-train` counts as a stated decision. Whether a given crawler reads it is a separate question, which the [usage-preference signals check](/knowledge/usage-preference-signals) explains.
