## In short

A privacy policy says what happens to a buyer's data. Terms of service say what the buyer agrees to. Agents that buy on someone's behalf, and the programmes that let them, expect both to be easy to find. This check looks for both links on your home page and passes when both pages answer.

## What this check looks at

The scanner reads the home page as the server delivers it and ranks every link that could be the privacy policy or the terms. A link qualifies when its label or the last part of its path names the topic as a whole word:

| Page           | Words matched                                                                                      |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Privacy policy | privacy, Datenschutz, Datenschutzerklärung, confidentialité, privacidad, riservatezza              |
| Terms          | terms, conditions, T&Cs, AGB, Geschäftsbedingungen, Nutzungsbedingungen, CGV, condizioni, términos |

The ranking is the same as for the other policy links. A label made only of policy words beats a label that carries other words, so "Terms of Service" outranks "Promo T&Cs". A footer link beats a body link. Product, category and account URLs are never chosen. A same-origin link wins over one on another site, but a clearly labelled link to another site, such as a parent company's privacy policy, is kept when nothing better exists.

## Why it matters for AI agents

An agent that completes a purchase is acting inside your terms and handing over a buyer's personal data. The buyer, the agent's operator and the payment provider all have reason to know what those terms are. A policy that cannot be found is a reason for caution.

Every checkout programme the scanner tracks asks merchants for links to their policies. Google Merchant Center's checkout requirements say customers must be able to find the terms and conditions throughout checkout. What a privacy policy or terms must contain depends on the jurisdiction and is outside what this check can judge.

This check carries weight 3, which means blocking. It is part of the Level 4 gate, "Trusted merchant". A store cannot reach Level 4 until this check passes.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner fetches each linked page once.

- **Pass:** both the privacy policy and the terms page answer HTTP 200.
- **Warn:** only one of them answers HTTP 200, either because the other is not linked or because its link did not answer.
- **Fail:** neither answers HTTP 200.

When a page was linked but could not be read, the message names the link and the response, for example a 404 or a challenge page. This check does not count words or read the content. The [agent-readable policy pages check](/knowledge/agent-readable-policy-pages) looks at whether the text is substantial and open to the crawlers behind AI answers.

Links are read from the server-rendered HTML. A footer that only appears after JavaScript runs is invisible here. On Shopify, the platform's own pages at `/policies/privacy-policy` and `/policies/terms-of-service` are used when no link was found or the best link matched only by its path.

## How to fix it

Publish both pages, register them where your platform expects them, and link both from the footer of every page with plain labels such as "Privacy policy" and "Terms of service".

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WordPress has a privacy policy setting under Settings, Privacy. Select or create the page there. WordPress links it from the login screen and offers a policy guide to help you write it.
- Select the terms page under WooCommerce, Settings, Advanced, Page setup. Checkout then links it where buyers accept the terms.
- Add both to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus, or to the footer template part of a block theme.

### Shopify

- Write both under Settings, Policies. Shopify publishes them at `/policies/privacy-policy` and `/policies/terms-of-service`.
- Link both in the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation.
- Shopify marks policy pages as noindex by default. That does not affect this check, but it is reported by the agent-readable policy pages check.

### Any other stack

- Publish the pages at stable URLs such as `/privacy` and `/terms`.
- Link them from the `footer` element of the server-rendered HTML on every page.
- Make sure both answer HTTP 200 to a plain request without cookies. Consent walls, geo-blocks and bot challenges can stop an agent before it reads them.

## Verify it yourself

Find both links on the home page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oiE 'href="[^"]*"[^>]*>[^<]*(privacy|datenschutz|terms|conditions|agb)[^<]*' | head
```

Then check that each page answers 200:

```bash
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" https://example.com/privacy-policy/
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}\n" https://example.com/terms/
```

Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **Only one of the two.** Many small stores publish a privacy policy but no terms, or the reverse. The check warns.
- **A link to a third-party policy generator that fails.** Hosted policies can break when a subscription ends. The message names the failing link.
- **Promotion terms ranked as the store's terms.** A footer link "Giveaway T&Cs" can be picked if no better terms link exists. Add a plain "Terms of service" link.
- **Pages behind a cookie or age wall.** A page that answers with a redirect or challenge does not count.
- **Links only in the checkout.** Agents and shoppers look for terms before they reach checkout.

## FAQ

### Does an online store need terms and conditions?

Whether they are legally required depends on where you sell. For this check and for the checkout programmes it reflects, a terms page linked from the home page is expected.

### Where should the privacy policy link go?

In the footer of every page, with a plain label. WordPress also links the page you select under Settings, Privacy from places it controls.

### Can the privacy policy live on another domain?

Yes. If your company publishes one policy for several sites, a labelled link to it counts. The scanner prefers a same-origin page when both exist.

### Does the scanner check the content of my policies?

No. It checks that both pages are linked and answer. It does not judge whether they are complete or compliant, which depends on the jurisdiction.
