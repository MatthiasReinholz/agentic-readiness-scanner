## In short

In the EU, a store that announces a price reduction must state the prior price, defined as the lowest price it charged in at least the 30 days before the reduction. Since the Court of Justice's Aldi Süd ruling of 26 September 2024, a percentage or "save" claim must also be based on that lowest price. This check runs on product pages that show a struck-through price and looks for 30-day lowest-price wording nearby. It is a technical signal, never scored, and not legal advice: it cannot check whether the stated price history is true.

## What this check looks at

The scanner reads sampled product pages. When a page shows a reduced price, meaning a `del` or `s` element next to the price or two prices in the offer, it looks near that price for wording such as "lowest price in the last 30 days", "niedrigster Preis der letzten 30 Tage", "prix le plus bas des 30 derniers jours" or "precio más bajo de los últimos 30 días".

What the law says, by market:

- **European Union:** Article 6a of the Price Indication Directive, Directive 98/6/EC as amended by the Omnibus Directive (EU) 2019/2161, requires any announcement of a price reduction to indicate the prior price. The prior price is the lowest price the trader applied during a period of not less than 30 days before the reduction. Member states may set different rules for goods that perish quickly, for products on sale for less than 30 days and for progressive reductions. In Germany the rule is section 11 of the Price Indication Ordinance (PAngV).
- **The Aldi Süd ruling:** in case C-330/23, decided on 26 September 2024, the Court of Justice of the EU held that a reduction announced as a percentage or a promotional claim must be calculated from that prior lowest price, not from a higher price charged earlier.
- **United Kingdom:** there is no fixed 30-day rule. The Price Marking Order 2004, as amended from 6 April 2026, governs how selling and unit prices are shown. Whether a "was" price misleads is judged under the unfair commercial practices rules of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 and the Competition and Markets Authority's guidance.
- **Switzerland:** the Price Indication Ordinance (PBV) limits self-comparison with an earlier price. Since 1 January 2025 a store may compare with a price it actually charged for at least 30 consecutive days, or follow the older short-term rule. It does not prescribe the 30-day wording this check looks for. The [Swiss price display check](/knowledge-base/swiss-price-display) covers Swiss price display.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Clear prior prices let agents compare deals honestly. A shopping agent that reads a product page sees a current price and a struck-through price, and often reports the difference to its user as a saving. If the struck-through price is a real 30-day low, the saving the agent repeats is real. If it is an inflated anchor, the agent passes on a misleading claim, and its user may decide on that basis.

Stating the lowest 30-day price in plain text, next to the price, gives the agent the number the law defines. Keeping the struck-through price in the Offer markup equal to the visible one helps too. Google's [merchant listing documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/merchant-listing) describes a strikethrough price as a second price with `priceType` set to `https://schema.org/StrikethroughPrice`; the current price then counts as the sale price. The readiness checks cover [Offer markup](/knowledge-base/offer-price-currency-availability) and whether [markup matches the visible page](/knowledge-base/markup-matches-visible-page).

## How the scanner decides

The check runs only when product pages were sampled and the store appears to sell to the EU; the UK and Swiss rules above do not prescribe this wording, so stores selling only there are not checked. It looks only at pages that show a struck-through price. When none of the sampled pages shows one, it is reported as not applicable.

- **Found:** wording about the lowest price in the last 30 days appears near the reduced price.
- **Review:** a reduced price is shown without that wording.

What it cannot see:

- Whether the stated lowest price is true. The scanner sees one moment, not the price history.
- Whether the percentage or "save" amount is calculated from the 30-day lowest price.
- Whether an exception applies, such as perishable goods or a progressive reduction.
- Whether a struck-through price is a manufacturer's recommended retail price, which is a comparison rather than a reduction, and must be labelled as such.
- Prices shown only after JavaScript runs, in the cart or at checkout.

## How to fix it

Record price history, show the lowest price of the previous 30 days next to every reduction, and base percentage badges on it.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WooCommerce shows a sale price against the regular price, but it does not show a prior lowest price out of the box. Legal plugins for EU markets add price history and a 30-day lowest price line.
- If you build it yourself, store each price change with a date in post meta or a custom table, and print the line in the single product price template next to the sale price.
- Check that the theme's "sale" badge and any percentage use the 30-day lowest price as the basis.

### Shopify

- Shopify shows a compare-at price struck through next to the price. Make sure the compare-at price you enter is the lowest price of the previous 30 days, not an older, higher price.
- To show the line, store the 30-day lowest price in a product or variant metafield, kept current by an app or a script, and render it in the price block of the product template.
- Check percentage badges in the theme, which are usually computed from the compare-at price.

### Any other stack

- Keep a price history table per product and variant with timestamps.
- Compute the lowest price of the 30 days before the reduction started, and show it in text next to the reduced price, in the server-rendered HTML.
- Use that price as the basis for percentage and "save" claims, and as the strikethrough price in Offer markup.

## Verify it yourself

On a product with a reduced price, check that the struck-through price and the 30-day wording appear together:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/product/stoneware-mug/ | grep -oiE "<(del|s)[ >][^<]*" | head -3
curl -s https://example.com/product/stoneware-mug/ | grep -oiE "(lowest price|niedrigster preis|prix le plus bas|precio más bajo)[^<]{0,60}" | head -3
```

Then compare the stated lowest price with your own price history for the 30 days before the sale started.

## Common mistakes

- **A percentage based on an old, higher price.** After the Aldi Süd ruling, the basis must be the 30-day lowest price.
- **The 30-day line only on the category page.** Show it wherever the reduction is announced, including the product page.
- **Raising the price shortly before a sale.** The 30-day lowest price then stays at the low level, and the "discount" disappears.
- **Labelling a recommended retail price as "was".** A comparison with the manufacturer's price is not your prior price; say what it is.
- **Different numbers in markup and on the page.** Keep the strikethrough price in JSON-LD equal to the visible one.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

In the EU, Article 6a applies to traders announcing price reductions to consumers, whatever their size, with national exceptions such as for perishable goods. The UK and Switzerland have their own rules on reference prices, described above, without the same 30-day wording. Whether and how they apply to your offers is a question for a lawyer.

### Does every price shown with a strikethrough need the 30-day line?

In the EU, every announced price reduction does. A comparison with a recommended retail price is not a reduction of your own price, but it must be clearly labelled. The check cannot tell the two apart and asks for review.

### How does the Aldi Süd ruling change percentage discounts?

The Court of Justice held on 26 September 2024 that a percentage or promotional claim must be calculated from the prior lowest price. "25% off" must be 25% off the 30-day lowest price.

### Can the scanner check whether my price history is true?

No. It sees each page once. It checks only that the wording is there, never that the number is correct.

### What if a product has been on sale for less than 30 days?

Several EU countries allow a shorter period for products new to the market, and progressive reductions have their own rule. These are national choices; check the rule for each country you sell to.
