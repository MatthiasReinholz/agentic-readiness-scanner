## In short

When a chat assistant on your store answers with AI, people should know they are talking to a machine. EU and US state laws ask for that disclosure, and in the EU the duty often rests with the vendor that provides the AI system. This check detects known chat and AI assistant widgets on your home page and names them for review. The disclosure itself appears inside the widget after JavaScript runs, so the scanner cannot check it.

## What this check looks at

The EU and three US states have rules about disclosing AI conversations.

- **European Union.** Article 50(1) of the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, has applied since 2 August 2026. The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, kept that date. It requires providers to design AI systems that interact directly with people so that those people are informed they are interacting with an AI system, unless that is obvious. It is a provider duty: it binds whoever develops the system and places it on the market under its own name. For a chat widget bought as a service, that is often the vendor. A store that builds its own assistant, or offers one under its own name, may be the provider itself. Article 50(5) asks for the information to be clear and given at the latest at the first interaction.
- **California.** The BOT Act, Business and Professions Code §17941, makes it unlawful to use a bot to communicate with a person in California online with the intent to mislead them about its artificial identity in order to incentivise a purchase, unless the bot discloses clearly and conspicuously that it is a bot.
- **Utah.** The Artificial Intelligence Policy Act, as amended by SB 226 in 2025, requires a business using generative AI with a consumer to disclose it when the person clearly and unambiguously asks whether they are talking to a human. Stricter rules apply to regulated occupations in high-risk interactions. The act is set to be repealed on 1 July 2027 unless extended.
- **Maine.** 10 M.R.S. §1500-DD, enacted in 2025, makes it an unfair trade practice to use an AI chatbot in trade and commerce in a way that may mislead a reasonable consumer into believing they are engaging with a human, unless the consumer is told clearly and conspicuously that they are not.

The report lists each provision with a link to its official text.

## Why it matters for AI agents

The assistant on your store no longer talks only to people. A shopping agent acting for a customer may open the same chat to ask about stock, sizes or delivery times, and pass the answers on. Both the person and the agent need to know who answered. A model's answer can be wrong. A person on your team can commit the store, for example to a refund. An agent that cannot tell the two apart may present a model's guess to its user as the store's promise.

A clear disclosure at the start of the conversation, a bot name that does not pose as a person, and a visible handover to a human make that distinction explicit for people and machines alike. Agents also prefer not to chat at all when the facts are published: return, shipping and contact details on readable pages, as the [agent-readable policy pages check](/knowledge-base/agent-readable-policy-pages) describes, answer most questions without a conversation.

## How the scanner decides

The check runs when the store appears to sell to the EU or the US. The scanner looks for the loader scripts of known chat and AI assistant vendors in the static HTML of the home page, including script URLs written inside inline scripts. The list includes Intercom, Zendesk, Gorgias, Tidio, Drift, LivePerson, Ada, Crisp, HubSpot chat, Freshchat, Shopify Inbox, Re:amaze, Chatbase, Voiceflow, Botpress, Kustomer and Salesforce Embedded Service.

- **Review:** a known chat or AI assistant is loaded. The evidence names the vendor so you can check its disclosure.
- **Found:** no known chat or AI assistant was detected. In this check, Found means "none detected".

What the scanner cannot see:

- Whether the widget answers with AI, with people, or with both. Many of these vendors offer live chat staffed by people as well as AI agents.
- The disclosure itself, which appears inside the widget after JavaScript runs.
- Assistants from vendors not on the list, and widgets injected later by a tag manager.

The result is reported, never scored, and it is not legal advice.

## How to fix it

First find out whether your chat uses AI. Vendors name their AI features differently, for example Intercom's Fin or Tidio's Lyro. If any reply comes from AI, set up the disclosure in the vendor's own settings:

1. Make the first message of every conversation say that the visitor is talking to an AI assistant.
2. Give the bot a name and avatar that do not suggest a person.
3. Offer a way to reach a human, and announce the handover when it happens.
4. Keep the disclosure in every language the store uses.

Then read the vendor's documentation on the AI Act and on US bot-disclosure laws, which says what the vendor provides and what it leaves to you.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Chat plugins usually connect to a vendor account. Change the bot's name, welcome message and handover rules in the vendor's dashboard rather than in the plugin's script.
- If the site runs its own assistant, for example a plugin that connects to a model provider's API, the store may be the provider under the AI Act. Add the disclosure to the assistant's first message and its interface.

### Shopify

- Chat apps, Shopify Inbox included, are configured in the app's own settings in the Shopify admin. Look for the greeting, the bot name and any AI or automated reply settings there.
- Check every chat app installed, since a theme can load more than one widget.

### Any other stack

- Configure the disclosure in the vendor's dashboard or, for an in-house assistant, in the first message the assistant sends and in a persistent label in the chat window.
- Keep transcripts that show which replies came from AI and which from people, if your vendor supports it.

## Verify it yourself

See which chat vendors your home page loads:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -ioE "widget\.intercom\.io|static\.zdassets\.com|gorgias\.chat|code\.tidio\.co|js\.driftt\.com|static\.ada\.support|client\.crisp\.chat|js\.usemessages\.com|chatbase\.co|cdn\.voiceflow\.com|cdn\.botpress\.cloud" | sort -u
```

Then open the store in a private browser window, start a chat and read the first message. It should say plainly that you are talking to an AI.

## Common mistakes

- **A human persona for a bot.** A first name and a stock photo make the disclosure harder to notice and work against it.
- **Disclosure only in the privacy notice.** The point is that people know during the conversation, not after reading a policy.
- **Disclosure in one language.** A translated store with an English-only greeting.
- **Assuming the vendor handles everything.** Vendors provide settings. Whether they are switched on is up to you.
- **No route to a person.** People and agents who need a binding answer have nowhere to go.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

The EU duty in Article 50(1) binds the provider of the AI system, which for a bought-in chat service is often the vendor, and it applies to systems used in the EU. The California, Utah and Maine laws concern businesses that use bots or generative AI with consumers in those states, each with its own conditions. Whether your store, your vendor or both carry a duty is a question for a qualified lawyer.

### My chat is staffed by people. Why does the scan say Review?

The scanner sees the vendor's script, not who answers. Many chat vendors offer both live chat and AI agents. If only people answer, the AI disclosure rules described here do not concern that chat. Note it in your own records so the next review is quick.

### Is it enough to call the bot "Assistant"?

A neutral name helps, but the laws above turn on whether people are clearly told they are talking to a machine. An explicit sentence at the start of the conversation is clearer than a name alone.

### Do AI agents use my store's chat?

Some do. A shopping agent acting for a person can type into a chat widget like a visitor. A clear disclosure and a published route to a human help it report accurately who said what.
