## In short

Since 13 December 2024, the EU General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR) requires online offers of consumer products to show who made the product, with a postal and an electronic address, and an EU responsible person when the manufacturer is outside the EU. This check reads sampled product pages for a named manufacturer with contact details, in the visible text or in Product markup. It is a technical signal, never scored, and not legal advice: it cannot tell whether the details are correct or which safety warnings a product needs.

## What this check looks at

Article 19 of the GPSR, Regulation (EU) 2023/988, applies to products offered to consumers in the EU through distance sales, including online stores. It lists what the offer must show, clearly and visibly:

- **The manufacturer:** name, registered trade name or trade mark, and the postal and electronic address where they can be contacted.
- **The EU responsible person:** when the manufacturer is not established in the EU, the name, postal and electronic address of the economic operator in the EU that the GPSR makes responsible for the product.
- **Product identification:** information that identifies the product, including a picture, its type and any other product identifier.
- **Warnings and safety information:** any warning or safety information to be affixed to the product or included in its manual, in a language consumers can easily understand.

As a regulation, it applies directly and in the same words in every EU member state, so the duty is the same for a shop selling to Germany, France or Spain. National market surveillance authorities enforce it.

The scanner reads two sources on each sampled product page:

- **Product markup:** the schema.org `manufacturer` and `brand` properties in Product JSON-LD.
- **Visible text near manufacturer wording:** "Hersteller", "manufacturer", "fabricant", "fabricante", "produttore", "responsible person", "verantwortliche Person", "personne responsable".

The report lists the law with a link to its official text.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Shopping agents match products by manufacturer and identifier data. When an assistant compares offers for "the same" product across stores, it needs to know that two listings are the same item. Brand, manufacturer, GTIN (Global Trade Item Number) and MPN (manufacturer part number) are how it tells. Google's Merchant Center documentation on [unique product identifiers](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/160161) says they help match search queries with products, and that products with a GTIN submitted without one may have limited visibility.

The GPSR details add something a brand name alone does not: a real company with an address. An agent asked "who makes this and how do I reach them about a safety issue?" can answer from the page. An agent filtering out unidentified or dubious products can see that the product has an accountable manufacturer or EU responsible person.

The readiness checks cover the identifiers themselves: [GTIN present](/knowledge-base/gtin-present), [MPN when there is no GTIN](/knowledge-base/mpn-when-gtin-absent) and [brand as an entity](/knowledge-base/brand-as-entity).

## How the scanner decides

The check runs only when product pages were sampled and the store appears to sell to the EU. Otherwise it is reported as not applicable.

- **Found:** every sampled product page names a manufacturer together with a postal address or an email address, in markup or in the visible text.
- **Review:** a manufacturer or brand is named without contact details, or the details appear on only some of the pages.
- **Not found in the pages read:** no sampled page names a manufacturer.

What it cannot see:

- Whether the details are true, current or belong to the right company.
- Whether the manufacturer is inside or outside the EU, and so whether an EU responsible person is required.
- Whether warnings and safety information are needed. That depends on the product and is not checked.
- Details shown only in a tab or pop-up loaded by JavaScript, or only in a downloadable PDF.

## How to fix it

Store the manufacturer and responsible person as product data, render them on the product page in text, and add the manufacturer to the Product markup. Have the safety information reviewed per product type.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WooCommerce has no dedicated GPSR fields in core. Use product attributes, custom fields, or a legal plugin for EU markets that adds manufacturer and responsible person fields to products.
- Show them in a visible block on the single product template, for example in the description or an additional information section that is part of the server HTML.
- Add `manufacturer` to the core Product JSON-LD through the `woocommerce_structured_data_product` filter, or through your SEO plugin if it outputs the Product markup. Keep one Product block per page.

### Shopify

- Create product metafields for the manufacturer and the EU responsible person under Settings, Custom data, Products. For many products from the same maker, a metaobject for each manufacturer referenced from the product keeps the details in one place.
- Show them on the product page with a text block connected to the metafields in the theme editor, so they appear in the HTML.
- Extend the theme's Product JSON-LD with a `manufacturer` object from the same metafields. Shopify's vendor field usually feeds `brand`, not `manufacturer`.

### Any other stack

- Model the manufacturer and the EU responsible person as entities with name, postal address and email or contact URL, and link products to them.
- Render the details as text on the product page, in the initial HTML, near the description.
- Add `manufacturer` as an Organization with `address` and `email` or `url` to the Product JSON-LD.

## Verify it yourself

Check a product page for manufacturer wording and markup:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/product/stoneware-mug/ | grep -oiE "(hersteller|manufacturer|fabricant|fabricante|produttore|responsible person|verantwortliche person)[^<]{0,80}" | head -5
curl -s https://example.com/product/stoneware-mug/ | grep -o '"manufacturer"' | head -1
```

The [Schema Markup Validator](https://validator.schema.org/) shows the `manufacturer` node and its address.

## Common mistakes

- **A brand name without an address.** The GPSR asks for a postal and an electronic address.
- **Your own address instead of the manufacturer's.** A retailer that is not the manufacturer still has to show the manufacturer, unless it puts products on the market under its own name and so is treated as the manufacturer.
- **No EU responsible person for imported goods.** Products from manufacturers outside the EU need one.
- **Details only in a PDF or an image.** Put them in the page text so people and agents can read them.
- **A second Product block just for the manufacturer.** Extend the existing markup instead.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

The GPSR applies to consumer products placed on the EU market, including products sold online to consumers in the EU, whether the seller is in the EU or not. Some product categories have their own EU rules that take precedence in parts. Whether and how it applies to your products is a question for a lawyer.

### Is brand markup enough for the manufacturer?

No. A brand is a name; Article 19 asks for the manufacturer's name and its postal and electronic address. The check accepts a brand only as a sign for review, not as found.

### What is an EU responsible person?

An economic operator established in the EU, such as an EU-based manufacturer, importer, authorised representative or fulfilment service provider, who is responsible for the product's compliance. It is required when the manufacturer is not established in the EU.

### Do I have to show warnings on every product page?

Only where the product needs warnings or safety information on its label or in its manual. That depends on the product and is not checked.

### Does this also help with Google Shopping?

Brand, GTIN and MPN help Google match products. The `manufacturer` property is not a Google merchant listing field, but it is valid schema.org and readable by any agent.
