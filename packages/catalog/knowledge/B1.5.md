## In short

A product feed is one file that lists every product with its ID, title, link and price. Shopping channels ingest feeds you push to them, so a public copy is optional. A public feed linked from your home page or llms.txt lets any agent read the whole catalog in one request instead of crawling page by page. This check looks for such a feed and confirms that it really is a product feed.

## What this check looks at

A product feed is a structured export of the catalog. Two formats matter here:

- **Google Merchant Center format.** Usually an RSS 2.0 XML file that declares the `g:` namespace (`http://base.google.com/ns/1.0`) and carries fields such as `g:id`, `g:price` and `g:availability`. Google also accepts the same fields as tab-separated or comma-separated text.
- **OpenAI product feed format.** A CSV, TSV, JSON or JSON Lines file with columns such as `item_id`, `title`, `url`, `price` and `image_url`.

The scanner searches the places an agent would look, in this order:

1. Link tags with `rel=alternate` on the home page whose type is RSS, Atom, XML or JSON.
2. Links in llms.txt whose text or URL suggests a feed.
3. robots.txt `Sitemap` lines and comment lines that contain a feed URL.
4. Feed paths and data files listed in the sitemap index.

## Why it matters for AI agents

An agent that wants to compare your catalog has two options. It can crawl hundreds of product pages and parse each one, or it can read one feed. The feed is faster, cheaper and less error-prone for the agent, and it puts far less load on your server.

Be clear about what a public feed does not do. Google Merchant Center, ChatGPT shopping and other channels ingest feeds through their own upload or fetch settings. None of them documents reading a feed it happens to find on your site. A public feed is a convenience for independent agents and tools, not a requirement of any channel.

That is why this check has weight 1, nice to have. It is still one of the alternatives for the Level 2 "Readable catalog" gate. A store reaches that gate with a product sitemap, a platform API, Shopify's products.json or UCP catalog, or a public feed. Any one of them suffices.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner collects feed candidates from the four sources above and fetches at most the first two.

- A link tag is a candidate when its URL or title mentions product, feed, merchant, Google or shopping. If no link qualifies and the page has exactly one RSS or Atom link, that link is tried.
- A sitemap entry is a candidate only when it looks like a feed file or feed path. A product page whose slug contains the word "feed" is never a candidate.

A fetched candidate passes when it answers HTTP 200 and is one of these:

- **Google XML:** an XML feed that declares the Google namespace, or whose items carry `g:id` or `g:price`.
- **Google tabular:** a CSV or TSV whose header has `id`, `title`, `link` (or `url`) and `price`.
- **JSON:** items that carry the same four fields.
- **OpenAI format:** a CSV, TSV, JSON or JSON Lines body with `item_id` (or `sku`), `title`, `url` (or `link`) and `price`. The report records it as an OpenAI-format feed.

The check fails when no candidate is found, or when neither of the first two candidates is a product feed. The evidence names every candidate and why it was rejected, for example "XML without the Google Merchant namespace". A feed found by this check is also what the [feed freshness check](/knowledge-base/product-feed-freshness) inspects.

## How to fix it

Start from the feed you already send to a shopping channel. Most stores have one. Make a copy reachable at a stable public URL, then announce it.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WooCommerce does not publish a product feed by itself. Feed plugins for Google Merchant Center generate an XML or CSV file on a schedule and give it a public URL.
- Use a URL that says what it is, such as `/feed/products.xml`. The scanner recognises feed paths and file names.
- Add the link tag to the home page head through your theme's header hook or an SEO plugin's custom head field. Then add the URL to the Catalog section of llms.txt.
- WordPress already announces the blog RSS feed at `/feed/`. That feed holds posts, not products. Give your product feed link a title such as "Product feed" so it is tried first.

### Shopify

- Shopify sends product data to Google and other channels through its sales channel apps, which do not publish a public feed file.
- A feed app can publish a public Google-format XML file. Link it from the theme's `theme.liquid` head and from llms.txt.
- Shopify stores already expose `/products.json` and the UCP catalog, which also satisfy the Level 2 gate. See the [products.json check](/knowledge-base/shopify-products-json) and the [agentic storefront check](/knowledge-base/shopify-agentic-storefront).

### Any other stack

- Generate the feed from the same database that renders product pages, at least once a day.
- Serve XML with `Content-Type: application/xml` or `application/rss+xml`, CSV with `text/csv`, and JSON with `application/json`.
- Announce the URL in a home page link tag, in llms.txt, or in robots.txt as a comment such as `# Product feed: https://example.com/feed/products.xml`.

## Verify it yourself

Check that the feed answers and carries the Google namespace or the expected columns:

```bash
curl -sI https://example.com/feed/products.xml
curl -s https://example.com/feed/products.xml | head -c 1500
```

For an XML feed, look for `xmlns:g="http://base.google.com/ns/1.0"` near the top. For a CSV, check that the first line names `id`, `title`, `link` and `price`. Then confirm the home page announces it:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -i 'rel="alternate"'
```

## Common mistakes

- **Announcing only the blog feed.** An RSS feed of posts is not a product feed and fails the format test.
- **Hiding the feed behind a token.** A feed URL with a secret key or a login works for the channel it was made for, but agents without the key cannot read it.
- **Renaming columns.** A CSV with `product_name` and `cost` instead of `title` and `price` is not recognised as a product feed.
- **Listing the feed only in a place no one reads.** A feed that exists but is linked from nowhere cannot be discovered.
- **Letting the file go stale.** A public feed with last month's prices misleads agents. Keep it on the same schedule as your channel feeds.

## FAQ

### Does Google Merchant Center read a feed linked from my home page?

Not by discovery. Merchant Center reads feeds you register in its own settings, either as an upload or a scheduled fetch of a URL you enter. A link tag on your home page does not register anything.

### Is a public product feed a security risk?

It exposes only what your product pages already show: titles, prices, links and images. Leave out cost prices, supplier data and internal stock counts. Never put an API key in the feed URL.

### Which format should I choose, Google or OpenAI?

Choose the one you already generate for a channel. Both are recognised. Google's XML format is the most widely produced by feed plugins, so it is the simplest place to start.

### My store fails this check. Is that a problem?

Usually not. The check has weight 1. If your products are in a sitemap or a platform API, your catalog is already enumerable and the Level 2 gate is met another way.
