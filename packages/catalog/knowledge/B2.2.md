## In short

An Offer is the part of product markup (the schema.org structured data on a product page) that says what the product costs and whether it can be bought now. Without price, currency and availability, an agent knows what you sell but not whether a shopper can buy it. This check passes when every Offer on your sampled product pages carries all three. It is a blocking check and a Level 2 gate.

## What this check looks at

In schema.org, the shared vocabulary for structured data, a `Product` describes the thing and an `Offer` describes the terms of sale. The offer sits under the product's `offers` property. The scanner requires three fields on each offer:

| Field           | What it holds                                 | Example                        |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| `price`         | The amount, without a currency sign           | `"24.00"`                      |
| `priceCurrency` | The currency as an ISO 4217 three-letter code | `"EUR"`                        |
| `availability`  | A schema.org ItemAvailability URL             | `"https://schema.org/InStock"` |

An `AggregateOffer` may use `lowPrice` instead of `price`. On a `ProductGroup`, the offers usually live on each variant under `hasVariant`. The scanner counts those as the product's offers. Shopify's native markup works this way.

Accepted availability values are the schema.org ItemAvailability members: InStock, OutOfStock, PreOrder, BackOrder, Discontinued, InStoreOnly, LimitedAvailability, OnlineOnly, PreSale and SoldOut. Both `http://` and `https://` forms are accepted.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A shopping question almost always includes price and stock. "Find me a waterproof jacket under 150 euros that ships this week" cannot be answered from a product name alone. The Offer is where structured data answers it.

Merchant listings are Google's shopping results that show a product with its price, stock and delivery. Google's documentation for them requires an Offer with a price. It also states two rules this check enforces:

- The price must be greater than zero for merchant listings.
- If `priceValidUntil` names a date in the past, Google says your listing may not display.

An `AggregateOffer`, which gives a price range, is eligible for product snippets (the price and rating line under a search result) but not for merchant listings. Those need an Offer.

This check carries weight 3, blocking. With the [Product JSON-LD check](/knowledge-base/product-json-ld), it gates Level 2 "Readable catalog" and every level above it.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reads every Product and ProductGroup node on the sampled product pages and collects their offers, including variant offers.

- **Pass:** every product's offers carry a price, a currency and a valid availability URL.
- **Warn:** some offers are complete and others are not, or some products have no offer while others have a complete one. A store where all offers are complete is still demoted to a warning when an offer has a price of 0 or below, or when `priceValidUntil` is a date that had already passed when the page was served. A bare date such as `2026-12-31` counts as valid until the end of that day.
- **Fail:** Product markup exists but has no Offer at all, or no offer is complete.
- **Skip:** no sampled page carries Product JSON-LD. That failure belongs to the Product JSON-LD check.

An AggregateOffer passes when it is complete, but the report notes that it is not eligible for merchant listings. The evidence counts missing prices, currencies and availability values separately, so you can see which field is the problem.

## How to fix it

Generate the offer from live product data, not from a template with fixed values.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WooCommerce core adds an Offer with price, currency and availability derived from the product's stock status.
- For a variable product whose variations have different prices, core outputs an `AggregateOffer` with `lowPrice` and `highPrice`. That passes, but it is not eligible for Google merchant listings. To give each variation its own Offer, model the product as a ProductGroup. See the [variants check](/knowledge-base/product-variants-markup).
- Core also sets `priceValidUntil`, using the sale end date when a sale is scheduled. If that date passes and a page cache keeps the old HTML, the markup carries an expired date. Purge the page cache when sales end.
- Products with an empty price, such as "price on request" items, produce an incomplete or zero-price offer. Give them a real price, or keep them out of the catalog.

### Shopify

- Shopify themes that use the native product markup output an Offer per variant with price, currency and availability.
- Custom themes sometimes hard-code `"availability": "InStock"` without the schema.org URL. The scanner rejects bare values. Use the full URL.
- Check that currency matches the market. A store selling in several currencies should output the currency of the price shown on that page.

### Any other stack

- Write the price as a plain number in a string, with a dot as the decimal separator: `"1299.00"`, not `"1.299,00 €"`.
- Map your stock states to ItemAvailability URLs: in stock to `InStock`, out of stock to `OutOfStock`, backorders to `BackOrder`, preorders to `PreOrder`.
- Only add `priceValidUntil` when the price really expires. If you add it, keep it in the future.

This shape covers a product sold in one currency with a sale price that ends on a known date:

```json
"offers": {
  "@type": "Offer",
  "url": "https://example.com/product/stoneware-mug/",
  "price": "19.90",
  "priceCurrency": "EUR",
  "availability": "https://schema.org/InStock",
  "priceValidUntil": "2026-12-31",
  "itemCondition": "https://schema.org/NewCondition"
}
```

## Verify it yourself

Run a product URL through Google's [Rich Results Test](https://search.google.com/test/rich-results). Under "Merchant listings", missing or invalid offer fields appear as errors or warnings. The [Schema Markup Validator](https://validator.schema.org/) shows the raw Offer values.

To check availability values quickly:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/product/stoneware-mug/ | grep -o '"availability": *"[^"]*"'
```

Every match should start with `https://schema.org/` or `http://schema.org/`.

## Common mistakes

- **Bare availability values.** `"InStock"` without the schema.org URL is not a valid ItemAvailability value here.
- **Currency symbols in the price.** `"€24"` is not a number. Put the currency in `priceCurrency`.
- **An expired `priceValidUntil`.** A date set once and never updated eventually lies in the past.
- **Zero-price placeholders.** Free samples or "call for price" items output `"price": "0"`, which merchant listings reject.
- **Offers missing on the ProductGroup and its variants.** The group exists, but nothing under it carries a price.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between Offer and AggregateOffer?

An Offer is one price for one purchasable item. An AggregateOffer summarises several offers with a price range, using `lowPrice` and `highPrice`. Google accepts AggregateOffer for product snippets, but merchant listings require an Offer.

### Is priceValidUntil required?

No. The scanner does not require it. It only warns when the field is present and names a past date, because Google says the listing may not display in that case.

### Which availability value should I use for preorders?

Use `https://schema.org/PreOrder` for items customers can order before release. Use `BackOrder` for items that are temporarily out of stock but can still be ordered.

### Should the price include tax?

Use the price your page shows to the shopper. The [markup versus page check](/knowledge-base/markup-matches-visible-page) compares the two, and a tax-inclusive page with a tax-exclusive markup price will not match.
