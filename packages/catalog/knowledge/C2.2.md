## In short

Some privacy laws ask a business to appoint a person responsible for data protection and to publish how to reach them: the data protection officer (DPO) under the GDPR, the encarregado under Brazil's LGPD, and the person in charge of the protection of personal information under Quebec's Law 25. This check reads your privacy notice and reports whether it names such a role together with an email address. It cannot tell whether your business must appoint one.

## What this check looks at

The rules differ in who must appoint someone, but agree that the contact must be public:

- **European Union.** The [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), Article 37(7), asks a controller or processor that has designated a DPO to publish the DPO's contact details and communicate them to the supervisory authority. Article 37(1) makes a DPO mandatory when the core activities consist of large-scale regular and systematic monitoring of individuals, or large-scale processing of special categories of data. Member state law can ask for a DPO in more cases. Article 13(1)(b) asks the privacy notice to give the DPO's contact details where there is one.
- **Brazil.** The [LGPD](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2015-2018/2018/lei/l13709.htm), Article 41, asks the controller to appoint an encarregado, and §1 asks for the encarregado's identity and contact information to be published clearly and objectively, preferably on the controller's website. The ANPD's Resolution CD/ANPD 18/2024 adds that it must be in a prominent, easily accessible place on the website. Small processing agents exempted from appointing an encarregado under Resolution CD/ANPD 2/2022 must still offer a communication channel for data subjects.
- **Canada (Quebec).** The private-sector privacy act, section 3.1 as amended by Law 25, makes the person with the highest authority in the business responsible for the protection of personal information by default, with the option to delegate in writing. The title and contact information of that person must be published on the business's website.

Under PIPEDA, the federal law for the rest of Canada, the openness principle also asks organisations to make available the name or title and address of the person accountable for their privacy practices.

## Why it matters for AI agents

People increasingly ask assistants to act on their privacy rights: "ask this store what data it has about me" or "delete my account at this shop". An agent can only route that request well when the store names who handles it and gives an address. A published DPO, encarregado or privacy officer email is the most direct answer, and it is the one the laws above point to.

The same contact helps answer engines describe a store accurately. "Privacy questions go to `privacy@example.com`, handled by the data protection officer" is a fact an agent can quote. A notice that only offers a general contact form gives it nothing to quote. A role and an email address in the privacy notice, in text, are easy for any crawler to find.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reads the privacy notice found by the [privacy notice check](/knowledge-base/privacy-notice-linked). It looks for a named role followed, within about 600 characters, by an email address. The roles it recognises include data protection officer, DPO, Datenschutzbeauftragter, délégué à la protection des données, encarregado, responsable de la protection des renseignements personnels and privacy officer.

- **Found:** a role is named and an email address follows it closely.
- **Review:** the role is named without an email address near it, or the notice gives an email address but names no role.
- **Not found in the pages read:** the notice names no role and gives no email address.

The check runs only when the store appears to sell to the EU, Brazil or Canada. Otherwise it is reported as not applicable. When no readable privacy notice was found, this check is skipped, because there is nothing to read.

The scanner reads only the privacy notice, as the server sends it. A DPO contact on a separate page, in an image or behind a form is not seen. It cannot tell whether your business must appoint a DPO or encarregado, and not every business must. Where none is required, a contact for privacy requests still is. This check is reported, never scored.

## How to fix it

Add a section to your privacy notice titled with the role, for example "Data protection officer", "Encarregado" or "Person in charge of the protection of personal information", and give the name or title and an email address. If you have not appointed anyone and are not required to, give a dedicated email address for privacy requests instead. Ask a lawyer whether your business must appoint a DPO, encarregado or privacy officer, and have the wording reviewed.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Edit the page selected under Settings, Privacy, and add a section with the role and an email address, in text.
- Use a dedicated address such as privacy@ or dpo@ that someone reads, rather than a personal mailbox that may change.
- If a legal-text plugin generates the notice, enter the DPO details in its settings so they are not overwritten.

### Shopify

- Edit Settings, Policies, Privacy policy, and add a section with the role and an email address.
- If you use Shopify's generated policy text, check that the DPO or privacy contact appears. Add it if the generator leaves it out.
- Keep the same address in the Contact information policy if you list privacy contacts there.

### Any other stack

- Add a heading with the role and, directly below it, the name or title and an email address as text or a `mailto:` link.
- Keep the role and the email address close together, in the same short section.
- Use the term the law uses in each language version, such as "encarregado" in Portuguese or "responsable de la protection des renseignements personnels" in French.

## Verify it yourself

Look for a role and an email address in your privacy notice:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/privacy | sed -e 's/<[^>]*>/ /g' | grep -oiE '(data protection officer|dpo|datenschutzbeauftragte[rn]?|encarregad[oa]|privacy officer|protection des (données|renseignements personnels)).{0,200}' | grep -oiE '.{0,80}[a-z0-9._%+-]+@[a-z0-9.-]+\.[a-z]{2,}' | head -3
```

No output means no role is followed closely by an email address. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **A role without a contact.** "We have appointed a data protection officer" without an email address does not let anyone reach them.
- **Only a contact form.** Several of the laws above ask for contact information, and the scanner needs an email address.
- **The contact on a separate page.** The scanner reads the privacy notice, so put the contact there, or at least repeat it.
- **A personal address that goes stale.** A role address such as `dpo@example.com` survives staff changes.
- **The wrong term for the market.** A Brazilian site that names a "DPO" but not the encarregado, or a Quebec site without the French term, may confuse readers and machines.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

It depends on the law and your business. Under the GDPR, a DPO is mandatory only in the cases of Article 37(1) and where member state law asks for one. A business that appoints one voluntarily must still publish the contact. Under the LGPD, controllers must appoint an encarregado unless they qualify as small processing agents, who must still offer a contact channel. Under Quebec's Law 25, every business has a person in charge, by default its highest authority. The scanner runs this check only for EU, Brazilian and Canadian markets and cannot tell which rules apply to you.

### Do I have to name the person, or is a role address enough?

The GDPR asks for the DPO's contact details, which a role address can provide. The LGPD asks for the encarregado's identity and contact information. Quebec asks for the person's title and contact information. Ask a lawyer what your market expects. The scanner looks for the role and an email address.

### We have no DPO. What should the notice say?

Do not claim one. Give a contact for privacy requests, such as `privacy@example.com`, and say who handles them. The scanner reports an email address without a role as review, so a person can check that this is intended.

### Can the DPO be an external service?

Under the GDPR, a DPO can be a staff member or an external provider under a service contract (Article 37(6)). Under the LGPD, the encarregado can also be external, including a company. Under Law 25, the person in charge can delegate the function in writing to another person. Publish the contact details of whoever holds the role.
