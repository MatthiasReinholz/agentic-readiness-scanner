## In short

Privacy laws in most markets ask a business that collects personal data to publish a privacy notice and to say who is responsible for the data and how to reach them. This check follows the privacy link on your home page and reports whether the page names a company or controller with an email or postal address. It cannot judge whether the rest of the notice is complete or accurate.

## What this check looks at

Two things are common to almost every privacy law: the notice must be easy to reach, and it must identify who is responsible.

- **European Union and United Kingdom.** The [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), Article 13(1)(a), and the UK GDPR, Article 13(1)(a), ask the controller to give its identity and contact details when it collects personal data.
- **Switzerland.** The revised Federal Act on Data Protection (revFADP), Article 19(2)(a), asks for the identity and contact details of the controller.
- **California.** The California Online Privacy Protection Act (CalOPPA), Business and Professions Code §22575, asks operators of commercial websites that collect personal information from California residents to conspicuously post a privacy policy. §22577 explains that a text link on the home page that includes the word "privacy" is one way to do that.
- **Canada.** PIPEDA's openness principle (Schedule 1, Principle 4.8) asks organisations to make their privacy policies readily available. In Quebec, the private-sector privacy act, section 8.2 as amended by Law 25, asks businesses that collect personal information by technological means to publish a privacy policy on their website.
- **Australia.** Australian Privacy Principle 1 asks APP entities to have a clearly expressed, up-to-date privacy policy and to make it freely available, usually on their website.
- **Brazil.** The LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018), Article 9, gives data subjects the right to easy access to information about processing, including the identity and contact details of the controller.
- **Colombia.** Ley 1581 de 2012 and Decreto 1377 de 2013, Article 13, ask the controller's data processing policy to state its name, address, email and telephone.

The [privacy policy and terms check](/knowledge-base/privacy-policy-and-terms) in the readiness score asks only whether the pages exist. This check adds whether the privacy notice says who is responsible. The page lists every law this check is tagged with.

## Why it matters for AI agents

An agent that buys on someone's behalf hands the store that person's name, address and payment details. Before it does, it is reasonable for the agent, and the person, to know who will hold the data and how to reach them. A privacy notice linked from the home page with a named controller answers both questions in one place.

Answer engines asked "what does this shop do with my data?" or "how do I delete my account at this shop?" quote the privacy notice. They can only quote what they find. A plain link labelled "Privacy" in the server-rendered footer is the route every crawler and agent takes, and a named controller with an email address gives them a concrete answer instead of "the shop does not say". The same name and address in your Organization markup lets the agent connect the notice to the seller.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reads the home page as the server sends it and looks for a link whose label names privacy in the page language. The words it recognises include privacy, Datenschutz, confidentialité, privacidad, privacidade, riservatezza and tratamiento de datos. It then fetches the linked page.

- **Found:** the page answers with HTTP 200 and names a controller or company together with a contact, either an email address or a postal address.
- **Review:** the page exists but names no one.
- **Not found in the pages read:** there is no privacy link on the home page.

A privacy link that leads to an error page does not count as found. The check runs for every store, because almost every market asks for a privacy notice.

The scanner does not run JavaScript, so a footer or consent banner built by scripts is not seen. It does not judge whether the notice covers what the law asks for, such as purposes, legal bases, recipients, retention or rights, or whether the business follows it. This check is reported, never scored.

## How to fix it

Publish a privacy notice that opens with who is responsible: the legal name of the business, its postal address and an email address for privacy questions. Link it from the footer of every page with a label that contains your language's word for privacy. Have the notice reviewed by a lawyer for the markets you sell to, because what it must contain differs by law.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WordPress creates a draft privacy policy page. Complete and publish it, then select it under Settings, Privacy.
- Select the same page under WooCommerce, Settings, Accounts and Privacy, so checkout and account forms link to it.
- Add it to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus, or in the footer template part in the Site Editor, with the label "Privacy policy" or its translation.
- Put the controller's legal name, address and email address in the first section of the notice.

### Shopify

- Write or generate the notice under Settings, Policies, Privacy policy. Shopify publishes it at `/policies/privacy-policy`.
- Check that the generated text names your business and gives a contact; edit it if it does not.
- Link it in the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation. Shopify links policies at checkout on its own, but the home page needs the link too.

### Any other stack

- Serve the notice at a stable URL such as `/privacy` and link it from a `footer` element in the server-rendered HTML of every page.
- Use a link label with the word for privacy in the page language, such as "Privacy policy", "Datenschutzerklärung" or "Política de privacidad".
- State the controller's legal name, postal address and email address as text near the top of the notice.

## Verify it yourself

Find the privacy link on your home page, then check that the page answers and names a contact:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oiE 'href="[^"]*"[^>]*>[^<]*(privacy|datenschutz|confidentialit|privacidad|privacidade|riservatezza)[^<]*' | head -3
curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' https://example.com/privacy
curl -s https://example.com/privacy | grep -oiE '[a-z0-9._%+-]+@[a-z0-9.-]+\.[a-z]{2,}' | sort -u
```

Replace `/privacy` with the URL the first command found. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **No name in the notice.** Templates often say "we" throughout without ever saying who "we" is.
- **The link only in the cookie banner.** Banners are usually built by JavaScript, so the server-rendered home page has no link.
- **A creative label.** "Your data" or "Trust" may not be recognised. Use the word for privacy.
- **A link to a parent company's notice that names a different company.** Make sure the notice names the business that sells to the customer, or explains the relationship.
- **A broken link after a redesign.** A privacy link that answers 404 is worse than none for a visitor and does not count.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

Almost certainly some privacy law asks you for a notice, but which one depends on where you are and where your customers are. The GDPR and UK GDPR apply to businesses established there and to businesses outside that target people there, without a size threshold. CalOPPA applies to commercial websites that collect personal information from California residents. Australia's APPs generally apply to businesses with annual turnover above AUD 3 million, with exceptions. The scanner runs this check for every store and cannot tell which laws apply to yours.

### Does the link have to say "privacy"?

The scanner looks for the word for privacy in the page language. Under CalOPPA, a home-page text link that includes the word "privacy" is one of the ways to post a policy conspicuously. A clear label also helps visitors and agents.

### Is a link in the cookie banner enough?

Not for this check. Cookie banners are usually injected by JavaScript, so the link is not in the HTML the scanner reads. Put the link in the footer as well.

### Does a found result mean my privacy notice is compliant?

No. It means the home page links to a page that answers and names a business with a contact. What the notice must say depends on the law, and whether it is accurate depends on how you process data.
