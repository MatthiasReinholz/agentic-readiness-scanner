## In short

Peru's Consumer Protection Code asks businesses that sell to consumers to keep a complaints book, the Libro de Reclamaciones, and online sellers to offer it in virtual form with a visible link. This check looks for a link labelled "Libro de Reclamaciones" on your home page. It runs only when your store appears to sell to Peru, and it does not open the form behind the link.

## What this check looks at

The duty comes from Peru's Código de Protección y Defensa del Consumidor, [Ley 29571](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/indecopi/normas-legales/2000-ley-n-29571):

- **Article 150** asks commercial establishments to have a Libro de Reclamaciones, in physical or virtual form. Ley 32495, published on 11 November 2025, amended it to name e-commerce platforms expressly.
- **Article 151**, as amended by the same law, asks e-commerce platforms to display the link to the complaints book permanently, in a visible and easily accessible place.
- **The Reglamento del Libro de Reclamaciones**, approved by Decreto Supremo 011-2011-PCM and amended several times, sets the details: the complaint sheet format, the difference between a reclamo (about a product or service) and a queja (about customer service), and the time to answer. Since the 2022 amendment (Decreto Supremo 101-2022-PCM), the provider has 15 business days to answer.

In July 2026 the government published a draft decree to adapt the regulation to Ley 32495 for public consultation. Check the current text with Indecopi, the consumer authority, before relying on details that may change.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A shopper in Peru who asks an assistant "how do I file a complaint against this store?" expects the answer the law gives: the store's Libro de Reclamaciones. An answer engine can only point to it when the link is on the page in words it recognises. The phrase is fixed by law and the same on every Peruvian site, which makes it one of the easiest legal signals for a machine to read.

Shopping agents that weigh whether to buy from a store look for a working complaints route. A visible, standard complaints link is evidence that the store expects to be held to Peruvian consumer law, and a missing one is a reason for caution when the store clearly sells to Peru.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reads the home page as the server sends it and looks at every link.

- **Found:** a link's text, `title` attribute or the `alt` text of an image inside it contains "Libro de Reclamaciones".
- **Not found in the pages read:** no such link is on the home page.

The check runs only when the store appears to sell to Peru, detected from signals such as a `.pe` domain, prices in soles, a Peruvian address or a RUC number. Otherwise it is reported as not applicable.

The scanner does not follow the link, so it cannot tell whether the form works, records complaints, sends the consumer a copy or follows the format in the regulation. A link that only appears after JavaScript runs, or an image without alt text, is not seen. This check is reported, never scored.

## How to fix it

Set up a virtual complaints book that follows the regulation's complaint sheet, and link it from the footer of every page with the words "Libro de Reclamaciones". If you use a book icon as an image, give it the alt text "Libro de Reclamaciones". Have the form and the process reviewed by a Peruvian lawyer, because the regulation is being adapted to Ley 32495.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Create a page for the complaints book with a form that records the fields of the complaint sheet and emails the consumer a copy with its number. Form plugins and dedicated complaints-book plugins can do this.
- Add the page to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus, or in the footer template part in the Site Editor, with the label "Libro de Reclamaciones".
- If the theme shows an icon instead of text, set the image's alt text to "Libro de Reclamaciones".

### Shopify

- Use an app or a page with a form for the complaints book, and make sure each complaint gets a number and the consumer receives a copy.
- Add the page to the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation, with the label "Libro de Reclamaciones".
- Check that the theme renders the footer menu in the HTML, not only after scripts run.

### Any other stack

- Serve the complaints book at a stable URL such as `/libro-de-reclamaciones`.
- Link it from a `footer` element in the server-rendered HTML of every page, with the exact link text "Libro de Reclamaciones".
- Store each complaint with a sequential number and date, send the consumer a copy, and keep the records so you can answer within the legal deadline and show them to Indecopi when asked.

## Verify it yourself

Look for the link on your home page as the server sends it:

```bash
curl -s https://example.pe/ | grep -oi 'libro de reclamaciones' | head -3
```

No output means the phrase is not in the server-rendered HTML. Then open the link in a browser, file a test complaint and check that you receive a copy with a number. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **An icon without alt text.** A book icon is common, but without `alt="Libro de Reclamaciones"` machines cannot tell what it is.
- **A different label.** "Reclamos", "Quejas" or "Contáctanos" are not the term the law uses.
- **The link only on the contact page.** Article 151 asks for a permanent, visible link, and the scanner looks on the home page.
- **A plain contact form.** A complaints book has its own format, numbering and response deadline; a generic form does not meet them.
- **A link added by a script.** If a widget injects the link after the page loads, the server-rendered HTML has none.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

Ley 29571 protects consumers in Peru, and Articles 150 and 151 ask businesses that sell to them, including e-commerce platforms since Ley 32495, to offer a complaints book and, online, a visible link to it. Whether it applies to a store based abroad that ships to Peru depends on how it targets Peruvian consumers. The scanner runs this check only when it detects that the store sells to Peru, and it cannot tell whether the duty applies to your business.

### Can I use Indecopi's system instead of my own form?

The law asks the provider to keep the book. Ask Indecopi or a Peruvian lawyer which implementations are accepted for your business, especially while the regulation is being adapted to Ley 32495. For this check, what matters is the link labelled "Libro de Reclamaciones" on the home page.

### How long do I have to answer a complaint?

Under the regulation as amended in 2022, the provider has 15 business days to answer a reclamo or a queja. Check the current text, because the regulation may change again.

### Does the scanner test the complaint form?

No. It only checks that the home page links to it with the right words. Test the form yourself by filing a complaint and checking that you receive a numbered copy.
