## In short

When a store sells subscriptions, several laws expect customers to be able to cancel online, as easily as they signed up. This check runs only when the pages read offer a subscription, and then looks for a link on the home page that names cancellation. It is a technical signal, never scored, and not legal advice: it cannot tell whether the route actually works or whether a law applies to your business.

## What this check looks at

A subscription here means any offer that renews and charges again until the customer stops it: monthly boxes, "subscribe and save", memberships, software plans, service contracts. The rules for ending them differ by market:

- **California (US):** the Automatic Renewal Law in the Business and Professions Code, as amended by AB 2863 for contracts entered into, amended or extended from 1 July 2025, requires that a customer who signed up online can also cancel online, without extra obstacles.
- **United States, federal:** the Federal Trade Commission's "click-to-cancel" amendments to its Negative Option Rule were vacated by the US Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025, before they took effect. The Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA) still requires a simple way to stop recurring charges, and other states have their own automatic renewal laws.
- **Germany (EU):** section 312k of the Civil Code (BGB), in force since 1 July 2022, requires a cancellation button on websites where consumers can conclude ongoing contracts. It must be labelled "Verträge hier kündigen" or something equally clear, and lead to a confirmation page whose button reads "jetzt kündigen" or similar.
- **United Kingdom:** the subscription contracts regime in Part 4, Chapter 2 of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCC Act) is planned to apply from 1 January 2027. It includes easy ways to end a subscription. The report lists it with that date and does not treat it as missing before then.
- **Argentina:** Disposición 954/2025 of the national consumer authority, published on 4 September 2025, requires a "botón de baja" for cancelling services, alongside the withdrawal button covered by the [Argentine withdrawal button check](/knowledge-base/boton-de-arrepentimiento-argentina).

The report shows the full list of laws with links to the official texts.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A published cancellation route is an action an agent can take for its principal. People already ask assistants to "cancel that subscription for me". Browsing agents, such as the agent modes in ChatGPT and in AI browsers, act by reading a page and following links and forms. A labelled link to a cancellation page, and a form that works without a phone call, is something such an agent can find and complete. A route that only exists as "call us during business hours" or behind a chat that never ends is a dead end for the agent and for the person.

The same clarity helps before purchase. An agent comparing subscription offers can tell its user how to get out of each one, and a store with a visible exit is easier to recommend honestly.

This check does not prove that an agent can cancel. It only shows that a route is published on the pages read.

## How the scanner decides

The check first decides whether it applies. It runs when the home page or a sampled product page offers a subscription: wording such as auto-renew, billed monthly, subscribe and save, Abonnement or suscripción, or a `billingDuration` in price markup. When no subscription is detected, it is reported as not applicable. It runs for stores that appear to sell to the US, the EU, the UK or Argentina.

When it runs, it reads the links, buttons and image alt text on the home page, and the terms page:

- **Found:** a home page link or button names cancellation, for example "cancel subscription", "manage subscription", "Kündigung", "Verträge hier kündigen" or "botón de baja".
- **Review:** the terms mention cancellation but the home page has no cancellation link, or no cancellation route is found at all.

This check never shows not found. A missing route is always a review, because the scanner cannot tell whether a law applies. Rules that are not in force yet, such as the UK regime from 1 January 2027, are listed with the day they apply from.

What it cannot see:

- Whether the route works, asks for a login, or adds steps after the first click.
- Whether the cancellation is confirmed, or when the contract ends.
- Anything in the customer account, the checkout or the confirmation emails.
- Whether a law applies to your business, for example whether you sell to consumers in that market.

## How to fix it

Publish one cancellation page at a stable URL, link it from the footer and from the account area, and let customers cancel there without phoning or emailing. Have the wording and the flow reviewed for each market you sell to.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Subscriptions in WooCommerce usually come from a subscriptions extension, which adds a Cancel action under My Account, Subscriptions. Check that the action is enabled for customers in the extension's settings.
- Create a page called "Cancel subscription" that explains the steps and links to My Account, and add it to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus or in the footer template part of a block theme.
- For German customers, add a footer link labelled "Verträge hier kündigen" that leads to a form usable without signing in. Legal-text plugins for the German market offer such a form. Otherwise, a simple form that sends the request to the shop and confirms it by email can work.

### Shopify

- Shopify's own Subscriptions app and third-party subscription apps give customers a self-service cancel option in the customer account. Turn it on in the app's settings.
- Add a page under Online Store, Pages, called "Cancel subscription", that explains how to cancel, and add it to the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation.
- For German customers, link a page labelled "Verträge hier kündigen" from the footer with a form that does not require signing in.

### Any other stack

- Serve the cancellation page and the footer link in the initial HTML, so they can be read without JavaScript.
- Name the link plainly: "Cancel subscription", "Kündigung" or "Verträge hier kündigen", "Cancelar suscripción" or "botón de baja". Avoid vague labels such as "Manage plan" on their own.
- If you use schema.org price markup with `billingDuration` for subscription prices, keep the cancellation link on the same product page or in the shared footer.

## Verify it yourself

Look for subscription wording and a cancellation link in the raw HTML of your home page and a subscription product page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oiE "auto-?renew|billed monthly|subscribe (and|&) save|billingDuration" | sort -u
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oiE "cancel[^<]{0,30}subscription|kündig[^<]{0,30}|botón de baja" | sort -u
```

Then try it as a customer would: open the link in a private window and check that you reach a way to cancel without calling anyone.

## Common mistakes

- **Cancellation only by phone or email.** California and Germany both expect an online route when signing up happened online.
- **The link exists only inside the account.** A customer who forgot their password, or an agent without one, cannot find it. Link it publicly too.
- **A vague label.** "Manage membership" or "Help" does not tell anyone that cancellation lives there.
- **Retention steps that never end.** An offer to stay can be shown, but the customer must be able to finish cancelling.
- **The link is added by JavaScript only.** A footer injected by a script may be missing from the HTML that agents and this scanner read.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

It applies when you sell contracts that renew automatically. California's law covers automatic renewal offers to consumers in California. Germany's §312k covers consumer contracts for ongoing obligations concluded through a website. The UK regime is planned for 1 January 2027 for consumer subscriptions. Argentina's rule covers providers selling remotely to Argentine consumers. Whether any of these applies to your business depends on facts the scan cannot see. Ask a lawyer.

### Is the FTC click-to-cancel rule in force?

No. The Eighth Circuit vacated it on 8 July 2025, before its compliance date. ROSCA and state laws such as California's still apply.

### Does a "Found" result mean my cancellation process is compliant?

No. It means a link naming cancellation was found on the home page. The scan never signs in, never submits forms and cannot judge the steps after the first click.

### Why does the check say "applies from" for the UK?

The DMCC Act's subscription rules are not in force yet. The report lists them with the planned date, 1 January 2027, so you can prepare, and does not count them as missing before then.

### My store sells no subscriptions. Why is the check shown?

It is shown as not applicable. If it runs anyway, the pages read contained subscription wording, for example a "subscribe and save" option from an app. Check the evidence in the report for the page and phrase that triggered it.
