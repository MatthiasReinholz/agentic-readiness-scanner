## In short

Finding a policy link is not the same as being able to read the policy. This check fetches each return, shipping, privacy and terms page the scanner found and tests three things: it answers HTTP 200, it has more than 100 words, and robots.txt (the file that tells crawlers what they may fetch) lets the crawlers behind AI answers read it. A noindex directive (an instruction to keep the page out of search indexes) on an otherwise readable page produces a warning.

## What this check looks at

The other policy checks find the pages. This one asks whether an agent can use them. It looks at up to four pages: return policy, shipping policy, privacy policy and terms. Imprint and contact pages are covered by the [merchant identity](/knowledge-base/merchant-identity) and [contact channel](/knowledge-base/contact-channel) checks instead.

For each page it tests:

- **The response.** The page must answer HTTP 200 to the scanner's honest request.
- **The text.** The page must carry more than 100 words of visible text outside scripts, styles and similar elements. An empty shell that fills itself with JavaScript has almost none.
- **robots.txt.** None of the eleven search and assistant crawlers from the [crawler access check](/knowledge-base/search-and-assistant-crawlers-allowed), such as Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, may be disallowed on the page's path. This test applies to pages on the store's own origin.
- **Index directives.** A `noindex` in the robots meta tag or the `X-Robots-Tag` header is recorded.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Agents answer questions about your store in two ways. Some fetch your pages live when a user asks. Many answer from search indexes that crawlers built in advance. A policy page that robots.txt closes to those crawlers is missing from the index, so an assistant cannot quote your return window when someone asks about it.

`noindex` works differently. The page is still reachable for any agent that fetches it, but search engines keep it out of their index. That index is what grounds many AI answers. Shopify, for example, marks its policy pages as noindex by default. The page works for a live visit but is less likely to show up in an answer.

A page with almost no text is the third trap. A policy that loads from a script, or a page that only says "see our FAQ", gives an agent nothing to read.

This check carries weight 2, which means expected. It is not a level gate, but it decides whether the pages that the Level 3 checks found are of any use.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner takes the return, shipping, privacy and terms pages that discovery found. A return or shipping link that the [return policy](/knowledge-base/return-policy-page) or [shipping policy](/knowledge-base/shipping-policy-page) check rejected as a product page is left out. A page is readable when it answers 200, has more than 100 words and no listed crawler is disallowed on its path.

- **Pass:** every found page is readable and none carries noindex.
- **Warn:** some found pages are readable and some are not. The check also warns when every page is readable but at least one carries noindex.
- **Fail:** none of the found pages is readable.
- **Skip:** no policy page was found. The finding then belongs to the policy checks themselves.

The message lists each problem by page: the HTTP status, the word count, the crawlers that robots.txt blocks, or the noindex directive.

## How to fix it

Serve policy pages as plain, server-rendered text. Keep them out of robots.txt `Disallow` rules and leave out `noindex` unless you have a reason to hide them from search.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Policy pages are ordinary WordPress pages. Check the SEO plugin's settings on each one: an option such as "Allow search engines to show this page" or a noindex toggle may be set.
- Some privacy and cookie plugins load their policy text with JavaScript or from an external service. Paste the text into the page instead so it is in the HTML.
- Review robots.txt, either the physical file in the web root or the SEO plugin's editor, for rules that disallow `/privacy`, `/terms` or `/legal` paths.

### Shopify

- Shopify's policy pages under `/policies/` are noindex by default. If you want them indexed, one approach is to publish the same text as a regular page under Online Store, Pages, and link that page from the footer. Regular pages are not noindex by default.
- Check `robots.txt.liquid` in your theme, if it exists, for rules that disallow `/policies/`.
- Apps that edit robots.txt can add such rules too. Review their settings after any change.

### Any other stack

- Render the policy text on the server. A single-page app that fetches the text after load shows crawlers an empty shell.
- Remove `noindex` from the robots meta tag and the `X-Robots-Tag` header on these pages.
- Remove robots.txt rules that cover the policy paths, or add `Allow` rules for them.

## Verify it yourself

Check the status, the noindex signals and the rough word count of a policy page:

```bash
curl -sI https://example.com/policies/refund-policy | grep -iE '^HTTP|x-robots-tag'
curl -s https://example.com/policies/refund-policy | grep -oiE 'name="robots"[^>]*'
curl -s https://example.com/policies/refund-policy | sed -e 's/<[^>]*>/ /g' | wc -w
```

Then test the path against robots.txt with Google Search Console's robots.txt report or Bing Webmaster Tools. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **Disallowing legal pages "because they are boring".** Some SEO advice suggests hiding thin legal pages. For agents, those pages answer real questions.
- **Policies inside a JavaScript widget.** The text never reaches the HTML that crawlers read.
- **A link to a short summary page.** A page that only says "see our full terms" with a PDF link has too few words.
- **Forgetting the default noindex.** On Shopify, the policy pages are noindex unless you change your setup.
- **Blocking with a wildcard rule.** A rule like `Disallow: /*?` or `Disallow: /pages/` can catch policy pages by accident.

## FAQ

### Should policy pages be noindex?

Not if you want AI answers to quote them. noindex keeps a page out of search indexes, which many assistants use to answer questions. The scanner warns rather than fails, because the page can still be fetched directly.

### Why are Shopify policy pages noindex?

Shopify adds the directive to its built-in policy pages by default. The pages still work for visitors and for agents that fetch them. They are just kept out of search indexes.

### How many words does a policy page need?

More than 100 words of visible text for this check. That is a floor to tell a real policy from an empty shell or a stub, not a target.

### Does the check fail if robots.txt blocks AI training crawlers on my policies?

No. Only the search and assistant crawlers count, such as Googlebot, Bingbot and OAI-SearchBot. Training crawlers like GPTBot are not part of this check.
