## In short

Before an agent recommends a store or pays it, it helps to know who the seller is, where it is based and how to reach it. This check looks for Organization JSON-LD (structured data in the page that describes the business) with a name and an address or contact detail, or an imprint page that shows a postal address. Either one passes.

## What this check looks at

There are two places where a store states its identity.

**Organization JSON-LD.** A block of structured data, usually on the home page, that describes the business. schema.org's `Organization` type, and subtypes such as `OnlineStore` or `LocalBusiness`, carry properties like `name`, `legalName`, `address`, `contactPoint`, `telephone` and `email`.

**An imprint page.** Many European stores publish a page with the legal name, postal address and registration details of the business. It is called Impressum in German, mentions légales in French, aviso legal in Spanish and note legali in Italian. In English it is often "Legal notice" or "Imprint".

The scanner looks at the home page first, then the imprint page linked from it, then the contact page. It finds the imprint link the same way it finds policy links: the label or the last part of the path must contain one of the words above. An about page does not count.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A shopper who asks an agent "is this store legitimate?" or "who actually sells this?" deserves an answer from the store itself. A name and an address that machines can read let the agent connect the shop to a real business, match it with other sources and show the shopper who they are dealing with.

Structured data is the most reliable route, because it does not depend on page layout or language. An imprint page is the human-readable equivalent and is common in Europe, where many businesses publish one.

This check carries weight 2, which means expected. It is not a level gate. It strengthens trust signals without blocking a level on its own.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner stops at the first source that satisfies the check:

1. **Home page JSON-LD.** It reads every Organization node, including subtypes and types ending in Store or Business. A node is complete when it has a `name` and at least one of `address`, `contactPoint`, `telephone` or `email`.
2. **Imprint page.** If the home page links an imprint or legal notice page, the scanner fetches it. Complete Organization JSON-LD there passes. So does text that shows a postal address: a postal code of four to six digits followed by a place name, such as "10115 Berlin", and a street name followed by a house number, such as "Musterstraße 1".
3. **Contact page.** If the home page links a contact page, complete Organization JSON-LD there passes.

The verdicts:

- **Pass:** any of the three sources above satisfies the check.
- **Warn:** an Organization node exists but has no name, or has a name but no address or contact detail. The check also warns when there is no Organization node but the imprint page answers without a complete postal address.
- **Fail:** no Organization JSON-LD anywhere it looked, and no imprint page with a postal address.

The address text test follows the common European format. Addresses written in other formats may not be recognised in plain text, which is one more reason to publish Organization JSON-LD.

## How to fix it

Add Organization JSON-LD to your home page with the business's name, postal address and a contact point. If you publish an imprint page, state the same details there in text and link it with a label such as "Imprint" or "Legal notice". Whether you must publish an imprint depends on where you are based and sell.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Most SEO plugins have a setting that describes the site as an organization, with name, logo and contact details, and output it as Organization JSON-LD. Fill in the address and contact fields too, not just the name and logo.
- The store address under WooCommerce, Settings, General does not necessarily reach your structured data. Check the page source, and copy the address into the SEO plugin's organization settings if it is missing.
- Create an imprint or legal notice page with the same details and link it from the footer.

### Shopify

- Shopify themes do not all output an Organization block with an address. View your home page source and search for `"Organization"` to see what is there.
- If the address is missing, add an Organization JSON-LD block to the theme layout, filled from your store details, or edit the existing block in the theme's code.
- Add a page under Online Store, Pages, for your legal notice, and link it from the footer menu with a label such as "Legal notice" or "Impressum".

### Any other stack

- Output one Organization (or `OnlineStore`) node on the home page with `name`, `url`, `address` as a `PostalAddress`, and a `contactPoint` with an email or phone number.
- Keep the legal name, address and contact details identical across the JSON-LD, the imprint page and your policies.
- Link the imprint or legal notice page from the footer.

## Verify it yourself

Look for an Organization node on the home page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oE '"@type"\s*:\s*"(Organization|OnlineStore|LocalBusiness|Store)"' | head
```

Then paste the home page URL into the Schema Markup Validator at validator.schema.org and check that the Organization has a name and an address or contact point. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **Name and logo only.** Many SEO plugin defaults output an Organization with a name and logo but no address or contact. The scanner warns.
- **The address in an image.** A picture of the imprint cannot be read as text.
- **Different details in different places.** A legal name on the imprint that differs from the JSON-LD makes the business harder to match.
- **An imprint reachable only through a cookie banner or popup.** The scanner follows links in the server-rendered home page.

## FAQ

### What is Organization structured data?

It is a JSON-LD block that describes your business to machines using the schema.org `Organization` type: its name, address, logo, contact points and profiles elsewhere. It usually sits on the home page.

### Do I need an imprint page?

That depends on the law where you are based and sell. For this check, an imprint page with a postal address is one way to pass. Organization JSON-LD with a name and contact details is the other.

### Can I use OnlineStore or LocalBusiness instead of Organization?

Yes. They are subtypes of Organization, and the scanner accepts them, as well as other types whose name ends in Store or Business.

### Does this check publish my address anywhere?

No. It only reads what your site already publishes. If you prefer not to show a street address, a contact point with an email or phone number in the JSON-LD also passes.
