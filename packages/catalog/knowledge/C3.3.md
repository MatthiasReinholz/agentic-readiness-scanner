## In short

Many countries give online shoppers a legal right to withdraw from a purchase within a fixed number of days: 14 in the EU and the UK, 10 in Argentina and Chile, 7 in Brazil and 5 business days in Colombia. This check reads the store's returns policy and its MerchantReturnPolicy markup and compares the stated period with the minimum for each market the store appears to sell to.

## What this check looks at

The statutory periods, by market:

| Market    | Minimum period   | Provision                                                     |
| --------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| EU        | 14 days          | Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, Art. 9                  |
| UK        | 14 days          | Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, regs. 29 to 31           |
| Argentina | 10 calendar days | Ley 24.240, Art. 34                                           |
| Chile     | 10 days          | Ley 19.496, Art. 3 bis (b), as replaced by Ley 21.398 in 2021 |
| Brazil    | 7 days           | Código de Defesa do Consumidor (Lei 8.078/1990), Art. 49      |
| Colombia  | 5 business days  | Ley 1480 de 2011 (Estatuto del Consumidor), Art. 47           |

These are rights to withdraw from a distance contract, not a store's voluntary returns promise. Some details differ:

- **EU and UK.** For goods, the period runs from the day the consumer receives them. If the trader does not inform the consumer about the right, the period is extended by up to 12 months.
- **Brazil.** The right applies to purchases made outside a commercial establishment, which includes the internet, and runs from signing or receipt.
- **Chile.** The right covers contracts made by electronic means. For goods, it does not apply to items that by their nature cannot be returned, deteriorate quickly, are made to the consumer's specifications or are for personal use, as set out in a 2024 regulation. For services, the provider may exclude it if it says so clearly before the contract.
- **Colombia.** The right of "retracto" applies to distance and non-traditional sales and runs from delivery, or from the contract for services.
- **Argentina.** The period runs from delivery or conclusion of the contract, whichever comes later.

Every market also lists exceptions, such as sealed hygiene goods or custom-made items. The scanner does not judge them.

The machine-readable form is schema.org `MerchantReturnPolicy`, whose `merchantReturnDays` gives the return window as a number and `applicableCountry` the countries it covers.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Shopping agents read return windows before they recommend a purchase. "Can I send it back if it does not fit?" is one of the first questions a person asks, and an agent answers it from the store's returns policy page and from `MerchantReturnPolicy` in the product markup, as covered by [shipping and return markup](/knowledge-base/shipping-and-return-markup).

A period stated as a number is something an agent can compare across stores and quote to its principal. A vague promise ("easy returns") or a policy that gives no number leaves the agent to guess, or to fall back on the statutory minimum without knowing whether the store honours it.

The number should agree everywhere. When the policy page says 30 days and the markup says 14, an agent has to decide which one to believe. The route to actually withdraw is a separate signal: [EU withdrawal button](/knowledge-base/eu-withdrawal-button) for the EU and [botón de arrepentimiento](/knowledge-base/boton-de-arrepentimiento-argentina) for Argentina.

## How the scanner decides

The check runs when the store appears to sell to the EU, the UK, Brazil, Chile, Colombia or Argentina, or when you choose one of those markets. Otherwise it is shown as not applicable.

The scanner reads the returns policy page found by the [return policy check](/knowledge-base/return-policy-page) and any `merchantReturnDays` value in the markup of the home page and the sampled product pages. On the policy page it looks for a number followed by a word for days, such as "14 days", "14 Tage", "10 días" or "7 dias". It takes the longest period it finds and compares it with the minimum from the table above for each detected market.

- **Found:** the longest stated period meets the minimum of every detected market. A store that sells in the EU and Brazil needs at least 14 days.
- **Review:** no period in days is found, or no returns policy was found and the markup gives no period.
- **Not found in the pages read:** the longest stated period is shorter than the minimum of at least one detected market.

What the scanner cannot see:

- Whether the period runs from the right day, such as delivery rather than order.
- Whether a number counts calendar days or business days, and which period a number on the page refers to. A page that says "30 days" for anything is read as a 30-day period.
- Exceptions, conditions, fees and who pays for return shipping.
- Whether the store honours the period in practice.
- Policies that appear only after JavaScript runs, at checkout or in order emails.

This is a technical reading of public pages, not legal advice.

## How to fix it

State the period as a number of days in a plain sentence on the returns policy, and publish the same number as `merchantReturnDays`. If you offer a longer voluntary window, state both. Have a lawyer review the policy.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Edit the returns or refund policy page (recent WooCommerce versions create a draft "Refund and Returns Policy" page on install) so it says, for example, "You can withdraw from your purchase within 14 days of receiving the goods."
- Add the return policy to product markup with your SEO or product-feed plugin's return settings, with `merchantReturnDays` set to the same number.
- Link the policy from the footer so this scanner and others find it.

### Shopify

- Under Settings, Policies, set up return rules with the return window and edit the refund policy text so it states the number of days.
- Check that the period matches every market you sell to in Shopify Markets.
- View a product page source to see whether your theme or an app outputs `MerchantReturnPolicy`, and add it if not.

### Any other stack

- Put the period in a sentence on the returns page, in each storefront language.
- Output `MerchantReturnPolicy` with `merchantReturnDays`, `returnPolicyCategory`, `applicableCountry` and `merchantReturnLink`, on product pages or once on the Organization.
- Keep one source for the number so the page and the markup cannot drift apart.

## Verify it yourself

Look for a period in days on your returns page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/returns/ | grep -ioE "[0-9]+ ?(calendar |business )?(days|tage|días|dias|jours|giorni|dagen)"
```

Then check the markup on a product page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/products/example/ | grep -oE '"merchantReturnDays"\s*:\s*[0-9]+'
```

Both should show the same number, and at least the minimum for each market you sell to. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **No number.** "Hassle-free returns" says nothing an agent or a consumer can rely on.
- **Different numbers in different places.** The policy page, the markup and the footer text should agree.
- **One period for all markets.** A 7-day window may be fine in Brazil and too short in the EU. Use at least the longest minimum of the markets you sell to.
- **Confusing returns with withdrawal.** A voluntary returns policy can be more generous than the law but never replaces the statutory right.
- **Counting calendar days in Colombia.** The Colombian minimum is five business days.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

The statutory periods apply to sales to consumers at a distance in those markets, with exceptions that differ by country and product. Sales only to businesses are generally outside them. The scanner cannot see whether you sell to consumers or whether your products are excluded, but a lawyer can.

### My store offers 30 days. Is that enough?

For this check, yes: 30 days meets every minimum in the table. Whether your policy text and conditions are otherwise correct is outside what the scanner can see.

### Do I need MerchantReturnPolicy markup?

The check accepts either the policy page or the markup. Markup makes the number machine-readable for search engines and agents, so publishing both is the most useful.

### Which period applies when I sell to several markets?

The scanner compares your stated period with the minimum of every market it detects, so the longest minimum decides. Stating one period that meets the longest minimum is the simplest for people, for agents and for this check.
