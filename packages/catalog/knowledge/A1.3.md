## In short

A product sitemap is the simplest way to hand a crawler or AI agent your whole catalog. When every published product URL sits in a sitemap, an agent can list your products without crawling category pages one by one. This check passes when the scanner reads at least one product URL from your sitemaps.

## What this check looks at

The check builds on the [sitemap check](/knowledge/sitemap-declared-and-reachable). Once a sitemap is found, the scanner looks for product pages in two ways:

- **A product sitemap by name.** In a sitemap index, child sitemaps whose file names mark them as product sitemaps are read first. Recognised names include WooCommerce's `wp-sitemap-posts-product-1.xml`, the `product-sitemap.xml` that Yoast SEO and Rank Math publish, and Shopify's `sitemap_products_1.xml`. Names in other languages, such as `produkte` or `produits`, are recognised too.
- **Product URLs by path.** In any URL set, entries whose path contains a product segment count as product URLs. Examples are `/product/`, `/products/`, `/p/`, `/item/`, `/dp/` and localised forms such as `/produkt/` or `/artikel/`.

Every URL in a recognised product sitemap counts as a product, except the bare store root that Shopify lists first. The scanner stops reading child sitemaps once it has found product URLs.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Answering "does this store sell a waterproof jacket under 200 euros" starts with knowing which products exist. Agents and the crawlers behind AI answers can find products by following links from your navigation. That is slow, and it misses products that only appear behind filters, deep pagination or a "load more" button. A product sitemap removes the guesswork.

The check carries weight 2, the expected tier. It also counts towards Level 2. To reach that level a store must make its catalog enumerable in at least one way: product URLs in the sitemap, a [platform store API](/knowledge/store-api-reachable), a [public product feed](/knowledge/public-product-feed), [Shopify's products.json](/knowledge/shopify-products-json) or [Shopify's agentic storefront catalog](/knowledge/shopify-agentic-storefront). Any one of them is enough, but the sitemap is the one every crawler already understands.

A product sitemap does not tell an agent the price, stock or variants. Those come from the product page markup and from feeds. It only makes the catalog findable.

## How the scanner decides

- **Pass:** at least one product URL was read, either from a child sitemap whose name marks it as a product sitemap or as an entry whose path matches a product pattern.
- **Warn:** the sitemap index names a product sitemap, but no product URL was read within the scan's budget. Typical causes are a child sitemap that answered an error, was gzip-compressed, or was larger than the 2 MB the scanner reads.
- **Fail:** the sitemaps read contain no product URL and the index names no product sitemap.
- **Skip:** when the [sitemap check](/knowledge/sitemap-declared-and-reachable) fails, this check does not run.

The scanner reads at most four sitemap files per scan. Product sitemaps are read first, so a large index does not hide them.

## How to fix it

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- In WordPress core sitemaps, WooCommerce products appear as `wp-sitemap-posts-product-1.xml` and onwards. If they are missing, a plugin or custom code has excluded the `product` post type.
- In Yoast SEO, open the search appearance settings for content types and make sure Products are shown in search results. Rank Math has a matching sitemap setting per post type.
- Products set to hidden catalog visibility, drafts and private products are left out by design. Check that the products you sell are published and visible.
- If you removed the `/product/` base from your permalinks, product URLs no longer match a path pattern. The product sitemap then has to be recognisable by its name, which the platform defaults already are.

### Shopify

- Shopify's `/sitemap.xml` index always links product sitemaps named `sitemap_products_1.xml`, with a query string for paging. Every product published to the Online Store sales channel appears in them.
- A product that is not published to the Online Store channel is not in the sitemap. Check the product's sales channels if one is missing.
- Shopify product URLs use `/products/`, which the scanner recognises.

### Any other stack

- Generate a dedicated product sitemap from your catalog database, and list it in your sitemap index.
- Give it a recognisable name such as `product-sitemap.xml` or `sitemap-products-1.xml`.
- Include only canonical, indexable product URLs that answer HTTP 200. Leave out filtered, sorted and session URLs.
- Serve it uncompressed and keep each file well under 2 MB if you want this scanner to read it in full; the protocol itself allows up to 50 MB.

## Verify it yourself

List the child sitemaps in your index, then count the URLs in the product sitemap:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/sitemap.xml | grep -io 'https://[^ ]*product[^< ]*'
curl -s https://example.com/wp-sitemap-posts-product-1.xml | grep -o 'https://' | wc -l
```

Replace the second URL with the product sitemap the first command shows. The count should be close to the number of published products in your admin.

## Common mistakes

- **Excluding products in an SEO plugin.** One unchecked box removes the whole catalog from the sitemap.
- **A compressed or oversized product sitemap.** It may be valid for search engines but unreadable within the scanner's limits. Split large sitemaps into several files.
- **Flat product URLs with a generic sitemap name.** A URL like `/blue-mug/` in a file named `sitemap-1.xml` cannot be recognised as a product. Use a product sitemap name or a product path segment.
- **Listing non-canonical URLs.** Variant, tracking or filtered URLs in the sitemap waste crawl budget and point agents at duplicates. See [canonical and hreflang](/knowledge/canonical-and-hreflang).

## FAQ

### Should products have their own sitemap?

Yes, if you can. A dedicated product sitemap is easy for crawlers and agents to find in the index, and it lets you track how many products are listed. WooCommerce, the common SEO plugins and Shopify all create one by default.

### Why are my WooCommerce products missing from the sitemap?

Usually an SEO plugin excludes the product post type, or the products are drafts, private or hidden from the catalog. Check the plugin's content type settings and the product's visibility.

### Does Shopify include all products in the sitemap?

Shopify includes products that are published to the Online Store sales channel. Products available only in other channels, or not yet published, are left out.

### Is a product feed a substitute for a product sitemap?

For reaching Level 2, yes: a public product feed, a store API or a product sitemap each make the catalog enumerable. Search crawlers still rely on sitemaps, so keep one even if you publish a feed.
