## In short

From 2016 until July 2025, online sellers in the EU had to link to the European Commission's Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) platform. The platform has closed and the rule that required the link was repealed with effect from 20 July 2025. This check reports when your home page, terms or imprint still link to the platform or mention it, so you can update the text. A leftover link is outdated legal text, not a breach in itself.

## What this check looks at

The ODR Regulation, Regulation (EU) No 524/2013, set up the platform and, in Article 14, asked traders established in the EU who sell online to put an electronic link to it on their websites. Many imprints and terms still carry a sentence such as "The European Commission provides a platform for online dispute resolution" with a link to `ec.europa.eu/consumers/odr`.

[Regulation (EU) 2024/3228](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/3228/oj) repealed the ODR Regulation with effect from 20 July 2025. The platform stopped accepting new complaints on 20 March 2025 and closed on 20 July 2025. Since then there is no duty to link to it, and the link no longer leads to a working complaints service.

The repeal did not remove other dispute-resolution duties. Under the ADR Directive (Directive 2013/11/EU), Article 13, traders who are committed or obliged to use an alternative dispute resolution (ADR) body still have to say so and name it. National laws can go further: in Germany, the Consumer Dispute Resolution Act (VSBG), §36, asks many traders with a website or general terms to state whether they are willing to take part in proceedings before a consumer arbitration board. Replace the old ODR paragraph rather than simply deleting it if such a statement is part of it.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Shopping agents and answer engines read a store's terms and imprint to answer questions such as "what can I do if there is a problem with my order?". A paragraph that still points to the ODR platform can lead them to cite a complaints route that no longer exists, which misleads the shopper and reflects on the store.

Outdated legal text is also a maintenance signal. An agent comparing two shops sees that one has kept its legal pages current and the other has not. Clear, current text about how complaints and disputes are handled is easier to quote and easier to trust.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reads the home page, and the terms page and imprint it found in the home page links.

- **Review:** any of them links to `ec.europa.eu/consumers/odr`, or names the ODR platform in text, for example "ODR platform" or "Plattform zur Online-Streitbeilegung".
- **Found (nothing outdated):** none of them does.

The check runs only when the store appears to sell to the EU. Otherwise it is reported as not applicable.

The scanner only reads those pages as the server sends them. A mention in a PDF, in order emails, on another policy page or after JavaScript runs is not seen. It does not check whether you give the ADR information your market asks for. This check is reported, never scored.

## How to fix it

Remove the ODR link and the paragraph that introduces it from your imprint, terms and footer. If the paragraph also said whether you take part in consumer dispute resolution, keep that statement, updated, because that duty did not end. Have the new wording reviewed by a lawyer, especially if your terms came from a generator or a template service.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Search your pages for "odr" and "Streitbeilegung" (or the equivalent in your language) under Pages, and edit the imprint and terms.
- Check widgets, the footer template part and reusable blocks, where an ODR link is often kept.
- If a legal-text plugin or service generates your terms, update or regenerate them there, so your edit is not overwritten.

### Shopify

- Edit Settings, Policies, Terms of service and Contact information, and any legal-notice page under Online Store, Pages.
- Check the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation, for a link to the ODR platform.
- If an app supplies your legal texts, update them in the app.

### Any other stack

- Search the templates, content database and translation files for `consumers/odr`.
- Remove the link and the sentence around it in every language version of the site.
- Keep or add a current statement on consumer dispute resolution if your market asks for one.

## Verify it yourself

Look for the old link on your home page, terms and imprint:

```bash
for path in / /terms /imprint; do curl -s "https://example.com$path" | grep -oiE 'ec\.europa\.eu/consumers/odr[^"]*|online-streitbeilegung|odr platform' | sed "s|^|$path: |"; done
```

Replace the paths with your own. No output means none of the three pages mentions the platform. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **Deleting the whole paragraph.** If it also said whether you take part in consumer dispute resolution, that statement may still be required.
- **Fixing one language only.** Multilingual stores often keep the old paragraph in the other versions.
- **Forgetting generated texts.** A legal-text service or plugin can put the link back on the next update.
- **Leaving it in the footer.** A small "ODR" link in the footer is easy to miss.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

The ODR link duty applied to traders established in the EU who sold goods or services online to consumers. It ended for everyone on 20 July 2025, so no store needs the link any more. The scanner runs this check only when it detects that the store sells to the EU.

### Is a leftover ODR link illegal?

The scanner does not decide that. It reports the link as outdated legal text for review. Whether outdated information has legal consequences in your market is a question for a lawyer.

### What replaces the ODR platform?

There is no EU-wide replacement platform. Consumers can still turn to national ADR bodies, and the European Consumer Centres help with cross-border complaints. Where your market asks you to name an ADR body or say whether you take part, do that instead.

### Do I still need to say whether I take part in dispute resolution?

Possibly. That duty comes from the ADR Directive and national law, not from the repealed ODR Regulation. In Germany, for example, VSBG §36 asks many traders to state it. Ask a lawyer what applies to your business.
