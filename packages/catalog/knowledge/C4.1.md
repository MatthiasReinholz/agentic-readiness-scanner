## In short

An accessibility statement tells customers how your store meets accessibility requirements, where it falls short and how to report a barrier. Since 28 June 2025 the European Accessibility Act asks most online stores selling to EU consumers to publish this information, and Brazil asks websites to show an accessibility symbol. This check looks for a link to such a statement on the home page. It is a technical signal, never scored, and not legal advice.

## What this check looks at

The scanner reads the links on the home page and looks for one whose label or path names accessibility in the page language, such as "Accessibility", "Barrierefreiheit", "Accessibilité" or "Acessibilidade". For Brazil it also accepts the accessibility symbol.

Which laws ask for what, by market:

- **European Union:** the European Accessibility Act (EAA), Directive (EU) 2019/882, applies to e-commerce services offered to consumers since 28 June 2025. Article 13 and Annex V require service providers to publish information on how the service meets the accessibility requirements, in their general terms and conditions or an equivalent document, and to make it available to the public in an accessible form. Member states have written this into national law.
- **Germany:** the Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz (BFSG) implements the EAA. Its Annex 3 lists the information: a general description of the service in an accessible format, explanations needed to understand it, how it meets the requirements, and the competent market surveillance authority. For most services that authority is the MLBF, the joint authority of the German states in Magdeburg.
- **Brazil:** the Brazilian Inclusion Law (Lei Brasileira de Inclusão, LBI), Law 13.146/2015, Article 63, requires accessible websites for companies with headquarters or commercial representation in Brazil, and its paragraph 1 requires the accessibility symbol to be shown prominently.
- **Ontario (Canada):** the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act (AODA) and its Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation require large organizations, those with 50 or more employees in Ontario, to publish their accessibility policies and post their multi-year accessibility plan on their website.

The report lists every law with a link to its official text.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A statement is mainly for people, but it is useful to agents too. It is a plain-language page about how the site works, which parts are known to be hard to use and which alternatives exist, such as ordering by phone or email. An agent helping a person with a disability can read it to decide whether to continue on the site or use the alternative.

The statement also signals that someone has looked at the accessibility tree, the structure of names, roles and labels that screen readers and browsing agents read. OpenAI's [publishers and developers FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq) says the agent in its Atlas browser uses the same labels and roles that support screen readers to understand a page. The statement itself does not prove that work was done; the [static accessibility basics check](/knowledge-base/static-accessibility-basics) looks at a few of those signals directly.

## How the scanner decides

- **Found:** the home page links a page whose label or path names accessibility in the page language (accessibility, Barrierefreiheit, accessibilité, accesibilidad, acessibilidade, toegankelijkheid, accessibilità), or, for Brazil, shows the accessibility symbol.
- **Not found in the pages read:** no such link or symbol on the home page.

The check runs only for stores that appear to sell to the EU, Brazil or Canada. Elsewhere it is reported as not applicable.

What it cannot see:

- Whether the statement's content is complete or true.
- Whether the information is placed in the terms and conditions instead of a separate page. The EAA allows that, but this check only finds a linked page.
- Whether your business is a microenterprise, which is exempt from the EAA for services.
- Whether the site is actually accessible.

## How to fix it

Write the statement from a real review of your store, publish it on one page, and link it from the shared footer so it appears on every page.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Create a page under Pages, Add New, titled "Accessibility statement" (or "Erklärung zur Barrierefreiheit" for a German shop).
- Add it to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus, or to the footer template part in the site editor of a block theme.
- Legal-text services and plugins for the German market offer statement templates. Fill them with facts from your own review, not generic claims.

### Shopify

- Create the page under Online Store, Pages, and add it to the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation.
- Shopify's own themes are built with accessibility in mind, but apps, custom sections and your content are yours to review. List known issues honestly.

### Any other stack

- Serve the page at a stable path such as `/accessibility` or `/barrierefreiheit`, and link it from the footer in the server-rendered HTML.
- Use a link label in the page language that names accessibility. An icon alone is not enough, except the symbol Brazil requires, which should still carry a text alternative.
- Include a contact for reporting barriers and the date of the last review.

## Verify it yourself

Look for an accessibility link on your home page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oiE "href=\"[^\"]*(accessib|barrierefrei|accesib|acessib|toegankelijk)[^\"]*\"" | sort -u
```

Open the linked page and check that it describes your store, names a contact and shows a date.

## Common mistakes

- **A statement copied from another site.** It describes someone else's barriers and contact details.
- **Claiming full conformance without an audit.** A statement should state known limitations; "100% accessible" is rarely true.
- **Linking only from the sitemap or a help centre.** Put the link in the footer, where people and agents look.
- **Using an overlay's generated statement.** An [accessibility overlay](/knowledge-base/accessibility-overlays) does not make a site conformant, so a statement based on it misinforms.
- **Forgetting the market surveillance authority.** Germany's BFSG asks you to name it.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

In the EU, the EAA covers e-commerce services offered to consumers, but microenterprises providing services are exempt: businesses with fewer than 10 persons and an annual turnover or balance sheet total of no more than EUR 2 million. In Brazil, Article 63 of the LBI covers websites of companies headquartered or represented in Brazil. In Ontario, the website duties apply to organizations with 50 or more employees. The scan cannot tell your size or status; ask a lawyer.

### Is the statement the same as the information required by the BFSG?

German law speaks of information on how the service meets the accessibility requirements, not of a "statement". In practice most shops publish it as an accessibility statement page. The law also allows it in the terms and conditions, which this check does not read.

### Does a found statement mean my store is accessible?

No. It shows that a statement is linked. Accessibility depends on the site itself, which needs testing with people and tools.

### Which standard should the statement refer to?

In the EU, the usual reference is EN 301 549, whose web requirements match the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA. Brazil has its own standard, ABNT NBR 17225:2025, aligned with WCAG. Ask an accessibility specialist which applies to you.
