## In short

Many products have no GTIN (Global Trade Item Number): handmade goods, custom work, private-label items. For those, Google documents brand plus MPN (manufacturer part number) as the identifier. This check looks only at sampled products without a GTIN and passes when each of them carries both `brand` and `mpn` in its Product JSON-LD.

## What this check looks at

An MPN is the part or model number the manufacturer gives a product, such as `EC-MUG-350`. It is not a global number like a GTIN. It is unique only within one brand. That is why it always travels with the brand: "Example Ceramics EC-MUG-350" identifies one product, while "EC-MUG-350" alone could be anything.

In the Product JSON-LD, the block of structured data that describes the product to machines, the two properties are `mpn` and `brand`. For a product sold in variants, the MPN often sits on each variant under `hasVariant`, and the scanner reads it there too.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Agents compare offers by matching identifiers. Without a GTIN, the next best key is the brand and the maker's own part number. With it, a system can tell your "Speckled mug, large" apart from your "Speckled mug, small", and can match your product with the same item sold elsewhere if you are not the only seller.

Google Merchant Center states that an MPN is required for all products without a manufacturer-assigned GTIN. It also says not to invent one unless you are the manufacturer. If you make the product yourself, you are the manufacturer, and your own model number is the MPN.

This check carries weight 2, which means expected. It also plays a special role in the Level 3 gate, "Identifiable products". That level requires a GTIN or this fallback. A store whose products have no GTINs can still reach Level 3 when this check passes, because it stands in for both the [GTIN presence check](/knowledge/gtin-present) and the [GTIN validity check](/knowledge/gtin-valid).

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reuses the GTIN reading of the presence check. It then takes the sampled products that have no value shaped like a GTIN and reads their Product JSON-LD. For each one it looks for an `mpn` on the Product or its first variant that has one, and for a brand name, either as `brand.name` or a bare string.

- **Pass:** every product without a GTIN carries both an MPN and a brand.
- **Warn:** at least one carries only one of the two, and none carries neither. The message says how many have a brand but no MPN, and how many have an MPN but no brand.
- **Fail:** at least one product without a GTIN carries neither an MPN nor a brand.
- **Skip (not applicable):** every sampled product has a GTIN. The fallback is not needed.
- **Skip (dependency):** the products without a GTIN have no Product JSON-LD at all. That is the [Product JSON-LD check](/knowledge/product-json-ld)'s finding.

Products that do have a GTIN are not judged here, even if they lack an MPN.

## How to fix it

For each product without a GTIN, assign an MPN and make sure a brand is set. If you are the maker, create a simple, stable numbering scheme and keep it. If you resell, take the MPN from the manufacturer's catalog or data sheet.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WooCommerce has no dedicated MPN field in core. Many SEO and product feed plugins add one and output it as `mpn` in the structured data. Use the field your plugin provides and confirm it appears in the page source.
- Without such a plugin, store the MPN in a custom field and add it to the Product JSON-LD through the `woocommerce_structured_data_product` filter.
- Set the brand with the Brands taxonomy under Products, Brands, so the brand half is in place too.

### Shopify

- Shopify has no standard MPN field. A common approach is a product or variant metafield, for example `custom.mpn`, defined under Settings, Custom data.
- Output it in the theme's product JSON-LD as `mpn`. Keep the Vendor field filled, since most themes use it as the brand.
- Do not repurpose the SKU field as the MPN unless your SKUs really are the manufacturer's part numbers.

### Any other stack

- Add an `mpn` field to your product model, per variant where variants differ.
- Emit `mpn` and `brand` together in the Product JSON-LD and in any product feed you publish.
- Leave the GTIN properties out for these products. An empty or placeholder GTIN is worse than none.

## Verify it yourself

Check a product without a GTIN for both properties:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/product/handmade-mug/ | grep -oE '"(mpn|brand)"\s*:\s*(\{[^}]*\}|"[^"]*")'
```

You should see one `mpn` and one `brand`. Google's Rich Results Test shows both in the parsed Product. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **MPN without brand.** A part number alone is not unique. Both must be present.
- **Inventing an MPN for a product someone else makes.** Google advises against it. Use the maker's number or leave it out.
- **Placeholder GTINs.** Values like `0000000000000` or "N/A" in a GTIN field do not help and may fail validation. Leave the GTIN out and rely on brand plus MPN.
- **MPN on the parent only.** If variants have different part numbers, put each one on its variant.

## FAQ

### What is an MPN?

A manufacturer part number. It is the model or part number the maker assigns to a product. Unlike a GTIN it is only unique within one brand, so it is always used together with the brand.

### I make my products myself. Can I create my own MPNs?

Yes. As the manufacturer, your own model numbers are the MPN. Keep them stable and unique per product and variant.

### Do I need an MPN if my products have GTINs?

Not for this check, which only looks at products without a GTIN. Google still recommends submitting other unique identifiers where they exist.

### What about one-of-a-kind items with no part number?

Google lists custom-made items such as art and handmade goods as cases where an MPN is optional. The scanner cannot tell those apart from other products, so it will still report the gap. Brand plus a stable item number of your own satisfies the check.
