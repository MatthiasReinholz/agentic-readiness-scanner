## In short

Webflow's Site settings has two Traffic control switches, one for search engine crawlers and one for AI bots. Switching either off writes rules into robots.txt, the file that tells crawlers what they may fetch, that disallow a whole group of bots. This check fails when either group is off, because the search group feeds search and the AI answers built on it, and the AI group includes the fetchers that read pages for ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity. It runs only on Webflow sites.

## What this check looks at

The switches live under Site settings, SEO, Indexing, Traffic control. Both groups are allowed by default. Each switch covers a group of user agents that Webflow documents:

| Group                  | User agents                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Search engine crawlers | YandexBot, Googlebot, Bingbot, Baiduspider, OAI-SearchBot, Sogou, DuckDuckBot, MojeekBot, SeznamBot, Qwantify, Yeti, Slurp, 360Spider                                                                                    |
| AI bots                | meta-externalagent, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, GPTBot, GoogleOther, Timpibot, SBIntuitionsBot, Bytespider, AI2Bot, Amazon Q, Claude-User, CCBot, Diffbot, YandexGPT, MistralBot, cohere-ai, youBot, NeevaAI |

Switching a group off writes rules for all of its agents into robots.txt. The change takes effect when you publish the site.

The same panel offers two finer tools. You can add custom robots.txt rules. You can also set a `Content-Signal` line, such as `Content-Signal: ai-train=no, search=yes, ai-input=no`, which is based on a proposed extension to RFC 9309, the Robots Exclusion Protocol. See [usage preference signals](/knowledge-base/usage-preference-signals) for how such signals work.

## Why it matters for AI agents

The search group holds Googlebot and Bingbot. Their indexes feed Google Search and Bing, and the AI answers built on them. It also holds OAI-SearchBot, which builds the index for ChatGPT search. With the group off, the site drops out of all of them.

The AI bots group mixes two kinds of agent. Some are training crawlers, such as GPTBot, ClaudeBot and CCBot. Others fetch pages to answer people: ChatGPT-User and Claude-User open a page when a person asks about it, and PerplexityBot builds Perplexity's answers. Switching the group off to refuse training also shuts out those fetchers. See [user-triggered fetchers](/knowledge-base/user-triggered-fetchers-allowed) for more on them.

## How the scanner decides

This check runs only on Webflow sites, when the scan recognises the platform (see [platform detection](/knowledge-base/ecommerce-platform-detection)). On any other platform it is skipped and left out of the report. It makes no extra request.

The scanner evaluates the robots.txt it already fetched for every agent in both groups, at the root path `/`. A group counts as switched off when at least 60% of it is disallowed: 8 of the 13 search engine crawlers, or 12 of the 19 AI bots.

- **Fail:** the search engine crawlers group is off, the AI bots group is off, or both. When only the AI group is off, the message names the agents in it that feed AI answers.
- **Pass:** both groups are allowed, which is Webflow's default.
- **Not applicable:** robots.txt could not be read, so the setting is not visible.

The evidence lists the disallowed agents of each group.

The weight is 1, which means nice to have. It is not a gate for any level.

## How to fix it

Turn the groups back on. If you want to refuse training only, use the finer tools in the same panel.

### Webflow

1. Open Site settings, then SEO, then Indexing.
2. Under Traffic control, switch "Search engine crawlers" back on.
3. Switch "AI bots" back on, unless you have decided to keep AI assistants away entirely.
4. To refuse model training while staying in AI answers, add custom robots.txt rules for the training crawlers you mean, such as GPTBot, ClaudeBot and CCBot, or set a `Content-Signal` with `ai-train=no`.
5. Save and publish the site. The settings take effect only after publishing.

The "Staging indexing" setting is a different thing. Switched off, it publishes a blocking robots.txt only on the webflow.io subdomain, not on your own domain.

## Verify it yourself

Look for the key agents of each group in robots.txt:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt | grep -inE -A2 "googlebot|bingbot|oai-searchbot|chatgpt-user|claude-user|perplexitybot"
```

None of them should come with `Disallow: /`. Run the command against your own domain, not the webflow.io subdomain, which can have its own staging rules. Rescan after publishing to confirm.

## Common mistakes

- **Switching off AI bots to refuse training.** The group also holds ChatGPT-User, Claude-User and PerplexityBot. Custom rules or a Content-Signal refuse training without shutting them out.
- **Forgetting to publish.** Traffic control changes do nothing until the site is published.
- **Checking the webflow.io address.** Staging indexing can block the subdomain while your own domain stays open. Check the domain you scanned.
- **A blanket rule in custom robots.txt.** `User-agent: *` with `Disallow: /` disallows both groups as well. The check reads the result, so it reports both switches as off.
- **Assuming OAI-SearchBot is in the AI group.** Webflow lists it with the search engine crawlers, so the search switch decides ChatGPT search.

## FAQ

### What does Webflow's AI bots switch block?

It disallows 19 user agents in robots.txt. They include training crawlers such as GPTBot and CCBot and also the agents that read pages for ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity answers, such as ChatGPT-User, Claude-User and PerplexityBot.

### Are the Traffic control switches on by default?

Yes. Both groups are allowed by default. Robots.txt rules for a group appear only after you switch it off and publish.

### How do I block AI training on Webflow without leaving AI answers?

Keep both switches on. Add custom robots.txt rules for the training crawlers you want to refuse, or set a `Content-Signal` line with `ai-train=no` in the same panel, then publish.

### Does the staging indexing setting affect my live domain?

No. Switched off, it publishes a blocking robots.txt only on the webflow.io subdomain. This check reads the robots.txt of the address you scanned.
