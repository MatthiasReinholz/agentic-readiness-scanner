## In short

Switzerland's Price Indication Ordinance (PBV) asks shops to show consumers the price they actually pay, in Swiss francs, with VAT and other non-optional charges included. For a store that appears to sell to Switzerland, this check looks for CHF prices on product pages and for a statement that VAT is included, such as "inkl. MWST". It cannot see prices that appear only after a currency switch, a geolocation redirect or at checkout.

## What this check looks at

This check concerns one market, Switzerland.

- **The rule.** The Price Indication Ordinance (Preisbekanntgabeverordnung, PBV, SR 942.211) implements the price indication duty of the Federal Act against Unfair Competition (UWG). Its Article 3 requires goods offered to consumers to show the price actually payable in Swiss francs, the retail price. Its Article 4 requires passed-on public charges, such as VAT and advance recycling fees, and other non-optional surcharges to be included in that price.
- **Who supervises it.** The State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) oversees the ordinance, and the cantons enforce it. SECO publishes guidance and answers to common questions on its website.
- **The statement.** The ordinance asks for VAT to be included. A visible note such as "inkl. MWST", "TVA incluse" or "IVA inclusa" is how a reader, and this scanner, can tell that it is.

The report lists the ordinance with a link to its official text on Fedlex. A related check, [prior lowest price on discounts](/knowledge-base/prior-lowest-price-discounts), also cites the PBV for reduced prices.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Agents compare prices across stores, countries and currencies. To compare a Swiss shop with a German one, an agent needs to know three things: the amount, the currency and whether tax is included. A price in euros forces it to convert at a rate it has to guess. A CHF price without a VAT statement leaves it unsure whether 8.1 % Swiss VAT still comes on top. Either way, the comparison it gives a shopper may be wrong, and the shop that looked cheaper may not be.

Put the same facts in your markup. An `Offer` with `priceCurrency` set to `CHF`, and a `priceSpecification` with `valueAddedTaxIncluded` set to `true`, states in structured form what the visible note states in words. The [offer price, currency and availability check](/knowledge-base/offer-price-currency-availability) covers the rest of the Offer.

## How the scanner decides

The check runs only when the store appears to sell to Switzerland. The scanner infers that from public signals, such as a `.ch` domain, CHF prices, Swiss language variants in `hreflang`, a Swiss address or a Swiss shipping destination in the markup, or from the markets you set for the scan.

On the sampled product pages:

- **Found:** offers are priced in CHF and the page states that VAT is included, for example "inkl. MWST", "TVA incluse", "IVA inclusa" or "incl. VAT".
- **Review:** prices are in CHF, but no VAT statement was found.
- **Not found in the pages read:** no CHF price is shown.
- **Not applicable:** no sign that the store sells to Switzerland, and no market override.

What the scanner cannot see:

- Prices that switch to CHF only through a currency selector, a cookie or a geolocation redirect. The scan reads the pages from one location without running JavaScript.
- Shipping costs, duties and fees added in the cart or at checkout. The scanner never enters checkout.
- Whether the CHF amount really includes VAT and all non-optional charges.

The result is reported, never scored, and it is not legal advice.

## How to fix it

Serve Swiss visitors a version of the product page that shows the CHF price including VAT, with the note next to it, in the HTML the server sends.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- For a shop that sells mainly to Switzerland, set the currency to Swiss franc under WooCommerce, Settings, General.
- On the Tax tab, set the shop to display prices including tax, and enter a price display suffix such as "inkl. MWST" so the note appears next to every price.
- For a shop that sells in several currencies, use a multi-currency extension that renders CHF prices server-side for Swiss visitors or on a Swiss URL, not one that converts prices in the browser.

### Shopify

- Under Settings, Markets, add Switzerland as a market and set its currency to CHF. Review the converted CHF prices, or set fixed CHF prices if your plan offers it.
- Under Settings, Taxes and duties, turn on tax-inclusive prices for the Swiss market.
- Many themes print a "Taxes included" line under the price when prices include tax. Translate it for each language, for example "inkl. MWST" in German.
- If Switzerland has its own domain or subfolder, such as `/de-ch/`, scan that URL, because that is where the CHF prices appear.

### Any other stack

- Render the CHF price and the VAT note server-side for the Swiss storefront or URL.
- Output `priceCurrency` `CHF` in the Offer markup, with the same amount as the visible price.
- Give each country its own URL rather than switching currency by cookie or IP address, so people, agents and scanners all see the same page.

## Verify it yourself

Fetch a product page of the Swiss storefront and look for the currency and the VAT note:

```bash
curl -s https://example.ch/products/example | grep -oE "\"priceCurrency\"[^,}]*"
curl -s https://example.ch/products/example | grep -ioE "CHF[^<]{0,20}|inkl\.? ?mwst|tva (comprise|incluse)|iva inclusa|incl\.? ?vat"
```

A `priceCurrency` other than `CHF`, or no VAT note, is what the scanner reports. Rescan the URL that Swiss visitors actually land on.

## Common mistakes

- **CHF only after a click.** A currency switcher that converts prices in the browser leaves the server-rendered page in another currency.
- **Prices net of VAT.** Business-style prices plus VAT are not the price a consumer pays.
- **The note in one language only.** A French-language Swiss storefront with "inkl. MWST" or no note at all.
- **Markup in EUR, page in CHF.** The structured data must match the visible price.
- **Surcharges at checkout.** Non-optional fees that appear only in the cart belong in the displayed price.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

The ordinance concerns offers of goods and services to consumers in Switzerland. The check runs when the scan finds signs that the store sells there, or when you set Switzerland as a market. Whether the ordinance applies to your business and how is a question for a qualified person.

### Which VAT rate has to be included?

Swiss VAT has three rates since 1 January 2024: 8.1 % standard, 2.6 % reduced, for example for most food and books, and 3.8 % for accommodation. The displayed price includes the rate that applies to the product.

### Can I also show prices in euros?

This check only looks for a CHF price and the VAT note. It does not flag other currencies shown alongside them.

### I have a Swiss storefront. Why does the scan say not found?

The scanner probably read a different storefront. It fetches pages from one location without cookies or JavaScript, so a geolocation redirect or a currency selector may show it another currency. Scan the Swiss domain or subfolder directly.
