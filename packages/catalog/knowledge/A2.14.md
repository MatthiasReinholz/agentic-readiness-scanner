## In short

robots.txt is a request crawlers read. A CDN or bot manager in front of your site can enforce its own rules and refuse GPTBot, ChatGPT-User or Claude-SearchBot even where robots.txt welcomes them. Those rules match the crawler's name, and the scanner never pretends to be another crawler, so it cannot see them. This check names the vendor it recognises and tells you where to look.

## What this check looks at

The check reads two things the scan already has:

- **The home page's response headers.** Many CDNs and bot managers add a header of their own: Cloudflare's bot cookie `__cf_bm` or its network error reporting, `x-vercel-id` for Vercel, `x-amz-cf-id` for Amazon CloudFront, `x-datadome` for DataDome, `x-iinfo` for Imperva, and similar ones for Akamai and Fastly.
- **robots.txt.** When Cloudflare's AI crawler policies are on with its managed robots.txt, Cloudflare puts a block of its own in front of your file, between `# BEGIN Cloudflare Managed content` and `# END Cloudflare Managed Content`.

When the scan recognised a hosted platform such as Shopify, Wix, Squarespace, Webflow, BigCommerce or Square Online, the platform runs the edge, and the check names it instead.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Bot managers now sort AI traffic into training crawlers, search crawlers and agents that act for a person, and let the owner block each kind with a switch. Cloudflare's AI Crawler and Agent access policies, Vercel's AI Bots ruleset and AWS WAF's CategoryAI rule all work like this. They match the user agent, and Cloudflare documents that its policies also apply to unverified clients that send a matching name.

So a store can tell every crawler "welcome" in robots.txt and still answer GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot or ChatGPT-User with a block page. ChatGPT search, Claude and Perplexity then lose the store, and nothing in the page source shows why.

The scanner cannot test this from outside without sending another company's user agent, which it never does. A request that claims to be GPTBot from the scanner's address would also show up in your own AI crawler analytics as a GPTBot visit, which is a reason in itself not to send one. [Why the scanner reports crawler blocks instead of working around them](/knowledge-base/guides/crawler-blocks-and-browser-scans) explains the rule.

## How the scanner decides

- **Warn:** robots.txt carries Cloudflare's managed block. The site uses Cloudflare's AI crawler policies, which can also refuse search crawlers and assistants' fetchers at the edge. The message lists what the block disallows and where to check the rest.
- **Pass, naming the vendor:** a CDN or bot manager was recognised. The message says where its AI crawler rules are set.
- **Pass, naming the platform:** a hosted platform runs the edge. robots.txt is what you control there.
- **Pass:** no vendor was recognised. One can still be present without a visible sign.

The check is reported, never scored. It cannot know your settings, so it never claims a block, and it never claims there is none.

## How to fix it

Make the edge agree with robots.txt. First decide which AI clients you want: the crawlers behind AI answers, the assistants' fetchers that act when a person asks, and a training decision. Then open the vendor's setting and match it.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Check your host's CDN or firewall panel and any security plugin with a "block AI bots" or "bad bots" switch. Such plugins can block by user agent in PHP before the page renders.

### Shopify

- Shopify runs the CDN in front of every store and handles bot management itself. You control crawlers through `robots.txt.liquid` only.

### Any other stack

- **Cloudflare:** Security, Settings, AI Crawler and Agent access policies. The Crawlers list in AI Crawl Control shows each AI crawler and whether it is allowed. Signed agents such as ChatGPT's cloud browser need an allow policy.
- **Vercel:** Firewall, the AI Bots managed ruleset. It allows AI bots until AI Crawlers or AI Assistants is set to Deny.
- **AWS WAF:** Bot Control's CategoryAI rule blocks verified and unverified AI bots once the rule group is on. SignalNonBrowserUserAgent blocks honest non-browser clients.
- **Akamai, Fastly, DataDome, Imperva:** each has AI bot categories or signals. Look for rules that deny them.

## Verify it yourself

Only you can see your edge rules. Three ways to check them:

1. Open the vendor's security events or bot analytics and filter by GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-User or PerplexityBot. Served requests are fine; challenges and blocks are what to fix.
2. Compare your server logs with the IP lists the operators publish, such as `https://openai.com/searchbot.json`, `https://claude.com/crawling/bots.json` and `https://www.perplexity.ai/perplexitybot.json`. The [crawler registry](/knowledge-base/crawlers) links each list.
3. Run Google Search Console's URL Inspection live test and Bing Webmaster Tools' URL Inspection. They fetch as Google and Bing, from their own addresses.

## Common mistakes

- **Turning on "block AI bots" to stop training.** Some switches block search crawlers and assistants' fetchers too, which removes the store from AI answers. Choose per category.
- **Trusting robots.txt alone.** A firewall rule wins over robots.txt every time, and it leaves no trace in the file.
- **Allowing by user agent only.** A rule that trusts the name `GPTBot` lets in anyone who copies it. Use the vendor's verified-bot categories or the operators' IP lists.

## FAQ

### Can the scanner test whether GPTBot gets through?

No. It would have to send GPTBot's name from its own address. That is impersonation, it would show up in your analytics as a real GPTBot visit, and bot managers that verify crawlers by IP address would treat it as a fake. The scanner reports what it can see and tells you where to look.

### Does Cloudflare block AI crawlers by default?

Cloudflare has changed its defaults for new domains more than once, and its documentation has not always been consistent. Check the AI Crawler and Agent access policies of your own zone rather than relying on a default.

### My site is on Shopify. Is there anything to set?

Shopify runs the CDN in front of every store and handles bot management itself. robots.txt, through `robots.txt.liquid`, is what you control.

### Why is this check never scored?

It cannot see the setting it is about. Scoring a guess would make the report less true, so it informs and points to the owner's own tools.
