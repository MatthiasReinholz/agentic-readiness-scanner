## In short

When something goes wrong with an order, the buyer, or the agent acting for them, needs a way to reach the seller. Merchant programmes require one. This check passes when your home page links an email address, a phone number or a contact page on your own site.

## What this check looks at

The scanner reads every link on the home page as the server delivers it and looks for three kinds of contact channel:

- a `mailto:` link to an email address
- a `tel:` link to a phone number
- a link to a contact, support, help, FAQ or customer service page.

For the third kind, the words must stand on their own. A short label of up to three words made of contact words, such as "Contact us", "Help", "Kundenservice" or "Customer service", qualifies. So does a path whose last segment is a contact word, such as `/pages/contact` or `/kontakt`. Longer labels like "Find the right support for your back" do not, and product, category, account and cart URLs never count, whatever their slug says.

A page on the store's own site counts, including its subdomains such as `support.example.com`. A link to a different site, such as a help desk on a third-party domain, is noted but counts for less.

## Why it matters for AI agents

An agent that is about to spend a shopper's money is more confident when the merchant can be reached. A contact channel tells the agent, and the shopper behind it, that someone answers if a parcel is lost or a size is wrong. It also lets an agent pass a question on that it cannot answer from the page.

Google Merchant Center's checkout requirements say you must give customers a way to contact you on your website. A contact form, an email address, a phone number or a link to a business social media profile all qualify there. UCP (Universal Commerce Protocol, an open standard for agents to discover and buy from stores) onboarding also asks for at least one contact method.

This check carries weight 2, which means expected. It is part of the Level 4 gate, "Trusted merchant", so a store cannot reach Level 4 until it passes. A warning does not satisfy the gate.

## How the scanner decides

The check uses only the home page the scanner already fetched. It makes no extra requests.

- **Pass:** the home page links at least one `mailto:` address, one `tel:` number, or one contact page on the store's own site. The message names the page or counts the addresses.
- **Warn:** the only contact link points to another site.
- **Fail:** the home page has no `mailto:`, `tel:` or qualifying contact page link.

The scanner does not open the contact page or test a form. It checks that a channel is linked where people and agents look for it. A footer built in the browser by JavaScript is invisible to this check, because the links are read from the server-rendered HTML.

## How to fix it

Add a contact link to the footer of every page. An email address and a contact page together cover the most cases. Use a plain label.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Create a Contact page under Pages with your email, phone number or a contact form.
- Add it to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus, or to the footer template part in the Site Editor for block themes.
- If you show your email address, make it a real link: `mailto:hello@example.com`. Many themes print it as plain text, which the check cannot use.

### Shopify

- Shopify themes include a contact page template. Create a page under Online Store, Pages, and choose the contact template.
- Add it to the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation. A link with the label "Contact" and a path such as `/pages/contact` qualifies.
- If your help centre runs on a third-party domain, link your own contact page as well, so the check finds a same-site channel.

### Any other stack

- Put a `mailto:` link, a `tel:` link or a link to `/contact` in the footer of the server-rendered HTML.
- Keep the label short: "Contact", "Contact us", "Help" or "Customer service".
- Make the contact page itself server-rendered so agents can read the details on it.

## Verify it yourself

List the contact links on your home page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oiE 'href="(mailto:|tel:)[^"]*"|href="[^"]*(contact|kontakt|support|help|faq)[^"]*"' | sort -u
```

If nothing prints, the server-rendered home page has no contact link the check can use. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **An email address as plain text.** Without `mailto:` it is not a link, and the check does not see it.
- **A chat widget only.** Chat bubbles load by JavaScript and are not links in the HTML. Add a contact page too.
- **Contact behind the account area.** A support form under `/my-account/` needs a login, so the scanner ignores it.
- **A long, clever label.** "We'd love to hear from you" is friendly but does not match. Use "Contact" in the label or the path.
- **Help desk on another domain only.** That warns. Add a same-site contact page.

## FAQ

### Does a contact form count?

Yes, if it sits on a page linked from the home page with a label or path such as "Contact" or `/contact`. The scanner does not test the form itself.

### Is an email link enough to pass?

Yes. A single `mailto:` link on the home page passes. Merchant programmes may ask for more, so a contact page with several channels is a good idea.

### Why does my help centre on another domain only warn?

A link to another site does not show that the store itself offers a channel. Links to the store's own subdomains count fully. Add a contact page on your site to pass.

### Does a social media link count as a contact channel?

Not for this check. Google Merchant Center accepts a business social media profile, but the scanner looks for email, phone or a contact page on your own site.
