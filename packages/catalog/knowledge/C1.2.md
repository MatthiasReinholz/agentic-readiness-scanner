## In short

Several markets ask an online seller to publish the numbers that identify it in public registers: a VAT ID (value added tax number) in the EU, a company number and registered office in the UK, a CNPJ (company tax number) in Brazil, a RUT in Chile, a NIT in Colombia. This check looks for the identifiers of each market your store appears to sell to. Where a number has a check digit (a last digit computed from the others to catch typos), the scanner validates it. It cannot confirm that a number is registered to you.

## What this check looks at

The numbers asked for depend on the market:

- **European Union.** The [E-Commerce Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/31/oj), Article 5(1), asks for the trade register and registration number where the provider is registered in one (point d) and the VAT identification number where the activity is subject to VAT (point g). In Germany, the Digital Services Act (DDG), §5, asks for the register, the register number and the VAT ID or business identification number.
- **United Kingdom.** The [Company, LLP and Business (Names and Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/17), regulations 24 and 25, ask every company to show on its websites its registered name, the part of the UK where it is registered (for example England and Wales), its registered number and its registered office address. The Electronic Commerce Regulations 2002, regulation 6, add the VAT number for VAT-registered providers.
- **Brazil.** [Decreto 7.962/2013](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2011-2014/2013/decreto/d7962.htm), Article 2(I), asks for the business name and the CNPJ number, or the CPF of an individual seller.
- **Chile.** The e-commerce regulation under the Consumer Protection Act (Ley 19.496), Supreme Decree 6 of 2021 in force since March 2022, asks sellers to give their name or company name, RUT, address and contact means.
- **Colombia.** The Consumer Statute (Ley 1480 de 2011), Article 50(a), asks e-commerce sellers to show their name or company name, NIT, address for legal notices, telephone and email.
- **Switzerland, Argentina and Peru.** The scanner also recognises a Swiss UID (`CHE-123.456.789`), an Argentine CUIT and a Peruvian RUC, and validates them when present.

The names, addresses and email addresses that go with these numbers are covered by the [seller identity check](/knowledge-base/seller-identity-imprint). The page lists every law this check is tagged with.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A registration or tax number is the most reliable key to a business. Shopping agents and answer engines that want to know whether a store is real can look the number up in a public register, such as VIES for EU VAT IDs, Companies House in the UK or the Receita Federal in Brazil, and compare the result with the name on the site. A matching number is a strong trust signal. A missing or malformed one is a reason for caution.

Machine-readable markup makes the lookup direct. schema.org Organization has `vatID` for the VAT ID, `taxID` for a national tax number such as a CNPJ or RUT, and `identifier` for other numbers such as a company register entry. An agent reading those properties does not need to parse an imprint written in another language.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner reads the imprint, contact and terms pages it found in the home page links, and the home page. When the home page links no terms page, a sitemap URL that names the terms is used instead. For each market the store appears to sell to, it looks for that market's identifiers:

| Market | Identifier                                                                       | Validation             |
| ------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| EU     | VAT ID with country prefix, usually after a label such as VAT, USt-IdNr. or TVA  | Format for the country |
| UK     | Company number after wording such as "company number" or "registered in England" | Format                 |
| CH     | UID, `CHE-123.456.789`                                                           | Check digit (mod 11)   |
| BR     | CNPJ                                                                             | Two check digits       |
| CL     | RUT                                                                              | Check digit (mod 11)   |
| CO     | NIT                                                                              | DIAN check digit       |
| AR     | CUIT                                                                             | Check digit (mod 11)   |
| PE     | RUC                                                                              | Check digit (mod 11)   |

The verdicts:

- **Found:** every detected market that asks for a number has a valid one.
- **Review:** a number is present but fails its check digit, or only some markets are covered.
- **Not found in the pages read:** no number is present.

The check runs only for markets the store appears to target, detected from signals such as the domain, the page language, prices and addresses. When no such market is detected, it is reported as not applicable. A valid check digit only shows that a number is well formed. It does not show that the number is registered, or registered to this business. Only the official register can confirm that. For the UK, the scanner looks for the company number only: it does not check the registered office or the part of the UK, which the rules also ask for. Numbers shown only in images, PDFs or after JavaScript runs are not seen. This check is reported, never scored.

## How to fix it

Find out which numbers your business has and which ones the markets you sell to ask for, then publish them as text on your imprint or legal-notice page and in your Organization markup. Copy the number from the official document, not from memory. Have the page reviewed by a lawyer or tax adviser for the markets you sell to.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Add the numbers to your legal-notice page as plain text, with a label: "VAT ID: DE123456789", "Registered in England and Wales, company number 01234567".
- If your SEO plugin's organisation settings have fields for a VAT ID or tax ID, fill them in so the Organization JSON-LD carries them. Otherwise add a small JSON-LD block to the theme or through a code-snippet plugin.
- If your invoices or order emails already show the VAT ID, use the same value.

### Shopify

- Enter the numbers in Settings, Policies, Contact information, which has a place for regulatory details such as VAT and trade register numbers, and link that page from the footer.
- Add `vatID` or `taxID` to the theme's Organization JSON-LD, usually in the layout file or a snippet the layout includes.
- UK companies: add the registered office and "Registered in England and Wales" (or Scotland, or Northern Ireland) next to the company number.

### Any other stack

- Print the numbers in the footer or on the identity page in the server-rendered HTML, each with a plain label.
- Output `vatID`, `taxID` and, for register entries, `identifier` as a `PropertyValue` on the Organization node.
- Keep the format the register uses, including the country prefix for EU VAT IDs and the punctuation for CNPJ and Swiss UID.

## Verify it yourself

Look for VAT IDs and Brazilian CNPJs on your identity page:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/imprint | sed -e 's/<[^>]*>/ /g' | grep -oE '\b(ATU[0-9]{8}|[A-Z]{2}[0-9]{8,12})\b|[0-9]{2}\.[0-9]{3}\.[0-9]{3}/[0-9]{4}-[0-9]{2}' | sort -u
```

Then confirm each number in the official register: VIES (ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies) for EU VAT IDs, Companies House for UK company numbers, the UID register (uid.admin.ch) for Swiss UIDs, and the Receita Federal CNPJ lookup for Brazil. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **A typo in the number.** One wrong digit makes a CNPJ, RUT or UID fail its check digit. Copy the number from the official document.
- **The EU VAT ID without its country prefix.** "123456789" is not a VAT ID, but "DE123456789" is.
- **The company number without the registered office.** UK rules ask for the number, the registered office address and the part of the UK where the company is registered, together.
- **The numbers only on invoices.** Several of the laws above ask for them on the website.
- **Different numbers in different places.** An old VAT ID in the footer and a new one in the imprint confuse people and agents alike.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

It depends on your business and where you sell. In the EU and UK, a VAT ID is asked for only from VAT-registered businesses, and a register number only from businesses entered in a register. The UK website disclosures apply to companies and LLPs (limited liability partnerships). In Brazil, Chile and Colombia, the rules above concern e-commerce sellers to consumers in those countries. The scanner runs this check only for markets it detects, and it cannot tell whether your business is registered or must be.

### Does a valid check digit mean the number is mine?

No. It only shows that the number is well formed. Anyone can copy a valid number. Agents and customers who want certainty look it up in the official register.

### Which schema.org property should I use?

Use `vatID` for a VAT ID and `taxID` for a national tax number such as a CNPJ, RUT, NIT, CUIT or RUC. Use `identifier` with a `PropertyValue` for a company register entry. All three sit on the Organization node.

### I sell from the UK to EU customers. Which numbers apply?

The UK company disclosures apply to UK companies wherever they sell. Whether you also need an EU VAT ID or other EU details depends on your registrations and how you sell, which is a question for a tax adviser. The scanner reports each detected market separately.
