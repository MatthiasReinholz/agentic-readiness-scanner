## In short

Crawlers discover products by following links, and they do not click buttons or scroll. A category page should list its products as ordinary links in the HTML and reach further pages through numbered page links. This check passes when the sampled listing page links to two or more products and either paginates with links or has no pagination at all.

## What this check looks at

A listing page is a category, collection or shop page that shows many products. The scanner picks one from the links on your home page: a URL with a listing segment such as `/collections/`, `/product-category/`, `/category/`, `/shop/` or `/c/`. It prefers a concrete category, such as `/collections/boots`, over a bare index, and among those the one linked most often. A product page is never chosen.

On that page, the scanner reads the server-rendered HTML and counts:

- **Product links:** anchors on the same origin whose path looks like a product URL, such as `/product/`, `/products/`, `/p/` or `/item/`.
- **Pagination links:** anchors whose URL names a page number, such as `/page/2/`, `?page=2`, `?p=2`, `?paged=2` or `?pg=2`, and a link element with `rel="next"` in the head.
- **Load-more controls:** buttons, inputs or links labelled "load more" or "show more", or whose class or id mentions load-more or infinite scrolling.
- **Category links:** links to further category pages, used to recognise a category index.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A sitemap lists your URLs, but crawlers also learn your catalog by walking your site. Links between listing pages and products tell them which products belong together and which are prominent.

Google's documentation on pagination is explicit: its crawlers follow links in `href` attributes and do not click buttons or trigger the scrolling that loads more items. Products that only appear after "load more" or an infinite scroll are invisible to such a crawler unless they are reachable some other way. Bing's guidelines likewise ask for standard anchor links for navigation. The crawlers behind AI answers build their indexes the same way.

The check carries weight 1, a nice to have. It is not a level gate, because a good [product sitemap](/knowledge-base/product-urls-in-sitemap) or a feed can make the catalog enumerable on its own. It is still the most natural path a crawler takes, so it is worth getting right.

## How the scanner decides

- **Pass:** the page links to two or more products, and it has pagination links or a `rel="next"` link.
- **Pass:** the page links to two or more products and shows no sign of pagination or a load-more control. A small category that fits on one page is fine.
- **Warn:** the page links to two or more products, has no pagination links, and has a load-more button or infinite-scroll control.
- **Warn:** the page links to exactly one product, too few to enumerate a catalog from.
- **Warn:** no product link is in the HTML. Either products are rendered by JavaScript, or their URLs do not look like product URLs to the scanner.
- **Warn:** the listing page could not be fetched or answered with an error status.
- **Skip:** no listing page was found on the home page, or the sampled page is a category index: it has no product links but three or more links to further category pages.

## How to fix it

Keep the visual design you like. Infinite scroll and load-more buttons are fine as an enhancement, as long as plain links exist underneath. Google recommends exactly that: give every page of results its own URL and link to it.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- WooCommerce category and shop pages paginate with links such as `/product-category/mugs/page/2/` by default. Most themes keep that.
- Infinite-scroll and "load more" features usually come from the theme or a plugin. Check that they replace the numbered links only visually, and that the links remain in the HTML.
- Product cards in WooCommerce templates link to the product with an anchor. Page builders sometimes turn cards into clickable boxes driven by JavaScript. Make sure each card still contains a real link.

### Shopify

- Shopify collections paginate with `?page=2` links when the theme uses Liquid's `paginate` tag, which most themes do.
- Some themes and apps switch collections to infinite scroll or a load-more button. Check the theme settings, and keep the pagination links in the template even if they are visually hidden.
- Filtering and sorting apps sometimes load products with JavaScript after the page renders. Fetch the collection with curl to see what a crawler sees.

### Any other stack

- Render the first page of every listing on the server, with each product as a link element with an `href` to the product URL.
- Give every page of results a unique URL, such as `?page=2`, and link the next and numbered pages from the page itself.
- Add a `rel="next"` link to the head when there is a next page. Google no longer uses it as an indexing signal, but it is a clear, standard hint.
- Implement "load more" as progressive enhancement on top of those links, and update the URL as more products load.

## Verify it yourself

Fetch a category page as a crawler sees it and look for product links and a page-2 link:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/collections/boots | grep -o 'href="[^"]*products/[^"]*"' | sort -u | head
curl -s https://example.com/collections/boots | grep -oE 'href="[^"]*(page=2|/page/2/)[^"]*"' | head -n 3
```

Replace the URL and the `products/` pattern with your own category page and product path. If the first command prints nothing, products are not in the HTML. If the second prints nothing on a category with many products, there is no crawlable next page.

## Common mistakes

- **Infinite scroll with no fallback.** The first page of products is visible to crawlers; every product after it is not.
- **Product cards without anchors.** A card that navigates with a click handler looks like a link to people and like nothing to a crawler.
- **Pagination with JavaScript links.** `href="#"` or `href="javascript:..."` with a click handler cannot be followed.
- **Product URLs the scanner cannot recognise.** If your product URLs have no product segment, such as `/blue-mug.html`, this check cannot count them and warns. The links still work for crawlers; the [sitemap check](/knowledge-base/product-urls-in-sitemap) is then the better measure.

## FAQ

### Is infinite scroll bad for SEO and AI search?

Only when it is the only way to reach more products. Google's crawlers do not scroll or click, so products loaded that way stay hidden unless numbered page links exist underneath.

### Does Google still use rel=next and rel=prev?

Google has said it no longer uses them as an indexing signal. They remain valid HTML and a clear hint for other crawlers, and the scanner counts `rel="next"` as pagination.

### Can I use a load-more button at all?

Yes, as an enhancement. Keep a real link to the next page in the HTML, for example inside the button's container, and let JavaScript take over for visitors.

### Why does the scanner say no listing page was found?

It looks for category or collection links on your home page. If your home page links to categories with unusual paths, or only through a JavaScript menu, the check is skipped.
