## In short

AI answers quote pages through the same snippet mechanism that search results use. A product page with `noindex`, `nosnippet` or `max-snippet:0` is excluded from Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, and some other directives limit Bing and Apple. This check reads the robots directives on sampled product pages and is a Level 2 gate.

## What this check looks at

Robots directives tell search engines what they may do with a page. They come in two places:

- **Meta tags in the HTML head.** The scanner reads the `robots`, `googlebot`, `bingbot` and `applebot` meta tags.
- **The X-Robots-Tag HTTP response header.** It carries the same directives and can be scoped to one crawler, as in `X-Robots-Tag: bingbot: noarchive`.

It also looks at two things in the page itself:

- **data-nosnippet.** An HTML attribute that tells Google not to use the text inside an element in a snippet. The scanner measures the largest element carrying it against the page's total word count.
- **isAccessibleForFree.** A property in structured data (JSON-LD) that marks content as paywalled when set to false.

Here is what each directive does, according to the vendors' own documentation:

| Directive                                                | Effect on AI answers                                                                                                                                   | Verdict       |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------- |
| `noindex`                                                | The page leaves the index that AI answers draw on                                                                                                      | Fail          |
| `nosnippet`, `max-snippet:0`                             | Google keeps the content out of AI Overviews and AI Mode; Apple generates no web answer from the page                                                  | Fail          |
| `data-nosnippet` around half the page or more            | The product content cannot be quoted                                                                                                                   | Fail          |
| `noarchive`, `nocache`                                   | Bing keeps the page out of Copilot answers or reduces it to URL, title and snippet, until 1 March 2027; Amazon reads `noarchive` as a training opt-out | Warn          |
| `ai-grounding:disallow`, `ai-grounding:disallowextended` | Bing's page-level opt-out of grounding, honoured from 1 February 2027                                                                                  | Warn          |
| `isAccessibleForFree: false`                             | Apple does not use the page as AI context                                                                                                              | Warn          |
| `noai`, `noimageai`                                      | No documented answer engine reads them                                                                                                                 | Recorded only |

## Why it matters for AI agents

An assistant answering "where can I buy this kettle" builds its reply from the indexed text of pages, cut into snippets. Google documents that pages need to be indexed and eligible to be shown with a snippet to appear in its AI features. Take away the snippet and the page has nothing for the answer to quote.

These directives often arrive by accident. A theme ships `nosnippet` to stop price scraping. A staging setting leaves `noindex` on after launch. A plugin wraps the product description in `data-nosnippet` to hide it from search. Each one quietly removes the product from AI answers while the page still looks fine in a browser.

The Bing directives are softer. They limit how Copilot uses a page instead of removing it from Bing. That is why they warn rather than fail.

## How the scanner decides

The check needs sampled product pages. When none were found, it is not applicable. It combines the header and the four meta tags of each page and looks for the directives above.

- **Fail:** any sampled product page carries `noindex`, `nosnippet` or `max-snippet:0`, or a single `data-nosnippet` element covers half or more of the page's words.
- **Warn:** no page fails, but at least one carries `noarchive`, `nocache` or a Bing `ai-grounding` directive, or its JSON-LD sets `isAccessibleForFree` to false.
- **Pass:** none of these is present.

`noai` and `noimageai` are listed in the evidence as declared preferences and never change the verdict. The evidence shows, per page, the header, the meta values, the `data-nosnippet` coverage and the paywall flag.

This check has weight 3, which means blocking. It is a gate for Level 2 and every level above it.

## How to fix it

Remove the directives from product pages unless you really want those products kept out of AI answers. Change them at the source, not page by page.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Under Settings, Reading, make sure "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" is unchecked. It adds `noindex` to every page.
- In your SEO plugin, check the default robots settings for the Product post type and for individual products. Yoast SEO and Rank Math both expose noindex and snippet options per content type.
- Search the theme and plugins for `nosnippet`, `max-snippet` and `data-nosnippet`. Remove them from product templates.
- Check server or caching plugin rules for an `X-Robots-Tag` header added to every response.

### Shopify

- If product pages carry `noindex`, look in `theme.liquid` and product templates for a robots meta tag added by the theme or an app.
- SEO apps can set `noindex` or `nosnippet` per product. Review their settings.
- Search the theme code for `data-nosnippet` wrapping the product description or price.

### Any other stack

- Find where the robots meta tag is rendered in your product template and remove the restrictive values.
- Check the web server, CDN and framework middleware for an `X-Robots-Tag` header. It is easy to forget because it is invisible in the HTML.
- Only set `isAccessibleForFree` to false on content that is actually behind a paywall.

## Verify it yourself

Check both places on a product page:

```bash
curl -sI https://example.com/products/example | grep -i "^x-robots-tag"
curl -s https://example.com/products/example | grep -ioE "name=\"(robots|googlebot|bingbot|applebot)\"[^>]*|data-nosnippet|isAccessibleForFree[^,}]*"
```

Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool also reports whether a page may be indexed. Rescan after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **Leaving the staging switch on.** One WordPress checkbox adds `noindex` to the whole store.
- **Using nosnippet to stop price scrapers.** It does not stop scrapers, and it removes the page from AI answers.
- **Fixing the meta tag and forgetting the header.** An `X-Robots-Tag` header applies even when the HTML is clean.
- **Wrapping the whole product area in data-nosnippet.** Use it for small pieces only, such as a review widget, if at all.
- **Expecting noai to work.** No documented answer engine reads it. It neither protects nor harms the page.

## FAQ

### Does nosnippet remove my page from Google AI Overviews?

Yes. Google documents that `nosnippet` and `max-snippet:0` keep a page's content out of AI Overviews and AI Mode. The page can still rank as a plain result, but it cannot be quoted.

### How do I stay in search but out of AI answers?

On Google you cannot, with snippet directives: Google documents the same preview controls for search results and its AI features. For training, use robots.txt training controls instead, which the [training-crawler policy check](/knowledge-base/ai-training-crawler-policy) covers.

### What does Bing's ai-grounding directive do?

It is a page-level opt-out from grounding Copilot answers, which Bing honours from 1 February 2027 when set under the Bingbot name. The scanner warns because it keeps the page out of those answers.

### Is data-nosnippet on a small element a problem?

No. The check only fails when one element carrying it covers half or more of the page text. Hiding a review widget or a footer is fine.
