## In short

"Can it be here by Friday, and what does delivery cost?" is one of the first questions a shopper asks, and an agent shopping for them asks it too. This check follows the shipping or delivery link on your home page and passes when the page names concrete terms: regions, costs or delivery times.

## What this check looks at

The scanner reads the home page as the server delivers it and ranks every link that could be the shipping policy. A link qualifies when its label or the last part of its path names the topic as a whole word, in several languages:

| Language | Words matched                                                                          |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| English  | shipping, delivery                                                                     |
| German   | Versand, Versandkosten, Versandinformationen, Lieferung, Lieferzeit, Lieferbedingungen |
| French   | livraison, expédition                                                                  |
| Italian  | spedizione, consegna                                                                   |
| Spanish  | envío, entrega                                                                         |

Whole words matter. "Versandkosten" counts, "Versandlager" (a warehouse) does not. The ranking is the same as for the return policy: a label made only of policy words beats one that carries a product name, a footer link beats a body link, and product, category and account URLs are never chosen. On Shopify, the platform's own page at `/policies/shipping-policy` is used when no link was found or the best link matched only weakly, for example by its path alone.

When the home page links no shipping page at all, the scanner looks in your sitemap. A URL on your own site whose last path segment names the policy document, such as `/shipping-policy`, is used instead. The page is still fetched and judged like a linked one.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Delivery cost and time often decide a purchase. An agent comparing offers needs them to compute the real price and to tell the shopper when the item arrives. A shipping page that says only "We ship fast!" gives it nothing to work with.

Shipping costs can also be described in structured data (machine-readable facts embedded in the page), through schema.org `OfferShippingDetails`, and in merchant feeds and account settings. The [shipping and return markup check](/knowledge-base/shipping-and-return-markup) covers the markup. The page stays the readable source that people, agents and reviewers check against.

Every checkout programme the scanner tracks asks merchants for their shipping terms. Google Merchant Center's [misrepresentation policy](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6150127) also counts not disclosing the total price, including fees, as dishonest pricing.

This check carries weight 3, which means blocking. It is part of the Level 3 gate, "Trusted merchant". A store cannot reach Level 3 until this check passes.

## How the scanner decides

The scanner fetches the linked page once and looks for signs of concrete terms in its text:

- an amount with a currency, such as `4.90 €`, `$5` or `12 EUR`
- a delivery time in days, such as `2-4 business days`, `3 Werktage` or `5 jours`
- a keyword such as free shipping, Versandkosten, versandkostenfrei, kostenlos, delivery time, Lieferzeit, worldwide, Europe or international.

The verdicts:

- **Pass:** the page answers HTTP 200, is not a product page, and contains at least one of those signs.
- **Warn:** the page answers but contains none of them.
- **Fail:** no qualifying link was found on the home page, the page did not answer HTTP 200, or the only matching link leads to a product page. A product page is recognised by Product JSON-LD (structured data that describes a product) or an `og:type` of product.
- **Skipped as inconclusive:** the page refused the scanner (for example 403 or a bot challenge), was rate limited, or failed temporarily (no answer, or 502, 503 or 504). A refusal says nothing about the policy, so it is never a fail and drops out of the score. The scanner still does not award Level 3 on a page it could not read. When nothing else stands in the way, the report says the level could not be decided rather than that it was missed.

The text test is a heuristic. It shows that the page states something concrete. It does not check that your rates are complete or correct. Links are read from the server-rendered HTML, so a footer built only by JavaScript is invisible to this check. When the whole home page is an empty shell that JavaScript fills in (fewer than 3 links and fewer than 50 words in the HTML), the failure message says so.

## How to fix it

Write a page that answers four questions: where you ship, what it costs, how long it takes, and which carriers you use. A small table per region is easy for people and machines to read. State currencies and units of time explicitly. Link the page from the footer of every page with a label such as "Shipping" or "Delivery".

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Your rates already live under WooCommerce, Settings, Shipping, in shipping zones. Use them as the source for the page so the two stay in step.
- Create a page under Pages, for example "Shipping and delivery", with a table of zones, costs and delivery times.
- Add it to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus, or in the footer template part of a block theme.

### Shopify

- Write the policy under Settings, Policies, Shipping policy. Shopify publishes it at `/policies/shipping-policy`.
- Copy the zones and rates from Settings, Shipping and delivery, and add delivery times in days.
- Link the page in the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation. Shopify marks policy pages as noindex by default, which the [agent-readable policy pages check](/knowledge-base/agent-readable-policy-pages) reports separately.

### Any other stack

- Publish the page at a stable URL such as `/shipping` or `/delivery`.
- Generate the cost table from the same configuration your checkout uses, so the page never drifts from the real rates.
- Link it from the `footer` of the server-rendered home page.

## Verify it yourself

Find the shipping link and check the page for concrete terms:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -oiE 'href="[^"]*"[^>]*>[^<]*(shipping|delivery|versand|livraison)[^<]*' | head
curl -s https://example.com/shipping/ | grep -oiE '[0-9]+([.,][0-9]{2})? ?(€|eur|usd|gbp|\$)|[0-9]+(-[0-9]+)? ?(business |working )?days' | head
```

If the second command prints nothing, the scanner will most likely warn. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **Prices in an image.** A rate table saved as a picture contains no text to read.
- **Rates only in the cart.** Many stores show shipping costs only at checkout. Agents and shoppers want them before that.
- **A vague promise.** "Fast, reliable shipping" without numbers warns.
- **A link to a shipping product.** A link to a "Shipping insurance" product page is not a policy. The scanner fails it on purpose.
- **Terms that contradict the feed.** If your page says free shipping over 50 euros and your feed says otherwise, systems that compare them may distrust both.

## FAQ

### What should a shipping policy page include?

Destinations, costs per region or order value, delivery times, carriers, and any thresholds for free shipping. Handling time before dispatch is worth stating too.

### Can I combine shipping and returns on one page?

Yes. A page called "Shipping and returns" can satisfy both this check and the return policy check, as long as it states concrete shipping terms and has enough text.

### Why does the check warn when my page has a rate table?

The table may be an image, may be loaded by JavaScript, or may use a format the heuristic does not recognise, such as prices without a currency. Add the currency and a delivery time in days as text.

### Does this replace shipping settings in Google Merchant Center?

No. Merchant Center takes shipping costs from your product data or account settings. The page is what people and agents read on your site. Keep both consistent.
