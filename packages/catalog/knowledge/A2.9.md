## In short

Squarespace has one box under Settings, Crawlers that blocks a list of 26 AI crawlers in robots.txt. Most of them collect data for model training, but the list also includes DuckAssistBot and YouBot, which fetch pages for AI answers. This check reports whether the box is on and, if it is, whether it also shuts out those answer agents, so you can decide which matters more to you.

## What this check looks at

The setting is called "Block known artificial intelligence crawlers". It is unticked by default. When you tick it, Squarespace writes a `Disallow` rule for each agent on its list into your robots.txt:

AI2Bot, Ai2Bot-Dolma, aiHitBot, Amazonbot, anthropic-ai, Applebot-Extended, Bytespider, CCBot, ClaudeBot, cohere-ai, cohere-training-data-crawler, DuckAssistBot, FacebookBot, Google-Extended, GoogleOther, GoogleOther-Image, GoogleOther-Video, GPTBot, img2dataset, Meta-ExternalAgent, MyCentralAIScraperBot, omgili, omgilibot, Quora-Bot, TikTokSpider and YouBot.

The list does not include OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot or Claude-User. Those agents can still read the site with the box ticked.

The scanner reads the robots.txt it already fetched. A Squarespace robots.txt starts with the comment `# Squarespace Robots Txt`.

## Why it matters for AI agents

Refusing model training is a legitimate licensing choice. This scanner never takes points for it. See [AI training crawler policy](/knowledge-base/ai-training-crawler-policy) for that side of the question.

The box is broader than training, though. DuckAssistBot and YouBot fetch pages for AI answers. DuckAssistBot, for example, feeds DuckDuckGo's AI-assisted answers. Blocking them removes your store from the answers they feed. Squarespace's own help article warns that blocking may remove the site from chatbot answers. It also notes that blocking does not remove content that was collected before.

The setting is a single box. It blocks the whole list or none of it.

## How the scanner decides

The check runs only when the scan recognised Squarespace (see [platform detection](/knowledge-base/ecommerce-platform-detection)). On other platforms it is skipped and left out of the report. It makes no extra request.

The scanner evaluates robots.txt for each of the 26 agents at the root path `/`, following RFC 9309, the Robots Exclusion Protocol. It takes the box as ticked when at least 60% of the list, 16 of 26 agents, is disallowed.

- **Pass:** the box is off, so the agents on the list may read the site.
- **Pass:** the box is on, but none of the disallowed agents feeds AI answers. Only training crawlers are kept out.
- **Warn:** the box is on and DuckAssistBot or YouBot is disallowed as well. The message names them.
- **Not applicable:** robots.txt could not be read, so the setting is not visible.

The evidence lists every disallowed agent and the answer agents among them.

The weight is 1, which means nice to have. It is not a gate for any level.

## How to fix it

Decide first which goal matters more: refusing training, or appearing in the AI answers that DuckAssistBot and YouBot feed. The box cannot do both.

### Squarespace

1. Open Settings, then Crawlers.
2. To stay in AI answers, untick "Block known artificial intelligence crawlers" and save. The training crawlers on the list are then allowed too.
3. To refuse training above all, keep the box ticked and accept that DuckAssistBot and YouBot are shut out. The check then stays at a warning, which costs little at weight 1.

Either way, the choice is yours. The warning is there so the trade-off is visible, not to push you towards one answer.

## Verify it yourself

Confirm that the file comes from Squarespace and look for the answer agents:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt | head -n 1
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt | grep -inE -A2 "duckassistbot|youbot|gptbot"
```

With the box ticked, these agents appear in robots.txt with `Disallow: /`. With the box unticked, they are not listed. Rescan to confirm.

## Common mistakes

- **Expecting the box to block every AI assistant.** OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot and Claude-User are not on the list.
- **Expecting the box to remove existing data.** Squarespace notes that blocking does not remove content collected earlier.
- **Assuming the box only affects training.** DuckAssistBot and YouBot feed AI answers, and the box blocks them too.
- **A blanket rule elsewhere in robots.txt.** `User-agent: *` with `Disallow: /` disallows every agent on the list too. The check reads the result, so it reports the box as on.
- **Ticking it on autopilot.** It is off by default. Turn it on only after weighing licensing against visibility in AI answers.

## FAQ

### Does Squarespace block AI crawlers by default?

No. The "Block known artificial intelligence crawlers" box is unticked by default. Until you tick it, the agents on the list may read the site.

### Does Squarespace's AI block stop ChatGPT from reading my site?

Not entirely. The list includes GPTBot, OpenAI's training crawler, but not OAI-SearchBot or ChatGPT-User. Those two can still read the site for ChatGPT search and for pages a person asks about.

### Why does the scanner warn when I block AI crawlers?

It does not warn because of training crawlers. It warns because the same box also blocks DuckAssistBot and YouBot, which fetch pages for AI answers. Blocking training alone always passes.

### Can I block only some of the crawlers on Squarespace's list?

The setting is a single box that covers the whole list. You choose between blocking all 26 agents and blocking none of them.
