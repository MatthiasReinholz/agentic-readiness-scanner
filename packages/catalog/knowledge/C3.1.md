## In short

Since 19 June 2026, online stores that sell to consumers in the EU must offer a withdrawal function: a link or button labelled "withdraw from contract here", or the official equivalent in the store's language, that lets a consumer withdraw from a purchase online in two steps. This check looks for that label on the home page. It cannot follow the flow behind it.

## What this check looks at

Directive (EU) 2023/2673 inserted **Article 11a** into the Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU. It applies to distance contracts (contracts made without meeting, such as online orders) concluded through an online interface where the consumer has a right of withdrawal: the right to cancel within a set period, usually 14 days, without giving a reason. Member states apply it from 19 June 2026. In Germany it is transposed as **BGB §356a**, "Elektronische Widerrufsfunktion bei Fernabsatzverträgen".

The trader must provide:

1. **A withdrawal function** on the online interface, labelled with the words "withdraw from contract here" or a corresponding unambiguous formulation. It must be clearly legible, prominently placed, easily accessible and available throughout the withdrawal period.
2. **A form of the statement** in which the consumer gives or confirms their name, the contract or part of it they withdraw from, and the electronic means for the acknowledgement.
3. **A confirmation function** labelled "confirm withdrawal" (German "Widerruf bestätigen") or a corresponding unambiguous formulation, which sends the statement.
4. **An acknowledgement of receipt** on a durable medium, such as an email, without undue delay, with the content of the statement and the date and time it was received.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A person who changes their mind can ask an agent to handle it: "withdraw from the order I placed at this shop on Monday". The withdrawal function is unusually agent-friendly, because the law fixes the entry point. The label is prescribed in each official language, so an agent can look for one known string per language instead of guessing what a store calls its returns page.

The steps behind it are just as structured: a name, the contract, an email address for the acknowledgement, and a button labelled "confirm withdrawal". Those are details an agent acting for its principal already has. The acknowledgement with date and time gives the principal a record that the agent did what it was asked.

Before a purchase, a shopping agent can also use the link as evidence that the store is set up for EU consumers. The returns side is covered by [statutory return period](/knowledge-base/statutory-return-period) and, for the machine-readable form, by [shipping and return markup](/knowledge-base/shipping-and-return-markup).

## How the scanner decides

The check runs when the store appears to sell in the EU, from signals such as an EU country domain, euro prices, the page language or an EU address, or when you choose the market yourself. Otherwise it is shown as not applicable.

The scanner reads the links, buttons and image alt text in the server-rendered HTML of the home page. It does not read other pages for this check, so put the link in the footer, which the home page shares with the rest of the site.

- **Found:** a link or button carries the official Article 11a label in any EU language, such as "withdraw from contract here", "Vertrag widerrufen", "renoncer au contrat ici" or "desistir del contrato aquí".
- **Review:** a link names withdrawal in other words, such as "Widerruf", "withdrawal form" or "rétractation". The law accepts other wording only when it is unambiguous, so a person should look.
- **Not found in the pages read:** no such link or button on the home page.

What the scanner cannot see:

- The second step ("confirm withdrawal") and the acknowledgement, which happen after the link.
- A function that exists only after signing in, in the customer account or in an order email.
- Whether the function is available for the whole withdrawal period, or whether the store must offer one at all.

This is a technical reading of public pages, not legal advice.

## How to fix it

Put the entry link in the shared footer with the official label in the store's language, build the two-step flow behind it and send an acknowledgement. Have a lawyer review the flow and texts.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Create a withdrawal page with a form for name, order number and email, and a submit button labelled "confirm withdrawal" in the store's language. A form plugin works. A WooCommerce extension that links requests to orders saves manual matching.
- Configure the form to send the consumer an email that repeats what they submitted and the date and time.
- Add the page to the footer menu under Appearance, Menus (or the footer block) with the exact label, for example "Vertrag widerrufen" for a German store.

### Shopify

- Create a page under Online Store, Pages, with a form app that collects name, order number and email and sends a confirmation email.
- Add it to the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation, with the official label.
- If you also offer withdrawal in the customer account, keep the public footer link so consumers who are not signed in can reach it.

### Any other stack

- Add the link to the shared footer template, in the server-rendered HTML, with the official label in each storefront language.
- Build the flow: a form with name, contract and email, a "confirm withdrawal" button, and an acknowledgement email with the content and the time of receipt.
- Keep the link available throughout the withdrawal period, which for goods usually runs 14 days from delivery.

## Verify it yourself

Search the home page for the label in your store's language:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -ioE "withdraw from contract here|vertrag widerrufen|renoncer au contrat ici|desistir del contrato aquí|recedere dal contratto qui|hier de overeenkomst herroepen"
```

No output means the label is not in the server-rendered HTML. Then click the link yourself, submit a test withdrawal and check the acknowledgement email. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **A link to the withdrawal policy text.** A page that explains the right is not a function that exercises it.
- **Own wording instead of the label.** "Returns" or "Widerrufsbelehrung" does not name the function. Other wording is allowed only when unambiguous, and the official label avoids the question.
- **Only in the customer account.** Consumers who bought as guests, or forgot their password, cannot reach it, and neither can this scanner.
- **No confirmation step or no acknowledgement.** The law asks for both.
- **A footer built by JavaScript.** Links that appear only after scripts run are invisible to many readers, including this scanner.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

Article 11a applies to traders who conclude distance contracts with consumers in the EU through an online interface, where the consumer has a right of withdrawal. Sales only to businesses are outside it, and some contracts have no right of withdrawal under Article 16 of the Consumer Rights Directive, such as goods made to the consumer's specifications. Whether that describes your store is a question for a lawyer.

### Which label should a multilingual store use?

Each storefront language can carry its own official label. The scanner accepts the label in any EU language, so the German version can say "Vertrag widerrufen" and the French one "renoncer au contrat ici".

### Is the withdrawal button the same as the German cancellation button?

No. The cancellation button under BGB §312k ("Verträge hier kündigen") is for ending ongoing contracts such as subscriptions. The withdrawal function under BGB §356a is for withdrawing from a distance contract within the withdrawal period. A store with subscriptions may need both.

### Can I put the function only in order confirmation emails?

The law places the function on the online interface through which the contract was concluded, available throughout the withdrawal period. A link in an email can help consumers, but the scanner looks for the function on the store's public pages.

### Does the UK have the same rule?

No. Article 11a is EU law and the UK is not bound by it. UK stores that sell to consumers in the EU may still be covered for those sales.
