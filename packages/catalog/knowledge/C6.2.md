## In short

EU copyright law lets anyone mine publicly available content for text and data mining, including AI training, unless the rightholder has reserved that use. For online content, the reservation has to be machine-readable: a sentence in your terms of service is not enough, according to a 2025 German appeal ruling. This check classifies the reservation your store publishes and, most importantly, checks that it does not also lock out the search crawlers that put your products in front of shoppers. Reserving is a right, not a duty.

## What this check looks at

This check concerns the EU.

- **The exception and the opt-out.** Article 4 of the Directive on copyright in the Digital Single Market (DSM Directive), Directive (EU) 2019/790, allows text and data mining (TDM) of lawfully accessible content. Article 4(3) makes that exception subject to a reservation by the rightholder "in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means" for content made publicly available online.
- **Germany.** Section 44b(3) of the Copyright Act (UrhG) implements the provision: a reservation for works available online is effective only if it is machine-readable. On 10 December 2025, the Hamburg Higher Regional Court (case 5 U 104/24, Kneschke v LAION) held that a reservation written in natural language in a website's terms of use did not meet that test. Courts in other Member States may decide differently.
- **AI model providers.** Article 53(1)(c) of the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, obliges providers of general-purpose AI models to put in place a copyright policy that identifies and complies with Article 4(3) reservations, including through state-of-the-art technologies. The general-purpose AI Code of Practice published in July 2025 commits its signatories to crawlers that follow robots.txt as specified in RFC 9309 and other appropriate machine-readable protocols.

The machine-readable forms in use today:

| Form                       | Where it lives                                                                     | Status                                                          |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| robots.txt training groups | `User-agent` groups for GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended and other training bots | RFC 9309, and each crawler operator documents its own token     |
| Shared training token      | a robots.txt group for `__usage_ai-training`                                       | IETF draft, not an RFC                                          |
| Content-Usage              | a robots.txt line or a response header, for example `train-ai=n`                   | IETF AI Preferences (AIPREF) drafts, not RFCs                   |
| TDM Reservation Protocol   | `/.well-known/tdmrep.json`, a `tdm-reservation` header or meta tag                 | W3C Community Group final report, not a W3C standard            |
| Terms of service text      | a clause in the terms or a legal notice                                            | natural language, not machine-readable under the Hamburg ruling |

The report lists each provision with a link to its official text.

## Why it matters for AI agents

A TDM reservation decides whether AI training crawlers may use your content. That is a licensing decision, and the scanner takes no side on it. What matters for agents is how the reservation is written.

Training crawlers are not the crawlers that sell your products. Search and assistant crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot build the indexes AI answers draw on, as the [search and assistant crawler check](/knowledge-base/search-and-assistant-crawlers-allowed) explains. User-triggered fetchers such as ChatGPT-User and Claude-User open a page when a person asks about it, as the [user-triggered fetchers check](/knowledge-base/user-triggered-fetchers-allowed) explains. A careless reservation, such as `Disallow: /` in the wildcard group, a block list that lumps every "AI bot" together, or a CDN toggle that blocks them all, shuts out these agents along with the training crawlers. The store disappears from AI answers and assistants cannot open its product pages, while the training decision could have been made on its own.

A precise reservation names the training crawlers, or uses a training-specific vocabulary such as TDMRep, and leaves the rest alone. The [AI training crawler policy check](/knowledge-base/ai-training-crawler-policy) scores whether a training decision is stated at all, and the [usage preference signals check](/knowledge-base/usage-preference-signals) checks that each signal is well-formed. This check adds the legal reading on top.

## How the scanner decides

The check runs when the store appears to sell to the EU. The scanner counts a reservation as machine-readable when it finds any of these:

- robots.txt keeps at least one AI training crawler ({{crawlers:training}}) out of the whole site
- `Content-Usage` lines in robots.txt
- a valid `/.well-known/tdmrep.json`
- a `tdm-reservation` header or meta tag set to `1`

It also reads the terms page for reservation wording, such as "text and data mining", "TDM" or "AI training". Then it checks whether robots.txt lets the search crawlers from the [search and assistant crawler check](/knowledge-base/search-and-assistant-crawlers-allowed) (Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and others) read the first sampled product page.

- **Found:** a machine-readable reservation exists, and the search crawlers can still read product pages.
- **Review:** a machine-readable reservation exists but also blocks one or more search crawlers. Or the reservation exists only in the terms, which is not machine-readable under the 2025 Hamburg ruling.
- **Found, with a note:** nothing is reserved. Reserving is a right, not a duty, so having no reservation is reported without a warning.

The scanner does not check the user-triggered fetchers here, and it does not judge whether every training crawler is covered. One blocked training crawler is enough for a machine-readable reservation.

What the scanner cannot see:

- Licences and agreements you have made with AI companies.
- Whether a given crawler honours a given signal. Support differs, especially for the draft vocabularies.
- Server-side or CDN bot blocking that does not appear in robots.txt. The [honest fetch check](/knowledge-base/no-block-on-honest-fetch) covers what the scanner itself is refused.
- Reservations embedded in images or other files.

The result is reported, never scored, and it is not legal advice.

## How to fix it

Decide first whether you want to reserve TDM for AI training. If you do, publish the reservation in robots.txt groups for the training crawlers, optionally add TDMRep, and keep the search crawlers and user-triggered fetchers allowed. Keep any sentence in the terms as a supplement.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Edit robots.txt through your SEO plugin's robots.txt editor, or the physical `robots.txt` file in the web root if there is one, which overrides the virtual file.
- Add one group listing the training tokens with `Disallow: /`, and leave the wildcard group open for the rest.
- Publish `/.well-known/tdmrep.json` as a static file in the web root, and make sure WordPress does not answer that path with an HTML page.
- If a security plugin offers to "block AI bots", check which tokens it blocks and remove the search crawlers and user-triggered fetchers from its list.

### Shopify

- Edit robots.txt through the `robots.txt.liquid` theme template under Online Store, Themes, Edit code, and add the training group after the default rules.
- Shopify does not let a theme serve files under `/.well-known/`. Use a meta tag named `tdm-reservation` with the value `1` in `theme.liquid` instead.
- Review any app that toggles AI crawler access and check the robots.txt it produces.

### Any other stack

- Add the training group where robots.txt is generated, and serve `tdmrep.json` with a JSON content type.
- A `tdm-reservation: 1` response header works for every page, including non-HTML files.
- If a CDN offers a managed robots.txt or an "AI bot" blocking setting, check exactly which crawlers it covers. Cloudflare's managed robots.txt, for example, can prepend AI rules to your own file.

## Verify it yourself

Check the machine-readable forms in turn:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/robots.txt | grep -inE "^user-agent:|^disallow: /$|content-usage|__usage_ai-training"
curl -s https://example.com/.well-known/tdmrep.json
curl -sI https://example.com/ | grep -i "^tdm-"
```

Then confirm that the wildcard group, and any group for OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot, Bingbot, ChatGPT-User or Claude-User, does not contain `Disallow: /`. Rescan to see the classification in the evidence.

## Common mistakes

- **Terms only.** A clause in the terms of service is the classic reservation that the Hamburg court found not machine-readable.
- **Blocking everything.** `User-agent: *` with `Disallow: /` reserves training and removes the store from search and AI answers at the same time.
- **Lumping all AI bots together.** Block lists that put OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot or ChatGPT-User next to GPTBot cost visibility and fetches the owner wanted.
- **A malformed tdmrep.json.** The file must be a JSON array of objects, each with a `location` and a `tdm-reservation` of 0 or 1.
- **Inventing tokens.** Tokens that no crawler operator documents reserve nothing.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

Reserving text and data mining is a right of the rightholder under EU law, not a duty, so there is nothing to comply with if you do not want to reserve. If you do want to reserve, EU law asks for an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means for online content. The obligation in Article 53(1)(c) of the AI Act binds providers of general-purpose AI models, not stores. Whether a reservation is effective for your content is a question for a qualified lawyer.

### Is a sentence in my terms of service enough?

Under the Hamburg Higher Regional Court's December 2025 ruling, a natural-language reservation in terms of use did not count as machine-readable under German law. Keep the sentence if you like, but publish a machine-readable reservation as well.

### Will blocking GPTBot remove my store from ChatGPT search?

No. GPTBot is OpenAI's training crawler. ChatGPT search uses OAI-SearchBot, and ChatGPT-User fetches pages when a person asks. Keep those two allowed if you want to appear in ChatGPT.

### Should I use robots.txt or TDMRep?

They complement each other. robots.txt groups are what the major AI crawler operators document. TDMRep is a vocabulary written specifically for TDM reservations under EU law. Publishing both costs little, as long as neither blocks the search crawlers.

### Are Content-Usage and the shared training token official standards?

Not yet. Both come from IETF drafts, which are work in progress and not RFCs. They are valid ways to express a preference, but support depends on each crawler operator.
