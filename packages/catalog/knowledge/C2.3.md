## In short

California requires businesses covered by its privacy law that sell or share personal information to link "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" from their website, or a combined "Your Privacy Choices" link with the official icon. This check looks for those links on the home page of stores that appear to sell in the United States. A missing link is shown as review, not as a breach, because many stores are not covered.

## What this check looks at

The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act, gives consumers the right to opt out of the sale of their personal information and of its sharing for cross-context behavioural advertising (ads based on what a person does on other sites and apps). "Sharing" includes passing browsing data to advertising platforms through pixels and tags, which is common in online stores.

Two texts set out the links:

- **Cal. Civ. Code §1798.135** requires a business that sells or shares personal information to provide a clear and conspicuous link titled "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information". A business that uses sensitive personal information beyond the permitted purposes must also link "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information". The statute allows a single link that combines both.
- **The CCPA Regulations**, Cal. Code Regs. title 11, set the details. §7013 covers the notice of the right to opt out and where the link goes (the header or footer of the website). §7014 covers the notice of the right to limit. §7015 defines the alternative opt-out link: "Your Privacy Choices" or "Your California Privacy Choices", shown with the opt-out icon published by the California Privacy Protection Agency.

The law applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet at least one threshold: annual gross revenue above an inflation-adjusted amount (originally 25 million US dollars), buying, selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households a year, or earning half or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information. Other US states have their own opt-out rules, but this check looks for the wording California prescribes.

## Why it matters for AI agents

The CCPA lets consumers act through an authorised agent, a person or business they name to submit privacy requests for them. An AI agent working for a shopper is a natural fit for that role: "opt me out of data sale on every store I bought from this year" is a reasonable instruction.

To carry it out, an agent has to find the opt-out route. Prescribed wording helps. A link with the exact text "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" or "Your Privacy Choices" in the footer of the server-rendered page is something an agent can find on any covered site without guessing. A route hidden inside a consent banner, built only by JavaScript or named in the store's own words is much harder to find and use.

A related signal travels with the agent itself: browsers and agents that send Global Privacy Control (see [Global Privacy Control](/knowledge-base/global-privacy-control)) ask for the same opt-out on every request.

## How the scanner decides

The check runs only when the store appears to sell in the United States, from signals such as a page language of `en-US`, US dollar prices, or a US address or shipping destination in the markup, or when you choose the market yourself. Otherwise it is shown as not applicable.

The scanner reads the links, buttons and image alt text in the server-rendered HTML of the home page.

- **Found:** one of them says "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information", "Your Privacy Choices", "Your California Privacy Choices" or "Limit the Use of My Sensitive Personal Information". The older "Do Not Sell My Personal Information" also counts. The opt-out icon counts only when its alt text carries one of these phrases.
- **Review:** none of these is on the home page. This is never shown as not found, because businesses below the thresholds, or that neither sell nor share personal information, need no link.

What the scanner cannot see:

- Whether the business is covered by the CCPA at all.
- Whether the link leads to a working opt-out, or whether opting out actually stops pixels and data sharing.
- Links added by a consent tool after JavaScript runs, or shown only to visitors located in California.

This is a technical reading of public pages, not legal advice.

## How to fix it

First, find out with a lawyer whether the CCPA applies and whether the store sells or shares personal information. If it does, add the link to the footer of every page and make the page behind it work.

### WordPress and WooCommerce

- Add a footer menu item under Appearance, Menus (or the footer block in a block theme) with the exact text "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information", pointing to an opt-out page.
- If you use a consent management plugin, check whether it offers a CCPA mode that creates the opt-out page and turns off advertising tags for visitors who opt out. Link its page from the footer menu rather than relying on a floating button only.
- View the page source and confirm the link text appears in the HTML.

### Shopify

- Under Settings, Customer privacy, Shopify offers a setting for the sale or sharing of data that can add a "Your privacy choices" link and an opt-out page. Turn it on if it fits your situation.
- If your theme does not show the link automatically, add the opt-out page to the footer menu under Online Store, Navigation.
- Check that apps and pixels you installed respect the opt-out through Shopify's customer privacy controls.

### Any other stack

- Put the link in the shared footer template so it is on every page, in the server-rendered HTML.
- Use the exact prescribed wording. If you use "Your Privacy Choices", show the official icon from the California Privacy Protection Agency next to it.
- The opt-out page must work without creating an account and must stop the sale and sharing for that visitor, including through advertising pixels.

## Verify it yourself

Search the home page for the prescribed wording:

```bash
curl -s https://example.com/ | grep -ioE "do not sell or share my personal information|your (california )?privacy choices|limit the use of my sensitive personal information"
```

No output means the link is not in the server-rendered HTML. Rescan with this scanner after the change.

## Common mistakes

- **Old wording.** "Do Not Sell My Personal Information" predates the 2023 amendments, which added sharing. The current text is "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information". The scanner still finds the old wording, so update it even when the check says found.
- **"Your Privacy Choices" without the icon.** The regulations pair the alternative link with the icon.
- **A link only inside the cookie banner.** The regulations ask for a link in the header or footer, and a banner that loads by JavaScript is invisible to many readers.
- **A link to a form that needs an account or asks for more data than needed.** An opt-out should be easy to use.
- **A link that changes nothing.** If advertising pixels keep firing after an opt-out, the link is decoration.

## FAQ

### Does this apply to my store?

The CCPA applies to for-profit businesses that do business in California and meet one of its thresholds: revenue above an inflation-adjusted amount, the data of 100,000 or more consumers or households a year, or half of revenue from selling or sharing personal information. The links are needed when such a business sells or shares personal information. The scanner cannot tell whether that is the case, but a lawyer can.

### What counts as "sharing" for an online store?

Under the CCPA, sharing means making personal information available to a third party for cross-context behavioural advertising, whether or not money changes hands. Advertising pixels that send browsing data to ad platforms are the usual example.

### Can I use one link instead of two?

Yes. The regulations allow a single "Your Privacy Choices" or "Your California Privacy Choices" link with the official icon, leading to a page where consumers can opt out of sale and sharing and limit the use of sensitive personal information.

### Why does the scanner show review instead of not found?

Because a missing link is legally fine for many stores. Businesses below the thresholds, or that neither sell nor share personal information, need no link. The scanner cannot see a business's size or data flows, so it asks for a human check.

### Do I need this if I only ship to California occasionally?

The law turns on doing business in California and meeting a threshold, not on how many orders go there. The scanner runs the check for any store that appears to sell in the United States. Ask a lawyer how it applies to your business.
