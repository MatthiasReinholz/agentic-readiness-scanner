## Summary

Find every generic environmental claim on your pages, such as "eco-friendly" or "climate neutral", and either replace it with a specific claim you can prove (what, how much, certified by whom) or remove it. Keep the evidence on file, and have every environmental claim reviewed by someone qualified in consumer law before it goes live.

## Snippet

```text
Instead of:  Eco-friendly sneaker
Write:       Upper made from 70 % recycled polyester (by weight).
             Recycled content certified under [name of certification scheme], certificate [number].

Instead of:  Climate neutral shipping
Write:       We buy carbon credits from [project name] for the emissions of our shipments.
             This does not reduce the emissions of the product itself.
```

## Agent prompt

Audit this store for generic environmental claims and prepare specific replacements for the owner to approve. Search product titles, descriptions, category pages, the home page, banners, badges, meta descriptions and structured data for wording such as eco-friendly, environmentally friendly, climate neutral, carbon neutral, CO2-neutral, planet-friendly, klimaneutral, umweltfreundlich, écologique, ecológico and their equivalents in the store's languages. Produce a list with each claim, its URL and its context. For each claim, ask the owner what evidence exists. Where there is evidence, rewrite the claim so it names the specific property, the share or amount and the certification scheme or test behind it. Where there is none, remove the claim. Never write that a product is neutral, reduced or positive in its climate impact because of carbon offsets; an accurate description of a contribution to a climate project may stay if the owner confirms it. Keep sustainability labels only when they come from a certification scheme or a public authority. Do not change claims without the owner's approval, do not claim that the result complies with any law, and tell the owner to have the final wording reviewed by a qualified person.
