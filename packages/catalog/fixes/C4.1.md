## Summary

Publish an accessibility statement on its own page and link it from the footer of every page with a label that names accessibility in the page language. Base its content on an actual review of your site, and have the wording reviewed by a lawyer for the markets you sell to.

## Snippet

```text
Accessibility statement (URL: /accessibility, linked in the footer as "Accessibility")

1. Our commitment
   Which shop this covers (domain, apps) and the standard you aim for,
   for example WCAG 2.1 Level AA as used by EN 301 549.

2. How the service meets the accessibility requirements
   Plain description of the shop and how to use it: navigation, search,
   product pages, cart, checkout, account, customer service.
   What you did: theme, alt text, form labels, keyboard use, captions.

3. Known limitations
   Parts that are not yet accessible, why, and the planned fix date.
   Accessible alternatives in the meantime (phone, email, chat).

4. Feedback and contact
   How to report a barrier or request content in another format,
   with an email address and a response time.

5. Enforcement
   The competent market surveillance authority, for example in Germany
   the MLBF (Marktüberwachungsstelle der Länder für die Barrierefreiheit
   von Produkten und Dienstleistungen), with its contact details.

6. Date
   When the statement was prepared and last reviewed, and how
   (self-assessment, external audit).
```

## Agent prompt

Add an accessibility statement to this store. 1) Create a page at /accessibility (or the localised equivalent, for example /barrierefreiheit for German, /accessibilite for French, /acessibilidade for Portuguese) titled "Accessibility statement" in the page language. 2) Fill it with these sections, using placeholders where you lack facts and marking them clearly for the owner: scope (domain and apps), the standard aimed for (WCAG 2.1 Level AA), a general description of the shop and how it meets the accessibility requirements, known limitations with planned fixes and accessible alternatives, a feedback contact (email address and response time), the competent market surveillance authority for each EU market served, and the date of preparation and last review. 3) Link the page from the footer on every page with the label "Accessibility" (or "Barrierefreiheit", "Accessibilité", "Accesibilidad", "Acessibilidade", "Toegankelijkheid", "Accessibilità" to match the page language). For a Brazilian store, also show the accessibility symbol prominently in the header or footer, as a linked image with alt text "Acessibilidade". 4) Render the link and the page in the server HTML so they are readable without JavaScript. Do not write that the site is fully accessible or compliant unless an audit supports it, and leave a note for the owner to have the final wording reviewed by a lawyer.
