## Summary

Do not disallow the fetchers that act when a person or an agent asks about your site (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User, Google-Agent, meta-externalfetcher, Amzn-User, MistralAI-User and Cloudflare's CloudflareBrowserRenderingCrawler) on product and policy paths. Most assistant fetchers ignore robots.txt for user-requested fetches anyway, so a Disallow mostly records an intent you probably do not hold. Cloudflare's crawler obeys it, so for that one a Disallow is a real block.

## Snippet

```robots
User-agent: ChatGPT-User
User-agent: Claude-User
User-agent: Perplexity-User
User-agent: Google-Agent
User-agent: meta-externalfetcher
User-agent: Amzn-User
User-agent: MistralAI-User
User-agent: CloudflareBrowserRenderingCrawler
Allow: /
Disallow: /cart/
Disallow: /checkout/
```

## Agent prompt

This site's robots.txt disallows at least one of the fetchers that act when a person or an agent asks about a page. Review robots.txt and remove any rule that disallows ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User, Google-Agent, meta-externalfetcher, Amzn-User, MistralAI-User or CloudflareBrowserRenderingCrawler on product, category and policy paths, including rules they inherit from a wildcard group. Keep cart, checkout and account paths disallowed for everyone. A named group replaces the `*` group for that fetcher, so give it the same private paths. Verify by fetching /robots.txt and checking that no group that applies to these fetchers has a Disallow rule matching a product URL.

## On WordPress

Edit robots.txt in the SEO plugin's editor, and check "block AI bots" plugins, which often list ChatGPT-User and similar fetchers.
