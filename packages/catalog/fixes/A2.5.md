## Summary

Do not disallow the fetchers and browser agents that act when a person asks an assistant about your site ({{crawlers:fetchers.assistant}}) on product and policy paths. Several of them may fetch anyway because a person asked, so a Disallow mostly records an intent you probably do not hold. Others obey robots.txt ({{crawlers:fetchers.honours}}), and for them a Disallow is a real block.

## Agent prompt

This site's robots.txt disallows at least one of the fetchers or browser agents that act when a person or an agent asks about a page. Review robots.txt and remove any rule that disallows {{crawlers:fetchers}} on product, category and policy paths, including rules they inherit from a wildcard group. Start with the major assistants ({{crawlers:fetchers.assistant}}). Keep cart, checkout and account paths disallowed for everyone. A named group replaces the `*` group for that fetcher, so give it the same private paths. Verify by fetching /robots.txt and checking that no group that applies to these fetchers has a Disallow rule matching a product URL.

## On WordPress

Edit robots.txt in the SEO plugin's editor, and check "block AI bots" plugins, which often list ChatGPT-User and similar fetchers.

## On Squarespace

The "Block known artificial intelligence crawlers" box under Settings > Crawlers adds a fixed list to robots.txt that includes DuckAssistBot, which fetches pages live for DuckDuckGo's AI answers. Leave it unticked to stay in those answers.
