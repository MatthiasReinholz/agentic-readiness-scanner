## Summary

Open your CDN or bot manager's AI crawler settings and make them agree with robots.txt. A firewall rule can refuse GPTBot, ChatGPT-User or Claude-SearchBot while robots.txt welcomes them, and no outside scan can see it. Check the crawlers and assistants you want, then confirm in the vendor's logs that their requests get through.

## Agent prompt

The scan recognised the CDN or bot manager in front of this site (named in the evidence), or found Cloudflare's managed block in robots.txt. Edge rules for AI clients cannot be seen from outside, so work with the site owner in the vendor's dashboard. First, list which AI clients the owner wants: the search crawlers behind AI answers ({{crawlers:index.core}} and {{crawlers:index.assistant}}), the assistants' fetchers and agents ({{crawlers:fetchers.assistant}}), and a training decision. Second, open the setting named in the evidence (`setting`) and make each rule match that list: allow what robots.txt allows, block what it disallows. Signed agents such as ChatGPT's cloud browser need an explicit allow on some vendors. Third, check the vendor's security or bot events for the past days and confirm that requests from these clients were served, not challenged or blocked. Do not loosen rules for cart, checkout or account paths. Report what you changed and what the logs show.

## On Shopify

Shopify runs the CDN in front of every store and handles bot management itself. Merchants control crawlers through robots.txt.liquid only.

## On Squarespace

Squarespace runs the edge for its sites. The crawler setting under Settings > Crawlers writes robots.txt, which A2.9 reports.

## On Webflow

Webflow runs the edge for its sites. The Traffic control switches under Site settings > SEO > Indexing write robots.txt, which A2.10 reports.
