## Summary

State a decision on AI training in robots.txt, in whichever direction you choose: a group for the shared token `__usage_ai-training`, or groups for the documented training crawlers. Allowing or disallowing is the merchant's call. Silence is the only thing this check notes.

## Snippet

```robots
# AI training: one shared decision (allow or disallow, your choice)
User-agent: __usage_ai-training
Disallow: /

# Search and assistants stay welcome
User-agent: *
Allow: /
```

## Agent prompt

This site's robots.txt says nothing about AI training: it has no group for the shared token `__usage_ai-training`, no group naming a documented training crawler and no Content-Signal or Content-Usage line with a training value. Ask the owner whether they want to allow or refuse training, then add that decision to robots.txt. Preferred: a group for `__usage_ai-training` (an IETF draft that Microsoft will honour from 1 February 2027 and Google intends to honour from early 2027) with `Allow: /` or `Disallow: /`. Optionally add groups for the individual training crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, meta-externalagent, Amazonbot, CCBot, Bytespider, MistralAI-Training). A named group replaces the `*` group for that crawler, so repeat any private paths it needs. Do not touch the groups for search and assistant crawlers such as Googlebot, Bingbot, OAI-SearchBot or Claude-SearchBot. Verify by fetching /robots.txt and checking that the new group is present and the search crawler groups are unchanged.

## On WordPress

Add the group in the SEO plugin's robots.txt editor, or in the physical robots.txt in the web root if the site has one.
