## Agent prompt

This site's robots.txt disallows one or more of the crawlers that build the indexes behind AI answers on a sampled product page. Review robots.txt and make sure {{crawlers:index.core}} are allowed on product, category and policy paths, and the major assistants' crawlers too: {{crawlers:index.assistant}}. If the owner disallows Google-Extended to refuse training, tell them it also keeps the site out of answers in Gemini Apps, and leave the choice to them. Check both the groups that name these crawlers and the `User-agent: *` group they fall back to: a named group replaces the `*` group entirely for that crawler, and a wildcard `Disallow: /` blocks every crawler without a group of its own. Applebot follows the Googlebot group when no Applebot group exists. Keep training-only decisions ({{crawlers:training}}) in their own groups. Keep cart, checkout and account paths disallowed if they are today. Verify by fetching /robots.txt and testing a product URL for Googlebot and Bingbot in Google Search Console's robots.txt report or Bing Webmaster Tools.

## On WordPress

Edit robots.txt in the SEO plugin's editor, or in the physical robots.txt in the web root if one exists, and check security plugins that add "block AI bots" rules.

## On Squarespace

The "Block known artificial intelligence crawlers" box under Settings > Crawlers adds a fixed list to robots.txt that includes Google-Extended. Leaving it unticked keeps the site in Gemini app answers.

## On Webflow

The Traffic control switches under Site settings > SEO > Indexing disallow whole groups of search and AI bots. Keep both on and publish.

## On Wix

Wix does not block AI crawlers by default. Rules that do were added in the Robots.txt Editor and can be removed there.
