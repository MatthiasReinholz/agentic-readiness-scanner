# Knowledge base

The hosted scanner publishes one article per check at `/knowledge-base/<slug>`. There are 100 articles: 76 for the readiness checks and 24 for the legal-signal checks. The index is at `/knowledge-base`, and each of the 9 readiness sections (A1 to B5) has its own page. Every page is also served as Markdown: append `.md`, or send `Accept: text/markdown`. The index is available as data at `/knowledge-base.json`, the whole knowledge base as one Markdown file at `/llms-full.txt`, and the MCP tool `explain_check` returns an article. Every page is listed in `/llms.txt` (a Markdown index written for AI tools) and `/sitemap.xml`, so people, search engines and agents can all read and cite it. A link by check id, such as `/knowledge-base/A2.2`, redirects to the article.

## Where the content lives

- `packages/catalog/knowledge/<CHECK-ID>.md` holds the authored article for each check. Every check in the catalog needs one. `pnpm --filter @agentic-readiness/catalog validate` fails otherwise, and also fails on a duplicate slug or a link that does not resolve.
- The page adds the rest from the catalog, so an article never repeats it: the check's weight and priority tier, the levels it gates, the checks it depends on, the platform it is limited to, the fix (summary, snippet, platform notes and the brief for a coding agent) and the list of specifications.
- `packages/catalog/src/generated/knowledge.ts` is generated by the catalog build. Do not edit it.

## Guides

Some topics belong to no single check: how the scanner works, why it reports what it reports, or a subject that spans several sections. These are guides, at `/knowledge-base/guides/<slug>`. They are listed on `/knowledge-base/guides` and at the top of the index. Like articles, they are served as Markdown and listed in `/knowledge-base.json`, `/llms.txt`, `/llms-full.txt` and the sitemap. There is 1 guide so far, `crawler-blocks-and-browser-scans`.

- `packages/catalog/knowledge/guides/<slug>.md` holds each guide. The file name is the slug.
- The front matter is the same as for articles (`slug`, `headline`, `description`, `keywords`), plus `related`: the check ids the guide is about, comma-separated. Each related check's article links back to the guide under "Guides on this topic", and the guide lists them under "Checks this guide is about".
- Sections are free, with two fixed ends. The first is `## In short` (the answer, in two or three sentences that stand alone). The last is `## Sources`, a list of at least two `- [title](https://...)` links to public sources. At least two sections sit between them, and no heading repeats. Every claim must be traceable to a source or to the scanner's own code. An optional `## FAQ` follows the article rules (3 to 6 `###` questions) and becomes FAQPage structured data (markup that tells search engines the page answers questions).
- The Markdown subset and the writing rules below apply unchanged. Articles and guides may link to each other (`/knowledge-base/<slug>`, `/knowledge-base/guides/<slug>`). The catalog build fails on a link that does not resolve or a related check that does not exist.

## Legal-signals articles

The 24 legal checks (C1.1 to C6.3, [decision 0015](decisions/0015-legal-signals.md)) look at the legal disclosures a store's public pages show. They are never scored. Their articles use the same format and are listed on `/knowledge-base/legal-signals` instead of the readiness index. Their pages open with the legal notice and list the laws from `packages/catalog/data/legal/laws.json`, so an article explains the duty per market by name without pasting the registry. Write them as explanations of what the law asks and what the scanner can see. Never tell a reader that a store complies. Answer "Does this apply to my store?" with the thresholds and markets, not with advice.

## File format

```markdown
---
slug: search-and-assistant-crawlers-allowed
headline: Let the crawlers behind AI answers read your product pages
description: One or two sentences, 100 to 180 characters, used as the meta description.
keywords: four to eight comma-separated search phrases
---

## In short

## What this check looks at

## Why it matters for AI agents

## How the scanner decides

## How to fix it

### WordPress and WooCommerce

### Shopify

### Any other stack

## Verify it yourself

## Common mistakes

## FAQ

### A question a store owner or developer would actually type?
```

The front matter needs `slug`, `headline` (10 to 90 characters), `description` (100 to 180 characters) and `keywords` (at least 3). All eight `##` sections are required, in this order. Under "How to fix it", use the three platform subsections when they apply. A check that concerns only one platform uses the subsections that make sense. The FAQ has 3 to 6 `###` questions, each ending with a question mark and answered in one short paragraph. They become FAQPage structured data.

The slug is permanent once published, because it is the URL. Keep it short, lowercase, kebab-case and keyword-bearing.

## Supported Markdown

The renderer accepts a deliberate subset, so every article renders the same everywhere:

- `##` and `###` headings, paragraphs, `-` bullet lists and `1.` numbered lists (no nesting)
- fenced code blocks with a language, such as `bash`, `robots`, `html`, `json`, `http`, `php` or `text` (a block without a language is shown as `text`)
- `inline code`, `**bold**`, `[links](https://...)` and links to other articles as `/knowledge-base/<slug>`
- pipe tables with a header row

No raw HTML, no images, no nested lists, no headings below `###`, no em dashes. The catalog build rejects each of these.

## Writing rules

- **Answer first.** "In short" answers the question in two or three sentences that stand alone. Agents and search snippets quote it.
- **Plain, short sentences.** One idea per sentence. No em dashes. Explain every acronym the first time.
- **Accurate before complete.** "How the scanner decides" must match the check's `method` in the catalog and its implementation in `packages/core/src/checks`. State vendor behaviour only when the check's specifications or the vendor's own documentation say so.
- **Never overclaim.** A passing check does not mean an agent will buy. Say what the check proves and what it does not.
- **Concrete fixes.** Name the settings screen, template, plugin type or header. Point to the platform's own mechanism before custom code.
- **Verifiable.** "Verify it yourself" gives a command or a free tool a reader can run against their own site.
- **Neutral and public.** Nothing about unreleased partner work, internal integration status, launch timing or commercial terms. No plugin is endorsed by name unless it is the platform's own or the common default, and then only as an example.
